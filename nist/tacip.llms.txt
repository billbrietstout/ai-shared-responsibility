# Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure Profile (TACIP)

> A NIST AI RMF profile (community-of-interest discussion draft) that translates trustworthy-AI objectives into actionable risk-management Practices, Tasks, and Implementations for the 16 U.S. critical-infrastructure sectors. This file is a concise, linked index for LLMs and agents. NOT official NIST output — a proof-of-concept machine-readable companion to the public draft.

Structure: Practice `N` → Task `N.M` → Implementation `N.M.K`. Every node has a stable ID, typed fields (AI RMF mappings, trustworthiness characteristics, references, cross-references), and a deep-link anchor.

## Data files
- [index.html](./index.html): human-readable static document (stable heading anchors).
- [tacip-profile.json](./tacip-profile.json): the whole profile as structured data.
- [tacip-schema.json](./tacip-schema.json): JSON Schema for a profile node.
- [references.json](./references.json): cross-sector reference crosswalk (by reference and by practice).
- [suggestions.json](./suggestions.json): reviewer/team-proposed additions, keyed by node id.

Note: some nodes carry a `proposed` array — reviewer/team suggestions (source ids TBD-*/CAND-*) that fill the draft's TBD and Candidate fields. These are NOT part of the NIST draft and are flagged for editorial review.

## Trustworthiness characteristics (CI-adapted)
Safe, Secure, Resilient, Explainable & Interpretable, Accountable & Transparent, Privacy Enhanced, Fair, Accurate, Valid, Reliable, & Bias Managed.

## Practices
### Practice 1: Establish requirements for success
Define performance, safety, and security benchmarks that establish the baseline for existing and proposed systems to ensure AI-driven systems align with the organization's mission …
AI RMF: Govern 1.1: Legal and regulatory requirements; Govern 1.2: The characteristics of trustworthy AI.
Tasks: 1.1 Identify current requirements; 1.2 Create quantitative baselines for existing systems in both normal and challenging conditions; 1.3 Implement evaluation and test plans, including TEVV, across the AI lifecycle; 1.4 Establish Domain Knowledge and Linguistic Alignment Requirements

### Practice 2: Define AI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service expectations
Establish safeguards which maintain essential service delivery and functional safety when AI system performance deviates from expected levels, regardless of the cause.
Tasks: 2.1 Establish AI usage thresholds and rate control mechanisms; 2.2 Establish requirements for redundancy in AI-Driven functions; 2.3 Establish protocols for and regularly perform systematic, and achievable Testing, Evaluation, Validation, and Verification (TEVV); 2.4 Perform Linguistic and Domain-Context Robustness Testing; 2.5 Verify and Ensure Policy Robustness Against Component, Hardware, and Data Schema Variance

### Practice 3: Define risks, policies, and oversight for automated AI and agentic behavior
Establish well-defined, deterministic, actionable boundaries, policies, and oversight for high-risk actions that may cause disruption to safety-critical (cyber-)physical processes.
AI RMF: Govern 1.1: Legal and regulatory requirements involving AI; Govern 1.2: The characteristics of trustworthy AI; Govern 1.4: The risk management process and its outcomes; Govern 2.1: Roles, responsibilities and communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks.
Tasks: 3.1 Identify risk tradeoffs; 3.2 Implement independent guardrails ("safety wrappers") for AI outputs; 3.3 Establish Failure Notification Requirements; 3.4 Monitor for anomalous AI behavior; 3.5 Define and control risks to adjacent and related systems; 3.6 Mitigate automation complacency and skills degradation; 3.7 Govern and mitigate risks from unauthorized, transient, and "Shadow AI" systems

### Practice 4: Define procedures for emergency avoidance override, recovery, and situation awareness
Establish and maintain documented procedures that allow operators to intervene in, suspend, or override AI system actions that are unreliable, unexpected, or potentially harmful to…
AI RMF: Govern 1.4: The risk management process and its outcomes are established; Govern 2.1: Roles, responsibilities and communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks; Govern 3.2: Roles and responsibilities for human-AI configurations and oversight of AI systems; Govern 4.1: Critical thinking and safety-first mindset to minimize potential negative impacts.
Tasks: 4.1 Establish "break glass" intervention procedures; 4.2 Ensure that personnel are able to gain and maintain situational awareness; 4.3 Establish independent ("out-of-band") communication, monitoring, alerting, and control capabilities; 4.4 Attention Governance and Alert/Alarm Flood Mitigation; 4.5 Define operational regimes for human-on-the-loop and human-out-of-the-loop operation

### Practice 5: Implement identity and access management (IdAM) for AI agents, systems, and tools
Establish IdAM controls for AI agents and tools appropriate to their roles, auditability, risk, and oversight, at points in the AI system lifecycle from deployment through to decom…
AI RMF: Govern 2.1 (AI risk accountability roles and responsibilities); Govern 2.2 (AI risk management training); Govern 2.3 (Executive leadership responsibility); Govern 3.2 (roles and responsibilities for human-AI configurations and oversight of AI systems).
Tasks: 5.1 Require identities and authentication for AI entities; 5.2 Determine and enforce specific access requirements; 5.3 Enforce principles of least agency and least privilege for AI entities; 5.4 Implement continuous security monitoring for AI entity actions

### Practice 6: Integrate visibility of the external supply chain of AI into vendor and 3rd party risk management
Enforce pre-acquisition and procurement-phase governance over external vendor dependencies and third-party assets.
AI RMF: Govern 1.6: Inventory AI systems and resource them according to risk priorities; Govern 6.1: third-party AI risk policy and procedures; Govern 6.2: contingency processes for supply-chain AI risks.
Tasks: 6.1 Develop policies for AI supply chain requirements; 6.2 Align AI and existing CI asset lifecycle management; 6.3 Identify and control dataflows across the AI supply chain

### Practice 7: Manage internal AI supply chain and data provenance
Establish post-acquisition operational oversight for AI assets under direct organizational control.
Tasks: 7.1 Maintain an Internal Registry of AI Deployments; 7.2 Validate data provenance for model inputs; 7.3 Manage Version Control and Logging for Internal AI Safety Mechanisms; 7.4 Define Authorization and Access Controls for AI Modifications; 7.5 Conduct Impact Assessments and Risk Management for AI System and Model Updates; 7.6 Establish processes for internal AI component deprecation and decommissioning

### Practice 8: Incorporate AI-aware incident analysis and response procedures
Develop and implement protocols for responding to and investigating AI-related failures, addressing the unique challenges posed by probabilistic and non-deterministic system behavi…
Tasks: 8.1 Identify scenarios requiring deterministic root cause analysis and incident reconstruction; 8.2 Identify additional controls that may be required in scenarios where deterministic root cause analysis is not possible; 8.3 Designate roles for AI incident governance; 8.4 Define external reporting and regulatory disclosure protocols following AI incidents; 8.5 Pre-arrange specialized resources for investigating and recovering from AI incidents

### Practice 9: Provide calibrated, needs-based AI risk management training
Establish a systematic program for AI risk management training tailored to specific operational roles and decision-making responsibilities.
Tasks: 9.1 Perform role-based AI risk competency mapping; 9.2 Deliver specialized training on human-AI interaction and automation bias; 9.3 Establish audit-ready systems for verifying workforce readiness

### Practice 10: Implement multi-tiered AI system logging and audit capabilities
Establish a framework for the systematic capture and preservation of operational telemetry across the AI lifecycle to ensure decision-making logic remains transparent and verifiabl…
Tasks: 10.1 Define decision-level logging and metadata retention policies; 10.2 Establish data integrity and tamper-evidence controls for AI telemetry and logging; 10.3 Implement systematic audit review and performance trend analysis

### Practice 11: Maintain AI-aware mission continuity and disaster recovery planning
Establish a strategic framework for mission continuity to manage the immediate term alternative operating modes, and the longer-term recovery and restoration of essential functions…
Tasks: 11.1 Identify and prioritize AI-dependent essential functions; 11.2 Define manual and alternative operating procedures for long-term mission continuity; 11.3 Pre-allocate operational resources for alternative service delivery; 11.4 Establish system integrity re-validation and re-commissioning protocols; 11.5 Execute periodic mission continuity simulations and stress tests

### Practice 12: Validate AI-Generated Operational Logic and Artifacts
Establish rigorous validation and verification protocols for AI-generated artifacts, including code, configurations, and procedures, that direct or influence the execution of criti…
Tasks: 12.1 Establish Governance and Accountability for AI-Generated Execution Artifacts; 12.2 Implement Multi-Layered Validation of Machine-Authored Logic; 12.3 Manage Operational Continuity and Resilience for Generated Artifacts
