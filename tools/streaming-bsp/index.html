Vendor risk workbench for streaming Business Security Partners (BSPs)

A portfolio tool for Business Security Partners who sit with consumer and content units at streaming providers. Tier suppliers by pre-release intellectual property (IP) and member data, assign one accountable title per control, and demand evidence past a System and Organization Controls 2 (SOC 2) snapshot.

Audience Business Security Partner · Business Information Security Officer (BISO) · Content security · generative AI (GenAI) / generative media (GenMedia) advisors · Third-party risk
Practitioner aid. This page applies the Coalition for Secure AI (CoSAI) Shared Responsibility Framework (SRF) to streaming-provider BSP work. It is a proposed extension, not part of CoSAI SRF v1.0, and not an endorsement of any named supplier. Validate every control and contract term with your security, legal, privacy, and content-protection functions before use.

A Business Security Partner does not own a codec, a digital rights management (DRM) stack, or a model-serving cluster. The BSP owns the risk story across the decisions and systems in a named business portfolio: how the unit ships work, which vendors see pre-release picture or member tickets, and what must be true before a launch locks.

Historical subscription video on demand (SVOD) leak cases cluster in post-production and localization houses that held masters, not only in cloud AI platforms. GenAI and GenMedia vendors sit on the same path: scripts, stills, talent likeness, and unreleased episodes become prompts and training inputs unless a named control blocks that use. Member-facing support AI adds a second custody class: tickets, personally identifiable information (PII), and tools that can act on accounts.

Use this page per vendor: name the portfolio and lifecycle stage, pick a category, set the tier, run ROCA (risk → obligation → control → accountable) so “shared” is never the final owner, then record the treatment in the Business Risk Profile (BRP). The general AI-supplier page remains useful for categories that are not streaming-specific; this workbench reorders the map around content custody first.

Method

ROCA: risk → obligation → control → accountable

“Shared responsibility” is a starting description of who operates a control, not a finished accountability answer. For each material risk in the portfolio, complete all four steps. Stop when any step has no named owner.

1
Risk

What fails, for whom, and how bad. Name the asset: masters, scripts, talent likeness, member ticket, or agent action.

2
Obligation

The statute, studio license schedule, guild term, privacy rule, or internal policy that binds a party if the risk materializes.

3
Control

The concrete control that implements the obligation: no-train contract, isolated parser, multi-factor authentication (MFA) on the content management system (CMS), human approval before account reset.

4
Accountable

One job title that owns the control. If two parties operate it, split the activity into two rows. Do not leave the cell as “shared.”

The BSP is rarely the Accountable title for the business risk. The BSP advises and challenges before decisions lock, keeps the Business Risk Profile current, and escalates when a control has no owner. The business unit or a named specialist (content protection, DRM, platform, privacy) is usually Accountable; the vendor is Accountable only for the portion of the control it can actually implement.

BSP loop

Where vendor assessment sits in the partner loop

Vendor work is not a once-a-year questionnaire. It is how GenAI, GenMedia, and content partners enter the same advise-before-lock loop the BSP already runs for the portfolio.

Stage What the BSP does Vendor-shaped example
Context Learn how the BU makes money, ships work, and takes risk. Named relationships with BU leaders. Which post houses, localization partners, and AI tools already hold portfolio assets.
Intent Sit in reviews while the plan is still changeable. New GenMedia software as a service (SaaS) for stills; support agent that can pull PII; localization house adding AI captioning.
Assess Score what can go wrong, to whom, how bad, how likely. Apply ROCA. Category + tier + evidence package below; flag missing no-train or sub-processor disclosure.
Treat Opinionated guidance the BU can act on: ship, delay, deny, accept with conditions, remediate first. Enterprise path only; deny personal accounts for proprietary material; require human approval for account tools.
Business Risk Profile Record residual risk, owners, and review cadence. Re-open on material change. Model, sub-processor, or autonomy change triggers re-assessment, not waiting for the annual SOC 2.

Step 1

Tier the vendor before you assess it

Depth of assessment tracks the risk the vendor introduces, not the size of the contract. Score data sensitivity, autonomy, and stack depth. The highest of the three sets the tier. On a streaming path, a localization house with masters is often Tier 1.

Axis Streaming trigger
Data Pre-release picture, audio, or scripts; talent likeness or voice; member tickets and PII; forensic-watermarked screeners.
Autonomy Agent that can act in support tools, production systems, content CMS APIs, or account administration.
Stack depth Hosts models or weights; trains or fine-tunes on provider data; holds masters in a partner CMS; sits on encode, package, or DRM path.
Tier Triggers (any one) Assessment depth
Tier 1 · Critical Pre-release IP or member PII in custody; autonomous actions on production or support systems; L4/L5 model or platform hosting; masters in partner CMS. Full evidence package, contractual responsibility matrix with one accountable title per control, continuous evidence, named incident path, Content Security review where media is held.
Tier 2 · Elevated Internal business data; human-in-the-loop; application-layer AI features (L3); no masters, no account-admin tools. Core attestations plus category AI asks, data-use and sub-processor disclosure, admin-controls review.
Tier 3 · Standard Low-sensitivity data; bounded scope; no autonomy; written no-train and no retention of provider inputs. Baseline attestation (SOC 2 or International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 27001), acceptable-use terms, retention and deletion commitment.

Five families cover how streaming providers actually buy risk: houses that hold the show, GenAI tools that see the show, consumer AI that sees members, platforms that host AI, and M&A diligence cuts. Step 3

Record a treatment the BU can execute

Assessment without a decision is unfinished BSP work. Pick one treatment, name the owner, and write it into the Business Risk Profile.

Ship

Controls and evidence meet the tier. Residual risk accepted by the BU title named in ROCA.

Ship with conditions

Time-boxed gaps, compensating controls, and a re-open date. Conditions live in the BRP, not only in chat.

Remediate first

Launch blocked until named evidence arrives (no-train enforcement, MFA on CMS, kill-switch).

Delay

Architecture still changeable; wait for enterprise path, residency, or specialist review.

Vendor deny

Default for unvetted consumer GenAI on proprietary material, or partners who refuse custody minimums.

Accept with BRP entry

Rare. Requires a named Accountable title, obligation citation, and review cadence. Not “we’ll watch it.”

Step 4

Cross-cutting checks on every streaming vendor

These do not belong to one family. They apply whenever a partner builds, hosts, or services AI on the content or member path.

Pre-release IP in prompts and uploads

Scripts, stills, footage, and talent assets must ride an enterprise path with no-train enforcement, or they must not leave the authorized CMS.

Chain of custody across post and localization

Named systems, named people, watermarked review copies, and deletion on exit. Side-channel transfers are a finding, not a convenience.

Model and weight supply chain

Provenance, signing, and AIBOM for models that touch media or member data. Pass-through when a reseller embeds someone else’s model.

Training-data provenance and IP

What trained the model, opt-out handling, indemnity for IP claims from output, talent and guild consent for likeness and voice.

Sub-processors and residency

Where inference and media processing run, full sub-processor list, notice and opt-out on changes.

Agent autonomy and fail-closed halt

Human approval for high-impact acts, action audit, suspend mechanism, fail-closed when halt authority is unreachable.

Continuous vs point-in-time evidence

Re-open the BRP on model, sub-processor, or autonomy change. Annual SOC 2 alone is not enough for Tier 1.

Parsers and DRM are controls, not categories

IMF validation, isolated decode of vendor media, and content decryption module (CDM) robustness sit on the path. Vendors sit in Families A–D; do not invent a “codec vendor” family unless that party actually ships the system on a chip (SoC) or CDM.

What each attestation actually proves

Match the attestation to the risk. A SOC 2 says nothing about no-train enforcement on a GenMedia upload. An ISO 42001 says nothing about MFA coverage on a post house CMS.

Compact

Six supplier questions for a streaming BSP seat

  1. Houses that hold the show — post, VFX, localization: custody, MFA, watermark, AI-use policy, deletion.
  2. GenAI tools that see the show — enterprise path vs unvetted personal accounts.
  3. Models and inference — API hosts, open weights, third-party inference: no-train, residency, AIBOM.
  4. Agents that act — studio or support: autonomy, kill-switch, fail-closed.
  5. Platforms that host AI — cloud and MLOps isolation, keys, AI event logs.
  6. Support AI that sees members — tickets, PII, tool permissions, sub-processors.
