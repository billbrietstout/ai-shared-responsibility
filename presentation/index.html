05 / 14

Agents Execute. L1 Must Define.

The industry concentrates attention at L3 and L4. The obligations that govern agent behavior originate at L1 and L2.

L1 AI Business & Usage Business processes, use case authorization, risk tolerance, override policy Underinvested
L2 AI Information Data classification, consent, retention rules, information flows Underinvested
L3 AI Application Agent orchestration, tool calls, prompt logic, workflow execution Developer focus
L4 AI Platform Access controls, infrastructure security, runtime isolation Security focus
L5 AI Model Provider Model supply chain, training governance, capability attestation Emerging focus

Vibe coding at L3

Developers encode business logic without L1 authorization. Agents execute processes nobody formally approved.

Security starts too late

Hardening L4 access controls cannot compensate for obligations that were never defined at L1.

The correct order

L1 defines what agents are authorized to do. L2 defines what data they can use. L3 executes within those bounds.

An agent executing an undefined business process is not a governance failure at L3. It is a missing obligation at L1.

Why Open Matters

Today: Proprietary fragmentation

  • Every major AI platform has its own internal accountability model
  • Enterprise deployments build ad hoc governance in system prompts and policy docs
  • No interoperability between platforms or organizations
  • Each provider's definition of ownership is incompatible with the next
  • Regulators, auditors, and procurement teams have no common reference

Open schema: common surface

  • GRC platforms ingest it once, support all compliant deployments
  • AI gateways enforce it at the boundary without custom integration
  • Procurement teams require conformance as a standard contract clause
  • Regulators cite it as the accountability reference
  • Agent-to-agent handoffs carry portable governance context
Apache 2.0. Vendor-neutral. Maintained under OASIS open standards process.

Named Ownership Speeds Adoption Decisions

When each activity has one accountable party, procurement and incident response stop stalling on ownership questions.

Without clear accountability

  • Procurement cycles stall on vendor liability questions
  • Incident response is slow when ownership is unclear
  • Regulators apply precautionary restrictions when no party is named
  • Enterprises limit autonomy to reduce unquantifiable risk
  • Insurers price AI exposure as a black box

With machine-readable governance

  • Procurement teams verify conformance in hours, not weeks
  • Incident response follows the accountability graph immediately
  • Regulators cite the schema as the standard reference
  • Safer autonomy becomes achievable with defined bounds
  • Insurers quantify and price AI risk accurately
Clear accountability reduces friction. The cloud scaled because shared responsibility created trust. AI can scale the same way.

13 / 14

"Operational Civilization Infrastructure"

AI agents are moving from assistants to autonomous infrastructure. They will execute transactions, coordinate logistics, process claims, manage infrastructure, and make delegated decisions at a scale and velocity that has no historical parallel.

When that happens, governance, interoperability, runtime accountability, and institutional trust become as important as raw model capability.

The organizations that operate AI at scale long-term will not simply be the ones with the smartest models. They will be the ones with systems that are governable, observable, and accountable to the humans and institutions that depend on them.

Major AI platform providers have independently reached the same conclusion: enterprise trust is the binding constraint on adoption, not capability.

Without a shared accountability model, every platform risks becoming the default owner of every failure in the systems built on top of it. That is not a sustainable position at scale.

The SRF governance schema names one accountable party per activity in a vendor-neutral, open, machine-readable form that can travel with agent actions across organizational and platform boundaries.
