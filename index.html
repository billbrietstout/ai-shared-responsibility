Ambiguous ownership is a liability

When an AI incident occurs across a multi-vendor stack, the model provider points to the platform, the platform points to the application team, and the application team cites model limitations. No one fixes anything.

The same ambiguity makes regulatory compliance impossible to demonstrate. Auditors need a named owner for each control.

  • ○
    Vendor complexity
    One application, multiple model providers, cloud platforms, and agent frameworks. Isolated contracts create blind spots.
  • ○
    Incident response
    Without explicit owners for detection, containment, and remediation, teams default to finger-pointing.
  • ○
    Regulatory compliance
    Demonstrating adherence to NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, or ISO 42001 requires mapping each control to a designated owner.
  • ○
    Shadow AI
    Unsanctioned usage thrives in the gap between IT, business units, and external vendors.

The framework

Five layers. One accountable party each.

Layers are stacked in order of dependency. Governance requirements cascade from the business layer down to the model supply chain.

L1
AI Business & Usage
Governance, strategy, regulatory compliance. Cascades requirements downward.
