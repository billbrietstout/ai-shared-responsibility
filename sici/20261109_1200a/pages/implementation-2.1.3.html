Implementation 2.1.3: Implement Anomaly-Driven Detection and Throttling

This should be based on patterns of expected behavior of the AI system, particularly in situations where tight guardrails may be difficult to develop. For example, statistical process control can flag outliers in the values an AI system produces, and a session whose tool calls or token use depart from their established baseline can be throttled or paused. A Shewhart control chart can reveal a sudden shift while CUSUM and EWMA charts accumulate small deviations over time and can reveal a slow drift (see Dolin 2026). The baselines and deviation ranges come from Implementation 3.4.2: Establish Operational Baselines and Deviation Thresholds; this Implementation supplies the automatic throttling response.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which deviations from expected behavior trigger throttling, and which only raise an alert?

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Section 4.1 asks operators to implement anomaly detection and behavioral analytics, and notes that as processes mature, software safety thresholds can shift from setpoints to anomaly detection of increasingly sophisticated faults. Its worked example monitors a predictive maintenance system with statistical process control that detects outliers in predicted downtime values, which bears on detection where fixed guardrails are hard to define.
  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, Version 2026, December 2025, from the Agentic Security Initiative of the OWASP Gen AI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project. ASI08, Cascading Failures, lists rate limiting and monitoring among its mitigations, to detect fast-spreading commands and throttle or pause on anomalies, with quotas, progress caps and circuit breakers as blast-radius guardrails. This is the throttling response that this Implementation pairs with detection.
  • Research Statistically Valid Post-Deployment Monitoring Should Be Standard for AI-Based Digital Health, by P. Dolin, W. Li, G. Dasarathy and V. Berisha, Arizona State University, arXiv preprint version 3, February 2026. A position paper on clinical AI. Appendix D, on choosing a statistical test, describes Shewhart control charts for detecting sudden shifts, and CUSUM and EWMA charts for persistent small changes and gradual drift in monitored features. This bears on matching the detector to the kind of departure being throttled.
