Scenario B.14: Dosing agent misreads unusual source-water conditions

Scenario. A water treatment plant uses an SI agent to set chemical dosing from source-water quality readings. The agent was trained on several years of typical conditions. During an unusually heavy spring runoff, the source water reaches a combination of turbidity and chemistry it has never seen, and the agent extrapolates badly, driving dosing toward a dangerous level. An operator notices the change and reverses it in real time.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Safe, Robust

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Water and Wastewater Systems

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: The dosing agent is deployed without testing or defined behavior for source-water conditions outside the range it was trained on, so unusual but foreseeable conditions lead to unsafe outputs rather than a cautious fallback.
  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: The SI agent can write dosing setpoints with no independent limit on how far or how fast dosing may change, so a bad extrapolation goes straight to the process.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: Catching the unsafe change depends on an operator noticing it and reversing it in real time, which requires clear indications of what the agent has changed and a quick way to override it.
