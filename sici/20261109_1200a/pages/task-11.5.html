Task 11.5: Execute Periodic Mission Continuity Simulations and Stress Tests

Rehearsal helps an organization find gaps in its continuity plans and in the manual skills they rely on, including where they have fallen behind as an SI system’s role has grown. Conduct regular “fail-to-manual” exercises to verify that alternative operating procedures are effective and that personnel are capable of maintaining the mission under stress. Organizations that already run continuity exercises can add SI failure scenarios to those exercises. This contributes evidence of organizational preparedness that regulatory oversight may ask for, and identifies gaps in the continuity framework. These exercises should be performed both during development, to help ensure that resilience is “baked in,” and when in operation, to help ensure that resilience is maintained despite changes in the operating environment, task, and condition of the system. Scenarios can go beyond loss of availability, for example an SI system that keeps running while giving subtly wrong outputs, a compromised agent acting with valid credentials, a vendor service withdrawn without notice, or simultaneous failure of several systems that share a model or provider. Exercises can also test the decision to stop relying on the SI system, not only operation once it has stopped.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Resilient: Contributes evidence that continuity holds when tested: tabletop walk-throughs of the manual procedures and standby resources, with plans updated from what they reveal (Implementation 11.5.1: Conduct “Fail-to-Manual” Tabletop Exercises for Key Personnel), and full simulations of total AI infrastructure loss, including one whose cause is not apparent (Implementation 11.5.2: Execute High-Fidelity Simulation Drills Involving Total SI Infrastructure Loss and Recovery), in development and in operation. This Task does not cover keeping operators’ manual skills in practice between exercises, which Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Reliable: Exercising a failure of the AI system while it is in operation is itself an operational action, and can disturb the function it rehearses. Implementation 11.5.2: Execute High-Fidelity Simulation Drills Involving Total SI Infrastructure Loss and Recovery places full simulations in digital twins or dedicated test environments, which Implementation 1.3.2: Categorization of Testing Environments categorizes.
  • Secure: Exercise records and after action reports describe where continuity arrangements failed or were slow, and which scenarios personnel found hardest. An attacker could use them to choose the failure the organization is least ready for. Protecting these records is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MEASURE 2.7 (on measuring security and resiliency): This Task covers the resilience evaluation of continuity arrangements, through periodic exercises up to total AI infrastructure loss; it does not cover mapping the adversarial exposure that MEASURE 2.7 also asks to be evaluated. For that, see for example Task 3.8: Scope and Map the Expanded SI Attack Surface and Risk Surface.
  • MAP 3.4 (on defining operator proficiency): This Task covers an assessment of whether personnel can maintain the mission without the AI system under stress, including while the cause of a failure is still unknown; it does not cover keeping those skills exercised between assessments. For that, see for example Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which scenarios beyond loss of availability are exercised, including the decision to stop relying on the SI system?
  • Who plans and runs the exercises, and what staff time and budget are set aside, including cover for operational staff who take part?
