Implementation 7.6.3: Legacy Logic Vulnerability Management

Containing a deprecated SI system that has to stay in service helps the organization keep supporting the functions that depend on the system while limiting the risk the system carries. For AI systems that cannot be immediately decommissioned due to legacy constraints (e.g., dependent systems that cannot be updated), establish an “Extended Isolation Protocol.” This requires appropriate controls, such as redeployment onto isolated systems, additional monitoring, or stricter fail-over conditions, to contain risks associated with continuing to use an otherwise deprecated system. Where the developer or vendor no longer supports the SI system, continued support can come from in-house staff or from an external provider under contract. While such a system remains in service, vulnerability advisories for its runtime, libraries and supporting software should continue to be tracked, and its access to other systems limited to what its remaining dependents need. A list of deprecated SI systems kept in service, and of their unpatched components, points an attacker to the least-supported systems. Protecting it is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where vendor support has ended, is support continued in house or by an external provider under contract?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control SA-22, Unsupported System Components, asks that components be replaced when support is no longer available or that alternative support be provided. Its discussion allows exceptions for systems providing critical mission capabilities where newer technologies are not available, and notes that the increased risk can be mitigated by prohibiting connection to public or uncontrolled networks or by other forms of isolation. Applied here to deprecated SI systems, this corresponds to the Extended Isolation Protocol.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix F.7.16, the OT discussion of SA-22, notes that OT systems may contain unsupported components that have not been replaced because of operational, safety, availability or lifetime constraints, and asks organizations to identify alternative methods to continue supported operation and to consider additional compensating controls. This is the OT form of the legacy constraint this Implementation addresses.
