Implementation 1.4.3: Infrastructure Optimization

Define the specific operational states and physical constraints the system should “understand” (e.g., grid stability terminology in Energy) to help ensure optimization recommendations are technically valid. In the electric sector, for example, published definitions of System Operating Limits and Interconnection Reliability Operating Limits can supply part of this vocabulary. A system that performs well against one set of operating protocols may not carry over to another function in the same sector. The states and constraints defined should therefore be those of the functions that the system will actually support, and the scope of its testing should be taken as the range in which it is used.

References:

  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). Chapter 1, Key Terms, defines reliability, robustness and resilience for the bulk power system, with reliability measured against operational requirements such as those in NERC’s Reliability Terminology. In Chapter 3, Bias in Training Models notes that an LLM trained on one Reliability Coordinator’s operating protocols will likely not function effectively for generating regulatory reports, and that the scope of testing should be taken as the range of functions in which the tool is used.
  • Standard NERC Glossary of Terms, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC), online glossary as saved on 16 October 2026. The entries for System Operating Limit and Interconnection Reliability Operating Limit define the facility ratings, voltage limits and stability limits used to assess operating states, and the subset of those limits whose violation could lead to instability, uncontrolled separation or cascading outages. They are examples of the operational states and physical constraints that this Implementation asks to be defined.
