Scenario B.8: Pipeline operator ransomware shutdown

Scenario. An SI agent that schedules product flows for a fuel pipeline operates under a shared legacy service account with no multifactor authentication. Ransomware enters the operator’s business IT systems through that account. The pipeline’s physical controls are unaffected, but the operator cannot tell which of the agent’s recent schedules to trust and has no defined degraded mode for scheduling without it, so it shuts the pipeline down as a precaution. With no rehearsed manual scheduling process, the restart takes days, disrupting regional fuel supply.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure, Resilient

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Energy

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: The pipeline scheduling agent has no defined service-level expectations or safeguards for when its outputs cannot be trusted, so a compromise of the systems it depends on leaves a choice between running on suspect schedules and stopping the pipeline altogether.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: The SI agent operates under a shared legacy service account without multifactor authentication, so its actions cannot be distinguished from those of anyone else using the account, and one compromised credential gives ransomware a path into the systems the agent depends on.
  • Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning: No validated alternative operating mode lets the pipeline keep moving product without the SI scheduling agent, and manual scheduling has not been rehearsed, so a precautionary shutdown becomes a multi-day outage.
