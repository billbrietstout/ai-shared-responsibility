Scenario B.24: Optimizer moves a furnace outside its validated shutdown envelope

Scenario. A steel mill uses an SI optimizer to run a blast furnace for efficiency. Over time it settles on operating conditions that save fuel but lie outside the range for which the mill’s conventional shutdown sequence was validated. When attackers who have moved from the office network into the control systems disrupt the optimizer, operators trigger the standard shutdown, which does not work as designed from that state, and the furnace is seriously damaged.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Safe, Robust

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Critical Manufacturing

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: The SI optimizer can move the furnace into operating conditions where fallback and shutdown behavior has not been validated, so disruption of the optimizer leaves no safe way down.
  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: The optimizer’s operating envelope is not bounded by the conditions in which safety functions, including the shutdown sequence, are known to work.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: When the SI optimizer is disrupted, operators depend on a shutdown procedure that assumes conditions the optimizer has moved away from, and have no prepared procedure for the state the furnace is actually in.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: An SI identity that bridges office and control networks gives attackers a path from IT into the furnace-control systems.
