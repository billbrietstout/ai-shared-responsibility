Scenario B.32: Flight control sensor fault and authority handoff

Scenario. An SI-enabled flight control system receives corrupted or single-source sensor telemetry, for example from icing, hardware failure, or data spikes, and makes uncommanded control inputs or abruptly disengages automated protections. Unexpected control forces, cascading ambiguous alerts, and a rapid transfer of authority create mode confusion, so the crew cannot regain situational awareness and recover in time, risking injury or catastrophic loss.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Safe, Reliable

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Transportation Systems

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: SI-enabled control logic executes high-consequence physical commands based on unvalidated or single-source sensor feeds without deterministic cross-validation or hard boundary logic, allowing corrupted inputs to drive unsafe automated responses.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: The SI-enabled system steps down authority or disengages during an abnormal event without sufficient handoff pacing or clear “state-at-handoff” displays, leaving human operators to diagnose complex system behavior from a cold start.
  • Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust: Personnel trained primarily on nominal automation performance experience mental model lag when forced to take manual control during sudden, edge-case mode transitions, reinforcing the need for scenario-based training on degraded-mode handoffs.
