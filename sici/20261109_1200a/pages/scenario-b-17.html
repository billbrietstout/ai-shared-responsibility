Scenario B.17: Retired forecasting agent that still holds write access

Scenario. A utility replaces an SI load-forecasting agent with a new system and records the old one as retired. Two things are missed. The old agent’s service account and its write access to the dispatch system’s setpoint tables are never removed. And a conventional alarm that checked dispatch setpoints against the old agent’s forecasts, to catch implausible values, now receives no forecasts and silently stops checking. Months later, attackers find the forgotten account and use it to write false setpoints, and nothing flags them.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure, Accountable

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Energy

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: The retired agent’s write access to setpoint tables has no ongoing oversight or limits, so values written with its authority are accepted as if it were still in service.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: The retired agent’s service account remains active and unmonitored, giving attackers a trusted identity that nobody expects to be used.
  • Practice 7: Manage Internal SI Supply Chain and Data Provenance: The organization’s inventory of SI components no longer lists the retired agent, so its residual access, and the alarm that depended on its forecasts, are invisible to those responsible for the system.
  • Practice 13: Establish Procedures for Retiring SI Systems and Withdrawing Their Authority: The SI agent is recorded as retired without confirming that its credentials and write access were revoked, and without identifying what depended on it, including a protective alarm that stops working once its forecasts disappear.
