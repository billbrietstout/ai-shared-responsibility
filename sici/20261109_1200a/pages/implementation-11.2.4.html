Implementation 11.2.4: Unplanned Shutdown and Controlled Isolation Protocols

Establish documented, practiced protocols for executing controlled, rapid shutdowns of AI-dependent operational processes when automated or manual safety boundaries are intersected but where there are insufficient resources or time to continue operations on fallback legacy or manual systems. This helps prevent secondary process hazards (such as thermal or pressure excursions) during abrupt operational halts. Define in advance the conditions under which personnel should stop rather than continue on the fallback, and train them on those conditions. Attempts to keep a unit running when it should have been shut down were among the causes of the 1994 Milford Haven refinery explosion (see Health and Safety Executive 1997). Where SI-dependent assets are remote or not continually staffed, consider a means to bring them to a fail-safe state until personnel arrive, such as remote shutdown or disconnection. A remote shutdown path is only available if it was prepared in advance. It is also a route an attacker could use to stop the asset, so protect it in proportion to its impact if misused. Isolating a system can itself affect operational performance or safety, so assess each isolation option before writing it into the protocol.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Under which conditions do personnel stop rather than continue on the fallback?
  • Which isolation options are used for remote or unstaffed assets, given their effect on operations and safety?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.4.4 notes that OT components are often physically remote and not continually staffed, and that a system may need the capability to minimize impacts until personnel can arrive on-site, such as remote shutdown or disconnects. Section 6.2.4.5 notes that physically isolating a system under attack may not be possible without impacting operational performance or safety, and asks for a focused risk assessment to choose the response, which bears on the isolation half of this Implementation.
  • Incident Report Health and Safety Executive (1997) The Explosion and Fires at the Texaco Refinery, Milford Haven, 24 July 1994. Paragraph 8 of the executive summary lists attempts to keep the unit running when it should have been shut down among the combination of events behind the explosion. Recommendation 5 asks that staff training include clear guidance on when to initiate controlled or emergency shutdowns, which bears on the stop conditions this Implementation defines.
