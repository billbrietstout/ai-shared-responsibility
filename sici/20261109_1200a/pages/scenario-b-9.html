Scenario B.9: Backdoored model distributed through a trusted vendor update

Scenario. Attackers compromise a software vendor’s build process and insert a backdoor into an SI model that ships in the vendor’s signed updates to many organizations. The model behaves normally in customers’ acceptance testing and in everyday use. It misbehaves only when it sees a specific trigger that the attackers control, such as a particular phrase in an input, and customers who fine-tune or build on the model carry the backdoor into their own systems. Nothing in the model’s weights shows the backdoor to inspection.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Information Technology

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 6: Integrate Visibility of the External Supply Chain of SI into Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management: A trusted vendor ships an SI model through its signed update channel, and customers accept it on the strength of the signature and their own functional testing, neither of which can reveal a backdoor that activates only on a hidden trigger.
  • Practice 7: Manage Internal SI Supply Chain and Data Provenance: The backdoored model is deployed across many systems that fine-tune on, build on, or call it, and without provenance records it is hard to find everything that inherited it.
  • Practice 12: Independently Verify SI-Generated Operational Logic and Artifacts Across Their Lifecycle: Outputs from the backdoored model, including generated code and configurations, are relied on without independent verification, so a triggered misbehavior can propagate into the artifacts it produces.
