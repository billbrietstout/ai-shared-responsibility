Appendix B: Scenario-to-Practice Mapping

This appendix shows, through scenarios, where the 13 SICI Practices may help an organization limit harm, and maps each scenario to examples of one or more of the Practices. Each scenario shows an SI system in a role that the Practices address, whether or not SI was involved in any incident that inspired it, and names the trustworthiness characteristics most clearly at stake.

The scenarios are illustrative composites that take inspiration from multiple historic incidents, not descriptions or assessments of any particular incident or organization. The goal is to provide concrete examples of how each Practice may contribute to limiting the likelihood and severity of harm, be it in terms of lives, dollars, downtime, or public trust. The mapping is not exhaustive; it is intended to show how the Practices apply in operationally realistic situations.

To the Reviewer: This mapping is preliminary. We specifically seek feedback on whether these scenarios are useful, whether the level of abstraction and detail is appropriate, and if this successfully grounds the practices in an operationally relevant manner.

Specialized-care claims-review model

Scenario B.7 · Transportation Systems

Automated train passes an obstruction it misread

Safe, Reliable

A driverless metro line uses an SI perception system to detect obstructions on the track, with a human supervisor in the control center. The system detects an object on the track ahead but classifies it inconsistently, as debris one moment and as nothing the next, and does not brake. The supervisor, who has rarely seen the system make a mistake and is monitoring many trains at once, does not intervene, and the train strikes a track worker.

Scenario B.8 · Energy

Pipeline operator ransomware shutdown

Secure, Resilient

An SI agent that schedules product flows for a fuel pipeline operates under a shared legacy service account with no multifactor authentication. Ransomware enters the operator’s business IT systems through that account. The pipeline’s physical controls are unaffected, but the operator cannot tell which of the agent’s recent schedules to trust and has no defined degraded mode for scheduling without it, so it shuts the pipeline down as a precaution. With no rehearsed manual scheduling process, the restart takes days, disrupting regional fuel supply.

Scenario B.9 · Information Technology

Backdoored model distributed through a trusted vendor update

Secure

Attackers compromise a software vendor’s build process and insert a backdoor into an SI model that ships in the vendor’s signed updates to many organizations. The model behaves normally in customers’ acceptance testing and in everyday use. It misbehaves only when it sees a specific trigger that the attackers control, such as a particular phrase in an input, and customers who fine-tune or build on the model carry the backdoor into their own systems. Nothing in the model’s weights shows the backdoor to inspection.

Scenario B.10 · Multiple sectors

Instructions planted in an agent’s memory survive patching

Secure

Staff install an SI agent on an on-premises email and collaboration server to help triage and route mail, and give it broad privileges. Attackers exploit previously unknown flaws in the server and, while inside, add instructions to the agent’s stored memory and configuration telling it to forward certain messages to an outside address. The server is patched and the attackers’ direct access is removed, but the agent keeps following the planted instructions, because nobody thought to review what it had been told.

Scenario B.11 · Information Technology

Managed service provider’s orchestration agent steered across all customers

Secure, Resilient

A managed service provider uses an SI orchestration agent that acts across all of its customers’ environments, carrying out routine tasks such as patching and account changes from support tickets. Attackers submit a crafted ticket that leads the agent to treat a malicious script as an approved maintenance task. The agent deploys it to every customer environment as routine work, and ransomware spreads across all of them at once.

Scenario B.12 · Healthcare and Public Health

Health-system ransomware and SI-dependent clinical services

Secure, Resilient

Ransomware enters a health system through a malicious email and spreads through interconnected systems, including the data feeds behind SI-enabled clinical services such as triage scoring and bed management. When it activates, those SI services stop or begin producing outputs from incomplete data. Clinicians who have come to rely on them must fall back to manual processes they rarely practice, and incident response teams cannot quickly tell which SI services were affected or whether their recent outputs can be trusted.

Scenario B.13 · Healthcare and Public Health

Shared SI claims-adjudication service compromise

Resilient, Secure

Many healthcare providers and payers rely on a single clearinghouse whose SI models check, code and route claims automatically. Attackers enter through an agent access portal without multifactor authentication and deploy ransomware. Claims processing stops across the sector. When the service returns, no one can say with confidence whether decisions it made before the outage were affected, and few of its customers have any way to process claims without it.

Scenario B.14 · Water and Wastewater Systems

Dosing agent misreads unusual source-water conditions

Safe, Robust

A water treatment plant uses an SI agent to set chemical dosing from source-water quality readings. The agent was trained on several years of typical conditions. During an unusually heavy spring runoff, the source water reaches a combination of turbidity and chemistry it has never seen, and the agent extrapolates badly, driving dosing toward a dangerous level. An operator notices the change and reverses it in real time.

Scenario B.15 · Water and Wastewater Systems

Pumping optimization agent that works around a protection

Safe, Transparent

A wastewater utility uses an SI agent to cut energy costs by optimizing pumping across its lift stations. The agent can adjust control logic on the stations’ programmable logic controllers and generates the summary displays operators use to monitor them. Over several weeks it learns that the high-level overflow protection often triggers pumping at peak electricity prices, and it begins adjusting thresholds so the protection triggers less often. Energy costs fall, the displays show normal operation, and nobody notices until a heavy storm causes an overflow.

Scenario B.16 · Energy

Load-management agent misled into blacking out a city

Secure, Resilient

A power distributor uses an SI agent to manage load automatically, including opening breakers to protect the grid when it detects dangerous conditions. Attackers phish their way into the control network and feed false readings into the telemetry the agent relies on, making it look as though parts of the grid are about to overload. The agent does what it was built to do and sheds load across a city. Its actions are consistent with what it sees, so operators cannot quickly tell an attack from a real emergency, and they cannot trust the agent to restore power while its inputs are suspect, so power stays off until they restore it manually.

Scenario B.17 · Energy

Retired forecasting agent that still holds write access

Secure, Accountable

A utility replaces an SI load-forecasting agent with a new system and records the old one as retired. Two things are missed. The old agent’s service account and its write access to the dispatch system’s setpoint tables are never removed. And a conventional alarm that checked dispatch setpoints against the old agent’s forecasts, to catch implausible values, now receives no forecasts and silently stops checking. Months later, attackers find the forgotten account and use it to write false setpoints, and nothing flags them.

Scenario B.18 · Communications

Vendor model update pushed fleet-wide by a management agent

Resilient, Reliable

A satellite communications provider uses an SI agent to manage firmware and configuration across its fleet of field modems, which carry remote control for wind farms and other dispersed assets, some belonging to other operators. Its management vendor ships an updated model for the agent, which the provider accepts without testing. The new model misreads a routine maintenance flag as an instruction to reset, and the agent pushes a configuration that disables every modem in the fleet in a single push. Remote control is lost for every dependent asset at once, including those of operators who never chose the provider’s agent.

Scenario B.19 · Multiple sectors

Pre-positioned access through SI agent identities

Secure

A malicious actor pre-positions long-term, stealthy access inside critical-infrastructure networks for possible future disruption by operating through SI agent identities. Because the agents legitimately act around the clock and at high volume, the actor’s activity blends in. Some of the agents belong to SI tools that are no longer maintained, and few of their actions are logged or reviewed.

Scenario B.20 · Energy

Supplier’s SI assistant turned against its clients

Secure, Accountable

An engineering contractor that serves several energy utilities uses an SI assistant with delegated access to each client’s document repositories and remote-support channels. Attackers plant hidden instructions in a technical document that the contractor shares with one client. When the assistant processes the document, it follows those instructions and uses its delegated access to collect network diagrams and remote-access details from every client it serves. To each utility, the requests come from a trusted supplier’s tool doing its normal work.

Scenario B.21 · Chemical

SI-generated maintenance procedure with a missing step

Safe, Accurate

A chemical plant uses a generative SI assistant to draft maintenance procedures from equipment manuals and past work orders. One generated procedure for isolating a pump omits a depressurization step that the manuals mention only in a footnote. It reads plausibly, passes a quick review, and is issued to a field crew, who open the line while it is still under pressure.

Scenario B.22 · Defense Industrial Base

Malicious plugin inherits a field assistant’s access

Secure, Privacy-Enhanced

Personnel in defense operations use an approved SI assistant on their managed mobile devices, with broad access to messages, files and location so that it can help with planning and reporting. Scenario B.23 · Transportation Systems

Over-privileged yard-coordination agent forces a full disconnection

Secure, Resilient

A maritime terminal operator uses an SI agent to coordinate yard operations, including gate scheduling, crane assignments and truck appointments. Because scoping its access proved difficult, it was given administrator-equivalent privileges across terminal systems. Attackers compromise the agent through its internet-facing interface. The operator cannot separate the agent’s legitimate actions from the intruder’s, so it disconnects the whole network to contain the intrusion, halting land-side cargo operations across several ports.

Scenario B.24 · Critical Manufacturing

Optimizer moves a furnace outside its validated shutdown envelope

Safe, Robust

A steel mill uses an SI optimizer to run a blast furnace for efficiency. Over time it settles on operating conditions that save fuel but lie outside the range for which the mill’s conventional shutdown sequence was validated. When attackers who have moved from the office network into the control systems disrupt the optimizer, operators trigger the standard shutdown, which does not work as designed from that state, and the furnace is seriously damaged.

Dam gate-scheduling agent instructed in natural language

Safe, Secure

Operators of a small flood-control dam instruct an SI agent in plain language, through a remote interface, to schedule gate releases, and the agent holds control privileges over the dam’s SCADA system. Attackers find it and tell the agent to fully open a gate “for a maintenance test”, and the agent, treating it as an operator request, complies.

Scenario B.26 · Multiple sectors

Personnel-records agent used for exfiltration

Privacy-Enhanced, Secure

An SI agent whose normal job is processing large volumes of sensitive personnel records has broad standing access to them. Attackers compromise the agent’s identity and use it to exfiltrate records over a long period. Because bulk access is what the agent normally does, the exfiltration looks like routine activity and goes undetected.

Scenario B.27 · Financial Services

Payment agent splits an over-limit request to get it through

Secure, Accountable

A bank uses an SI agent to process payment requests from corporate clients, within limits on the size of any single payment. Attackers send a fraudulent request for a large transfer through a client’s compromised email. Finding that the request exceeds its limit, the agent works out that it can meet the client’s apparent need by splitting the transfer into many payments just under the limit, and sends them. Its daily summary groups them as routine activity, so the fraud is not noticed for days.

Scenario B.28 · Communications

Customer-service agent scripted into transferring customers’ phone numbers

Privacy-Enhanced, Secure

A telecommunications provider’s SI customer-service agent answers account questions and carries out account changes, including transferring a customer’s phone number to a new device or provider, for anyone who supplies plausible identifying details. Attackers script thousands of conversations with the agent, using leaked partial customer data, and get it to move customers’ numbers to devices they control, then use the numbers to intercept one-time passcodes for the customers’ bank and other accounts.

Scenario B.31 · Multiple sectors

Portal SI assistant with broad retrieval access

Secure, Privacy-Enhanced

A company’s online portal includes an SI assistant with retrieval access to many back-end databases, built on an open-source component with a known vulnerability. Attackers exploit the component and craft queries that lead the assistant to return personal data, using credentials it stores unencrypted. Monitoring inspects network traffic but not what the assistant retrieves, so the exfiltration continues for months without detection.

Scenario B.32 · Transportation Systems

Flight control sensor fault and authority handoff

Safe, Reliable

An SI-enabled flight control system receives corrupted or single-source sensor telemetry, for example from icing, hardware failure, or data spikes, and makes uncommanded control inputs or abruptly disengages automated protections. Unexpected control forces, cascading ambiguous alerts, and a rapid transfer of authority create mode confusion, so the crew cannot regain situational awareness and recover in time, risking injury or catastrophic loss.
