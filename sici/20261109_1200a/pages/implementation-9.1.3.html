Implementation 9.1.3: Include Data-Originating Roles in Competency Mapping

Many SI systems learn from, or act on, records created by field staff, so training those staff helps keep errors in the records from becoming errors in the SI systems’ outputs. Include personnel who create or record source data, such as field engineers, maintenance technicians, and operators completing work orders or condition reports, in the role-based mapping performed under Task 9.1: Perform Role-Based SI Risk Competency Mapping, even where those roles do not directly use or supervise an AI system. Training should cover which specific fields are consumed by downstream AI systems and analytics, what makes an individual entry usable or unusable for that purpose (for example, free text where a coded value is expected, blank fields silently treated as a default, or entries reconstructed from memory after the fact), the operational impact of a degraded record on the teams and systems that depend on it, and the correct handling of any manual transfer or synchronization step required to preserve the data. Where data capture depends on such a step, it should be treated as part of the task rather than as an administrative afterthought. Data collection indicators, such as the proportion of expected records actually captured, can help make this responsibility visible to the roles concerned. Feedback that shows these roles how the quality of their entries has affected downstream results can reinforce the training. Where these roles are measured on speed or volume, such as work orders closed per shift, those measures can reward the quickest entry over an accurate one, for example a catch-all closing code.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which data-originating roles are included?
  • Where these roles are measured on speed or volume, are those measures adjusted so they do not reward poor entries?

References:

  • Research Kang, J. et al., “A data quality management framework for equipment failure risk estimation: Application to the oil and gas industry”, Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence 136 (2024) 108834. Section 3.2.3 notes that maintenance technicians, not maintenance experts, often record the failure description in maintenance work orders, which leaves uncertainty about the accuracy of failure reports. Section 3.5 traces data loss to field technicians who skip a manual transfer and upload step, and suggests more training and data collection ratios as key performance indicators for field engineers. The case is equipment failure risk estimation in oil and gas drilling, cited here for the data-originating roles this Implementation adds to the mapping.
