Implementation 8.4.2: Establish Automated Notification and Detailed Impact Disclosure for Sector-Specific Authorities

Where regulatory timelines require rapid notification, automate the initial alert to relevant sector authorities based on the triggers defined in Implementation 8.4.1: Integrate SI-Specific Triggers into Existing Incident Disclosure and Reporting Protocols, followed by a more detailed impact disclosure prepared by the incident response team. Where release of the initial alert is automated, define in advance the conditions it covers, and test it. Otherwise, automation can detect a potentially reportable condition, preserve evidence, and assemble and route the initial report, while release to external authorities remains with the role designated under Task 8.3: Designate Roles for SI Incident Governance. The detailed impact disclosure can include, alongside the content the authority requires, the SI system and version involved, the actions it took or recommended, the physical and service consequences, and, where this is known, whether other operators using the same SI system or vendor may be exposed.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is release of the initial alert automated, and if so, which conditions does it cover?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control IR-6, Incident Reporting, reports incident information to defined authorities, with the types, content and timeliness of reports reflecting applicable laws and regulations, and enhancement IR-6(1), Automated Reporting, uses automated mechanisms for that reporting, which is the existing form of the automated initial alert to sector authorities this Implementation describes.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-61r3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management, April 2025, from NIST. Subcategory RS.CO-02 asks that law enforcement and regulatory bodies be notified of incidents based on criteria in the incident response plan and management approval, with designated individuals contacting them, and that coordination procedures set what must be reported to whom and when. That bears on keeping release with a designated role where it is not automated.
