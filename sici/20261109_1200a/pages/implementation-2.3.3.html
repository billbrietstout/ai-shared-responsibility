Implementation 2.3.3: Use Digital Twins to Regularly Test SI System Behavior

Digital twins and hardware-in-the-loop rigs let the organization test an SI system in conditions too dangerous or disruptive to create on the live process. Testing should cover anomalous conditions that are close to, or likely to result from, the system’s current operational state. For AI systems deployed in cyber-physical environments (e.g., OT, SCADA, ICS, robotics, autonomous systems), testing should extend beyond offline data replays to high-fidelity digital twins and Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) simulation environments.

Consider incorporating where relevant:

  • Physical Dynamics & Non-Linearities: High-fidelity modeling of underlying physical processes, including thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, mechanical inertia, friction, wear-and-tear, substitution of nominally equivalent parts and components through repair and maintenance, and non-linear process behaviors across nominal and extreme operating regimes.
  • Actuator Kinematics & Sensor Realism: Modeling of physical actuator response times, valve travel rates, motor saturation, sensor noise, calibration drift, signal quantization, and intermittent telemetry drops.
  • Control Network Latency & Jitter: Replication of OT/ICS communication bus characteristics (e.g., Modbus, PROFINET, CAN bus latency), network jitter, packet drops, and processing delays between the AI agent, local PLCs, and physical assets.
  • Internal Human Operator Dynamics: Simulation of operator behavioral factors, including realistic human response latencies during alarms, cognitive fatigue, manual override timing, mistimed interventions, and automation bias (both over-reliance and delayed manual takeover).
  • External Human & Social Interactions: Modeling of external human inputs and behavioral variables, including rapid consumer demand surges, pedestrian/vehicular traffic patterns (for mobile or field-deployed assets), field technician maintenance actions, and unauthorized physical access events.
  • Ambient & Exogenous Environmental Stressors: Simulation of external environmental conditions, including weather extremes (freezing, heatwaves, severe storms), ambient temperature/pressure fluctuations, electromagnetic interference (EMI), solar/radiation noise, and physical site degradation.
  • Process Upset & Fault Injection Capabilities: Deterministic fault injection for sudden equipment trips, rapid environmental shifts, sensor failure modes, and simulated cyber-physical attack vectors (e.g., spoofed sensor telemetry or delayed command execution). These should be deterministic between tests to ensure that results are repeatable and can be traced appropriately.
  • Hardware In the Loop Interfacing (HIL): Direct integration with physical programmable logic controllers (PLCs), safety instrumented systems (SIS), or hardware proxies executing real-time control cycles to validate closed-loop stability before production deployment. Where a twin or HIL rig shares networks with production control systems, separating it from them can help keep injected faults and simulated attacks from reaching live equipment. A twin detailed enough for this testing is also a detailed description of the process, and warrants the protection given to other sensitive engineering information.

While digital twins and HIL rigs offer safer environments for destructive and high-consequence stress testing, they cannot fully replicate all open-world operational variables. Testing strategies should balance twin-based evaluation with controlled, low-risk real-world testing (e.g., passive shadow mode or phased operational commissioning). In shadow mode, the AI system’s outputs are produced alongside normal operation without influencing it. In medical AI, for example, published shadow-mode evaluations have mostly reported aggregate performance metrics, and far fewer have verified outputs against ground truth in the live setting (see Tikhomirov et al. 2026). Deciding in advance how shadow-mode outputs will be checked can help. Testing strategies are discussed in Implementation 1.3.2: Categorization of Testing Environments. Evidence from a twin applies only as far as the twin has been checked against measurements from the real equipment. Where a high-fidelity twin is unavailable, lower-fidelity test environments can allow faster iterations early, with more realistic non-production systems, including hardware in the loop, used as the system matures. Continuous baseline comparisons between live sensor telemetry and digital twin predictions should be performed to identify model/twin divergence over time without exposing physical assets to unmanaged operational risk (Implementation 2.3.4: Recurrent Risk Evaluations). An AI system trained or tuned in a twin can come to rely on the twin’s simplifications, in the way Task 2.5: Verify that Learned Behavior Is Robust to Component, Hardware, and Data Schema Variance describes for beyond-specification behavior of a training system. Recording which parts of the twin have been checked against the real equipment, and to what fidelity, can help identify passing results that rest on a part of the twin known to be of low fidelity.

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Section 3.3, Conduct Thorough AI Testing and Evaluation, asks operators to test first on infrastructure designed for testing, with low-fidelity testing allowing faster iterations early and more realistic non-production systems, including hardware in the loop, as the system matures. It notes that virtualized controllers can speed testing when physical effects do not need modeling, which bears on matching test fidelity to what is being tested.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix E.2.8, Digital Twins, describes a digital twin deployed in OT as an anomaly detection tool that compares real-time sensor inputs against a baseline model. Detected anomalies most often indicate a maintenance or failure situation and can indicate an attack, which bears on this Implementation’s comparison of live telemetry with twin predictions.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing, a commission initiated by the Special Competitive Studies Project. Its Finding 9 discussion of robotics data notes that a simulation is useful only if it matches real equipment, materials and friction, and that the only way to check that match is against measurements from real machines. It adds that physical testing remains necessary, which bears on validating the twin itself.
  • Research A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence, by L. Tikhomirov and others, Nature Health, volume 1, May 2026. It reviews 75 published “silent trials”, also called shadow evaluations, in which AI outputs are produced in the intended setting without affecting operations. It finds that most reported area under the curve and similar technical metrics, and that far fewer verified outputs against ground truth in the live setting. This bears on planning how shadow-mode outputs are checked.
