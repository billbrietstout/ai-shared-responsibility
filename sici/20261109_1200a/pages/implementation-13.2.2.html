Implementation 13.2.2: Revoke Authority Delegated to Other Entities, and Record What Could Not Be Confirmed

Tracing authority that a system passed on helps an organization find actions that could continue after the system itself is retired. Where the AI system could delegate authority or create subordinate agents, trace and revoke that authority through every level it reaches. Tracing is more practical where delegation chains were kept visible in credentials and logs, for example through a correlation identifier shared by the events of one workflow, so that a chain can be reconstructed in order of delegation depth. Delegated authority is not limited to tokens. It can include tasks queued or scheduled for later execution, standing instructions or approvals left in workflow systems or with suppliers, and, in OT, schedules or setpoints written into control systems that continue to execute. Queued actions can be cancelled. An instruction that has already been acted on cannot be recalled, and its effects become a matter for Implementation 13.3.3: Determine and Record What Happens to Everything that Depended on the System. Where a delegation cannot be confirmed revoked, whether to complete the retirement on a recorded exception or to hold it open is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Completing leaves authority that may still be live, which a compensating control can cover. Holding it open leaves the retirement indefinitely incomplete, often because the means of confirmation has itself failed. Record what could not be confirmed, the decision taken and any compensating control. Under an externally imposed trigger the means of confirming revocation may itself have failed, which is when an unresolvable check is most likely.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where a delegation cannot be confirmed revoked, is the retirement closed, as a last resort, with a record of what could not be confirmed, or held open, and what compensating control covers any remaining authority? (see the Safe and Accountable versus Secure and Safe itself tension in Practice 13: Establish Procedures for Retiring SI Systems and Withdrawing Their Authority)

References:

  • Guidance Agentic Identity and Access Management, Workstream 4: Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems, from the Coalition for Secure AI. Section 3.5 requires that decommissioning revoke downstream delegated rights, and the paper’s Appendix D describes the mechanism that makes this achievable at retirement: short-lived, tightly scoped delegation tokens, a centralized revocation service supporting near real-time invalidation, and runtime components subscribing to revocation events so that they stop accepting revoked tokens rather than waiting for expiry. The paper’s Appendix E names delegation.revoked as a logged event, which is what allows a revocation to be shown rather than assumed. Its Section 1.2 core principles require delegation chains to remain visible in credentials and logs, and Appendix E gives a query that reconstructs a chain from events sharing a correlation identifier, ordered by delegation depth.
