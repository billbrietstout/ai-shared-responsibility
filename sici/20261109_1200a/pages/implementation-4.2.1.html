Implementation 4.2.1: Develop Situational Awareness Processes and Procedures for Personnel Who May Need to Intervene in the SI System’s Operation

Tailoring situational awareness to each role helps personnel intervene with the information they need, without more than they can absorb under pressure. Divide personnel into groups based on the decisions that they will be expected to make in intervening in the AI system (including assessing the need to intervene), and their expected background knowledge. Include personnel who regularly work with the system as well as those who may need to interact with the system at short notice, during an incident, and in an emergency. Include what personnel should do when conditions they observe directly do not match the picture presented by the SI system or the control room, such as whom to tell, how to record the discrepancy, and which indication to act on in the meantime. A recorded discrepancy can also feed the reporting channels in Task 8.6: Establish Non-Punitive Reporting for SI-Related Near-Misses. For example:

  • Engineers who work with the system day-to-day may require a summary of deviations from normal operation to anticipate and avoid potential incidents. These summaries help prevent important information from being buried within “normal” status reports that they may become accustomed to skimming over.
  • External maintenance engineers, who may be familiar with the equipment family but not the specific installation, may benefit from a combination of printed material and dynamic displays that highlight how this AI system’s operational learning has caused its behavior to differ from a generic or freshly deployed system, allowing them to avoid taking actions that would ordinarily be safe but may pose a risk for this specific deployment.
  • Emergency responders who may be non-technical and need to make decisions under time pressure may only need just enough information to decide which of a pre-set number of emergency modes to put the system in, such as reverting to a “safe” controller or shutting down the system completely.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • When directly observed conditions contradict the displayed picture, which indication do personnel act on, and who decides?

References:

  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). In Chapter 3, Out of the Loop Syndrome notes that new technologies are likely to convey less of the data that supports operators’ situational awareness. It proposes that operators ask whether, if the tool failed spontaneously, they would understand the state of the system well enough to keep it operating, and adds that this awareness should include what the tools have been doing over time. This bears on the deviation summaries and installation-specific briefings in this Implementation.
