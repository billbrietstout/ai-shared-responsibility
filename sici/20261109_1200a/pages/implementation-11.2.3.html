Implementation 11.2.3: Human Factors and Cognitive Transition Protocols for Control Handoffs

An agreed handover helps personnel take over from an SI system quickly and safely, whether in an emergency or a planned transition. Establish standardized interface and procedural protocols for transitioning operational authority from automated, agentic, or advisory AI systems to manual human control during emergencies, system outages, or recovery scenarios. To reduce the risk of “cold start” cognitive shock, where personnel taking over from an AI system lack immediate context regarding current system state, such as accumulated transaction backlogs, clinical patient trends, or recent automated decisions, consider the following in handover protocols:

  • Generate a State-at-Handoff Summary Display: Automatically render a concise “State-at-Handoff” screen summarizing recent AI actions, active system trends, accumulated task queues, unacknowledged alerts, and open physical or policy constraints.
  • Enforce Bounded Handoff Pacing: Where execution time constants permit (Task 4.5: Define Operational Regimes for Human-on-the-Loop and Human-Out-of-the-Loop Operation), provide a structured step-through sequence that requires personnel to review and explicitly acknowledge current operational states (e.g., active setpoints, pending financial approvals, or clinical triage statuses) before manual control fully engages. Where the time constants do not permit, consider having the safe operating mode in Implementation 11.2.2: Develop a Validated “Safe Operating Mode” that Depends Minimally on SI Infrastructure hold the process stable while personnel orient, so that they are not asked to take control during a transient.
  • Support Incremental De-escalation: Allow personnel to assume manual control over specific sub-systems, transaction queues, or decision branches while leaving adjacent automated safety or policy interlocks active, avoiding unnecessary “all-or-nothing” operational disruptions where feasible.
  • Rehearse Handoffs from a Degraded State: Include in exercises handoffs in which the SI system had been subtly wrong for some time before the handover, so that personnel practice recognizing and correcting an inherited state rather than assuming it is sound. A State-at-Handoff summary drawn from the SI system’s own records can carry the same error. Measures such as time to stable control and errors made during the transfer can show where the summary display or pacing needs revision.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where process time constants do not allow a paced handoff, does the safe operating mode hold the process while personnel orient? (see the Safe versus Safe tension in Task 11.2: Define Manual and Alternative Operating Procedures for Long-Term Mission Continuity)

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 1270, Towards a Standard for Identifying and Managing Bias in Artificial Intelligence, March 2022, from NIST. The Glossary defines loss of situational awareness bias as automation leaving people unaware of their situation, so that when control is handed back they are unprepared to assume their duties, including unawareness of what the automation is and is not taking care of. It is the failure the State-at-Handoff summary and paced step-through in this Implementation are designed against.
  • Research Endsley, M.R. and Kiris, E.O., “The Out-of-the-Loop Performance Problem and Level of Control in Automation”, Human Factors volume 37 number 2, 1995, pages 381 to 394. In an experiment that automated a navigation decision task with an expert system, operators took longer to decide after the automation failed than operators who had worked manually throughout, and this went with lower situation awareness. Intermediate levels of automation corresponded to intermediate situation awareness, which bears on the cold start this Implementation addresses and on its incremental de-escalation.
