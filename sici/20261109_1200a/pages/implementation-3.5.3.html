Implementation 3.5.3: Impact Compartmentalization (Sandboxing)

Compartmentalization limits how far a misbehaving or compromised SI system can reach, especially toward safety functions. Restrict AI agent execution to isolated micro-segments of the network or other infrastructure. For example, an AI optimizing valve pressure should not be able to affect core safety instrumented systems (SIS). Apply the same separation to the paths used to administer and update the SI system: a segment that contains safety functions should not be administered from a less critical environment. Organizations that already zone their OT networks, for example under the ISA/IEC 62443 series, can treat the SI system and its management paths as further assets to place in those zones. For robots and other physical SI systems, the paths to compartmentalize can include wireless connectivity, cloud services and remote maintenance as well as the plant network. Where legacy network architecture makes micro-segmentation infeasible, a minimal implementation is read-only integration: the SI system receives process data, for example from a replicated historian or through a one-way gateway, and has no network path by which to write to the process, so any recommendation it makes is entered by a person or by a separately controlled system. Scale the strength of isolation to the consequence of failure, for example from process or container isolation for low-consequence tasks to a separate host or network zone for SI systems near operational technology. Where practical, make compartments non-persistent, so that an agent’s execution environment is discarded and recreated, rather than cleaned, after a session that misbehaved or may have been compromised.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How strong is the isolation for each SI system, given the consequence of its failure?
  • Where micro-segmentation is infeasible, is read-only integration used instead?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement SC-7(21), Isolation of System Components, uses boundary protection mechanisms to isolate components that support different missions or business functions, and its discussion notes that such isolation limits the potential harm from both hostile cyber-attacks and errors, which supports confining an AI agent so that it cannot reach a safety instrumented system.
  • Guidance Principles of operational technology cyber security, 2024, from the Australian Signals Directorate’s Australian Cyber Security Centre, co-sealed by CISA, NSA, the FBI and cybersecurity agencies of several other countries. Principle 4, Segment and segregate OT from all other networks, notes the long-recognized need to separate more critical OT networks, such as those essential to safety, from less critical ones. It adds that a more critical environment should never be administered from a less critical environment. These bear on the SIS example and on the administration paths in this Implementation.
  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 4.1 asks operators to prefer push-based or brokered architectures that move required features or summaries out of OT without granting persistent inbound access, and to use one-way transfer patterns and audited staging buffers where data must reach business networks. This bears on the read-only integration this Implementation names for legacy networks.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing, an initiative of the Special Competitive Studies Project. Under National Framework, it notes that a modern robot often combines sensors, wireless connectivity, proprietary software, cloud services, remote maintenance and the ability to act physically inside a facility. It adds that a network-isolated packaging cell does not have the same risk profile as a connected mobile robot. This bears on the paths this Implementation compartmentalizes for physical SI systems.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-160v2r1, Developing Cyber-Resilient Systems: A Systems Security Engineering Approach, from NIST. Table D-4 lists Non-Persistent Services as an approach to the Non-Persistence technique: refreshing services periodically, or generating them on demand and terminating them when no longer needed, with the informal description that a service should not run indefinitely because it may have been compromised while executing. This bears on non-persistent compartments here.
