Task 3.9: Assess and Document Organizational Readiness for Each SI-Enabled Capability

Determine whether the governance and oversight mechanisms applicable to an SI-enabled capability are executable for the specific capability and operating context. Make this determination before deployment, and again whenever a material change occurs. For the conditions that trigger reassessment, see Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers. This assessment is capability- and context-specific rather than a general organizational maturity assessment. This does not preclude also reviewing broader readiness for the same capability, such as training, procedures, safeguards and logging. This Task adds the question of whether its governance can be carried out. An organization may have established AI policies and governance structures while still lacking the accountability, authority, escalation paths, operational capability, or enforceability necessary to govern a particular SI-enabled capability. This is distinct from Task 4.5: Define Operational Regimes for Human-on-the-Loop and Human-Out-of-the-Loop Operation, which concerns whether a person can intervene in a single machine-speed action as it happens. This Task concerns whether a governance decision, once made, can be enforced at all, be it before deployment, throughout operation, or after material change. The conditions under which a capability is retired, and the authority to decide, are identified under Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control and recorded, with the deciding authority, under Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers. Record the determination, including what was assessed, the gaps that remain, and any conditions placed on operation, such as advisory use only or a limited scope. Where a gap remains, the capability may proceed only under a recorded decision by the role that holds authority to accept that risk under the same Implementation. That decision can rest on a compensating measure, or on reducing the capability’s scope or delegated authority to what can be governed.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Accountable: Contributes the demonstration that a governance decision reaches the system’s operating state: that restriction, suspension, rollback or reduction of delegated authority takes effect, within a window set against consequence, confirmable without relying on the capability’s own report, and holding until an authorized decision changes it. This Task does not cover intervention in a single machine-speed action as it happens. Task 4.1: Establish “Break-Glass” Intervention Procedures covers procedures while Task 4.5: Define Operational Regimes for Human-on-the-Loop and Human-Out-of-the-Loop Operation covers the feasibility of intervention.
  • Valid: Contributes a readiness determination made for this capability in this operating context, re-opened when the capability, use case, delegated authority or environment materially changes, so the determination does not outlive the conditions it was made under. This Task does not cover determining whether the AI system suits the deployment, which Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Reliable: Demonstrating that a suspension or a rollback takes effect means exercising it, and exercising it on a live deployment interrupts the function it governs. Implementation 1.3.2: Categorization of Testing Environments categorizes the environments in which such exercises belong, and names the applications for which an authorized outage window is the safer place.
  • Secure: A readiness determination that is slow or demanding for low-consequence capabilities can delay their approved use and lead personnel to adopt SI tools outside it, which Task 3.7: Govern and Mitigate Risks from Unauthorized, Transient, and “Shadow SI” Systems addresses. Scaling the depth of the determination to the delegated authority and the consequence, as Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers asks, can limit this. The readiness record itself lists the gaps that remain and the conditions placed on operation. An attacker could use it to find a capability whose restriction or suspension cannot yet be enforced. Protecting it is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Accountable versus Resilient: Where the restricted capability is not the one keeping a service running, authority that holds until an authorized decision reverses it is the protection intended. Where it is, a restriction that cannot be lifted quickly extends the loss of that service. One way to balance this is to settle the restoration decision and its authority at the same time as the restriction, so reversal is an authorized path rather than an exception; others include scoping the restriction to the function at issue rather than the whole capability, which the range of decisions in Implementation 3.9.5: Demonstrate Operational Enforceability anticipates, or carrying the service on the continuity arrangements in Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning while the restriction holds.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 2.1 (on establishing roles and responsibilities): This Task identifies an accountable owner for the SI-enabled capability, and tests that escalation paths terminate with a role empowered to act rather than only to receive or record; it does not cover the operator-level pathways for responding to individual AI outputs. For those, see for example Implementation 3.3.3: Actionable Operator Decision & Escalation Pathways.
  • GOVERN 1.3 (on establishing risk management processes): This Task scales the depth of governance to the authority delegated to a capability and to the consequence of its actions, and re-opens the readiness determination on material change; it does not cover setting the thresholds that place a capability in a consequence class. For those, see for example Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses.
  • MANAGE 2.4 (on decommissioning or superseding AI systems): This Task requires evidence, before deployment, that a decision to restrict, suspend, revert or reduce a capability’s delegated authority can be made effective, verifiable and durable in the running system; it does not cover the orderly removal of an AI component at end of life. For that, see for example Task 13.2: Withdraw All Authority and Confirm the Result.
  • GOVERN 1.5 (on establishing monitoring and review processes): This Task defines the material changes that should re-open a readiness determination, which gives the review an event trigger rather than only a calendar; it does not cover setting a frequency for periodic review.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where a gap remains, does the capability proceed, and under what conditions? (see the Accountable versus Resilient tension)
  • Who performs the readiness assessment, and how independent is that person or team from those who want to deploy the capability?
