Implementation 8.3.1: Identify Internal Incident Management Points of Contact

A named point of contact for each SI system gives incident response teams someone who knows the system’s criticality, dependencies, and fallback options when an incident starts. Identify internal incident management points of contact for every AI system that can affect the organization’s mission, and ensure that they are resourced and well-practiced to foresee, prepare for, and manage incidents relating to their AI system(s). This includes both internally hosted AI systems and purchased-as-a-service systems. For a purchased-as-a-service system, the point of contact should know the division of incident responsibilities agreed with the provider, including information flows and who has authority to act on the organization’s behalf. Before an incident occurs, they maintain documentation of their system’s mission-criticality, organizational impacts, fail-over options, and dependencies. They should also name an alternate who can act when they are unavailable, with contact details kept current in the incident response plan and checked during drills. Who leads prioritization of restoration during an incident, and how restoration is sequenced against preserving what reconstruction needs, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. The point of contact knows the system’s mission-criticality and dependencies best, and is also under the most pressure to restore it quickly (see the tension in Task 8.3: Designate Roles for SI Incident Governance). Record who leads and how preservation of SI system state is sequenced with restoration. Where few people are available, for example on a night shift or at a remote site, the point of contact may also have to lead restoration. Where that happens, record it and how the conflict is offset, such as by having a second person confirm that SI system state was preserved before restoration began (Implementation 3.9.6: Keep a Record of Design Decisions and Accepted Risks).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who leads restoration, and how is preservation of SI system state sequenced with it? (see the Resilient versus Accountable tension in Task 8.3: Designate Roles for SI Incident Governance)
  • Who is the alternate for each point of contact?
  • Where does each point of contact’s time to prepare for and practice incident response come from, alongside their other duties?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-61r3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management, April 2025, from NIST. Section 2.2 notes that asset owners may have valuable insights on response and recovery priorities for their affected assets. Where a provider takes on incident response responsibilities, it asks that these be defined in a contract, with the response team aware of the division, including information flows, coordination and authority to act on behalf of the organization. That bears on the point of contact for a purchased-as-a-service system.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-34r1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, May 2010, from NIST. Appendix A, Section 2.3 of each sample contingency plan template, asks that at a minimum roles be established for a system owner or business unit point of contact, a recovery coordinator and a technical recovery point of contact. That is a per-system pattern this Implementation extends to SI systems.
