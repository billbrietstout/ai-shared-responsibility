Implementation 13.1.2: Maintain a Current Account of System Behavior, Not Only Its Specification

Maintain documentation of how the AI system behaves in operation, reconciled periodically against the behavior it was specified and accepted for. Behavior may diverge over time through adaptation, data drift, changes in upstream systems, or changes in the supply chain, such as an update to a hosted model or a library it depends on, and the divergence is not always recorded. At retirement, the difference between the two determines what is actually being removed, and it cannot be reconstructed after the system stops running. The periodic log review and trend analysis under Task 10.3: Implement Systematic Audit Review and Performance Trend Analysis and the re-evaluations under Implementation 2.3.4: Recurrent Risk Evaluations can supply the observations. Recording each observed divergence against the accepted specification, with the date it was observed, keeps the account current. The account can include the tasks the system is relied on for in practice, including any it has taken on beyond its original scope, the systems and tools it calls, the outputs that others consume, the workarounds operators use, and its known failure patterns. Operator reports and incident reviews can contribute to it, alongside monitoring.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often is the account of operational behavior reconciled against the specification, and which observations feed it?

References:

  • Research NIST AI 800-4, Challenges to the Monitoring of Deployed AI Systems, March 2026, from NIST. The Executive Summary notes that pre-deployment evaluations are mostly done in controlled testing environments that cannot account for real-world dynamics, and Section 1 that models in real-world contexts do not always perform as expected from testing. Section 3.1.3 records practitioners asking how documentation can be kept in step with systems that change. This bears on why an account of operational behavior can depart from the accepted specification.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-37r2, Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy, from NIST. Task M-4 has security and privacy plans, assessment reports, and plans of action and milestones updated on an ongoing basis from the results of continuous monitoring, so that the documented state of a system follows its monitored state. This Implementation applies the same discipline to the documented behavior of an SI system.
