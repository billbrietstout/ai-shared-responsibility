Implementation 12.1.2: Provenance and Traceability

Recording where each generated artifact came from helps an organization trace a flaw to its source and find other artifacts that may share it. (Referencing Implementation 10.1.2: Runtime Telemetry and Artifact Tracking) Establish metadata requirements that uniquely link any generated code or procedure to the specific model version, prompt, and “inference context” used to create it. The record also supports lookup in the other direction: if a model version, prompt template or source document is later found to be flawed or compromised, the record can identify the artifacts it produced so that they can be re-reviewed. Marking the artifact itself as machine-authored, for example in its version-control history, can keep it distinguishable from human-authored logic after later human edits. The record stays useful only while the model version, prompts, configuration, and other state and information that influenced the output can still be identified, which may be long after the generating SI system is updated or retired (Implementation 10.1.5: Define What Each Operational Record Is For; see also Practice 13: Establish Procedures for Retiring SI Systems and Withdrawing Their Authority). Validation leads should rely on this hard configuration data for audit paths, rather than relying on post-hoc explanation tools which can mistake downstream symptoms for the root cause of an artifact error. See Implementation 8.1.2: Identify SI Systems that May Not Satisfy Root Cause Analysis Requirements for further details. Also record who or what generated, reviewed and verified each artifact, so that overlap between them is visible. A review performed by the same process or SI system that generated the artifact should not be counted as independent verification. Where a small team means one person both generates and reviews an artifact, record this and how it is compensated, for example by a later review or an added deterministic check.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What provenance is recorded for each artifact, how long are prompts and inference context kept, and how is personal or sensitive content in them protected? (see the Secure and Privacy-Enhanced effect in Practice 12: Independently Verify SI-Generated Operational Logic and Artifacts Across Their Lifecycle)
  • How are machine-authored artifacts marked, for example in version-control history?

References:

  • Guidance NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Appendix A.1.6, Content Provenance, lists what provenance metadata can include, such as the creators of generated content, date and time of creation, modifications and sources. It notes that, combined with other organizational accountability mechanisms, such approaches can help trace negative outcomes back to their source. Written for generated content generally, it bears on the metadata this Implementation attaches to each generated code file or procedure.
  • Guidance DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 2: Human Performance Tools for Individuals, Work Teams, and Management, June 2009, from the U.S. Department of Energy. Under Checking and Verification Practices, Independent Verification has an individual separated in time and distance from the one who produced a document confirm its accuracy where an error could have adverse consequences. It notes that this tends to catch more errors because the verifier is not involved in producing the document. Written for DOE facility operations, it bears on recording who generated and who verified each artifact.
