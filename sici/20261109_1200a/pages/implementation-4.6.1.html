Implementation 4.6.1: Design Rationale Displays that Give Personnel Something to Check

Where rationale is displayed, offer concise, “one-click” or hoverable rationale summaries highlighting the primary inputs, telemetry feeds, patient factors, transaction parameters, or business rules that drove the AI recommendation. Where a summary points to evidence that personnel can check for themselves, such as a sensor reading they can confirm against a second instrument, a transaction detail they can look up, or a rule they can compare with the governing procedure, it can help them judge a recommendation under time pressure without parsing raw logs. For example, where a language model summarizes a procedure or work order, citing the document, its revision and the passage relied on lets the reader confirm that the summary says what the source says. It can also reveal a summary that reflects an earlier revision the model saw in training rather than the current one. Rationale displays can help or hinder decisions, depending on the situation. A rationale can make a recommendation more convincing without making it easier to tell when it is wrong, and rationale displays have been found to increase acceptance of incorrect recommendations as well as correct ones (see Bansal 2021). Interface designers should therefore aim for rationale that gives personnel something to check rather than something to believe. They should also consider the time available to act: for responders who need only enough information to choose an emergency mode, see Implementation 4.2.1: Develop Situational Awareness Processes and Procedures for Personnel Who May Need to Intervene in the SI System’s Operation. Where rationale is displayed to support decisions in the moment, consider testing under Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle whether it improves decisions for the personnel who will use it, including whether they still reject incorrect recommendations. For training personnel on the effect of rationale on reliance, see Task 9.2: Deliver Specialized Training on Human-SI Interaction and Automation Bias.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is displayed rationale tested under Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle to show whether it improves decisions for the personnel who use it?

References:

  • Research Bansal, G. et al., “Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance”, Proceedings of the 2021 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems. A study of crowdworkers deciding with the help of AI recommendations on text classification and reasoning tasks. Explanations were either generated post-hoc (using LIME) or written by an expert, and neither reflected the model’s actual reasoning. They tended to raise accuracy when the AI was correct and lower it when it was wrong, with no net gain over showing a calibrated confidence score. The authors note the results may not generalize to expert users in high-stakes domains.
