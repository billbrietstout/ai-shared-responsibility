Implementation 13.1.4: Establish What Retirement the Product Itself Supports

Determine, for each acquired AI product or SI-enabled device, what its supplier provides for retiring it: whether configuration and operational data can be removed, whether ownership can be transferred without carrying that data with it, and whether the product can be rendered inoperable by logical or physical means. It also includes what evidence of removal the supplier provides, and what it retains regardless, such as data in backups or data already used to train a shared model. Further questions are whether credentials and tokens the product issued or holds can be revoked from the organization’s side, and whether scheduled actions and agents it created stop when it is disabled. It also includes whether the product lets the organization keep what it would need to interpret the system’s records after retirement, such as the model version, prompts, configuration, and other state and information behind past decisions (Implementation 10.1.5: Define What Each Operational Record Is For). For embedded and field-deployed AI, this is a property of the product rather than a choice available to the operator, and it is settled at acquisition whether or not anyone asked. This includes whether an edge device or gateway retains its model or cached instructions after central retirement, what state it adopts when it loses contact, and whether removal requires a site visit or an outage window. For an SI feature embedded in a larger platform, such as a historian or an enterprise application suite, it includes whether the feature can be disabled without disabling the platform. Where the supplier provides no such capability, record this as a constraint on retirement rather than discovering it at the point of retirement. Disclosure requirements for procurement are in Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies, and the offboarding process for a component that reaches end of life is in Implementation 6.2.3: Manage End-of-Life Transitions for Externally Sourced SI Components.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which supplier retirement capabilities are required at acquisition, and which gaps are accepted and recorded as constraints on retirement?

References:

  • Guidance NIST IR 8259r1, Foundational Cybersecurity Activities for IoT Product Manufacturers, from NIST. Section 4.3.5, Product Retirement Options, sets out what a manufacturer should communicate about retiring a product, and its questions are the ones an acquirer should be asking in return: whether ownership can be transferred without the user and configuration data going with it, and whether the device can be rendered inoperable, by logical means such as an application or by physical means on the device itself. It is written to manufacturers rather than to operators, which is what makes it useful here as the other side of the same conversation.
