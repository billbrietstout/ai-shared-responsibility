How Far to Go: Measures as a Matter of Degree

Many of the measures in this profile are a matter of degree rather than something an organization either does or does not do: how independent a safeguard is, how finely access is divided, how much is logged, how often something is reviewed. The difficult decision is usually where to sit. Phrases such as “as independent as practical” or “shares as little as practical” refer to that decision.

  • Choosing where to sit: How far to go is set by the consequence of a shared failure, weighed against the resources available, which are always finite. This is a design basis decision, as when infrastructure is designed for a flood of a chosen severity rather than for any flood. It should be made deliberately for the consequences involved, and what would happen beyond it should still be understood.
  • Recording what remains: Any dependency or gap that remains is a decision, made within any regulatory or contractual obligations and accepted by someone with the authority to accept that level of risk (Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers). It is recorded with its reason, so that it can be revisited when the system, its use, or the available resources change, and checked first when something goes wrong (Implementation 3.9.6: Keep a Record of Design Decisions and Accepted Risks).
  • Preparing alternatives: Alternatives such as reverting, isolating, suspending, or switching to a backup are available only to the extent that the organization has prepared them, and each carries its own considerations, including the cost of keeping it ready.
  • Resources as a tension: Because every measure draws on finite resources, this profile does not repeat that point in every item. It names “available resources” as one side of a tension only where a measure draws on a resource that other protections also depend on, such as operators’ attention or qualified reviewers’ time, and where the right choice changes with how much of that resource there is.
  • Side effects that cannot be resourced: A side effect listed under Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider assumes that a measure addressing it elsewhere can be resourced. Where it cannot, the choice returns to the item that caused it, and becomes a tension to balance there.
