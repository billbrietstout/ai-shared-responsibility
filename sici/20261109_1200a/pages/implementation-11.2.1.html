Implementation 11.2.1: Document Basic Manual Fallback Procedures for SI-Dependent Control Points

Recording how to do an SI component’s work by hand helps personnel carry out that work when the component is unavailable or cannot be trusted, including where they have lost practice at it. Write down the specific manual steps operators would take to keep a process running if its AI component became unavailable, at a level of detail someone unfamiliar with day-to-day operation of that specific system could still follow under pressure. Keep the procedures on paper or on an offline system so that they remain available if the systems that hold them are affected too. Where a step relies on non-digital controls, such as manual valves or physical breakers, record the extra time and travel it needs. Human response through such controls can be slower than the automated control it replaces. Involve a process engineer in writing the procedures. Review the procedures when the process, its equipment, or the SI system changes, so that the steps still match the plant. Protect them as sensitive information. They describe how the process is run without the SI system, which manual controls are used, and how long each step takes, which could help an adversary time an attack or anticipate the response. Protecting them is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice, balanced against keeping them at hand for the personnel who need them during an outage.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where are the manual procedures kept, and who may access them?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.2.4.5 asks that response plans be kept in paper form or on an offline system, and that the written plan give step-by-step actions to guide personnel during confusion or stress. Section 4.1.2 describes manual control mechanisms, such as manual valve controls and physical breaker switches, and notes that non-digital mechanisms may need additional time and human involvement, which bears on how the manual steps in this Implementation are stored and timed.
  • Guidance Principles of operational technology cyber security, 2024, from the Australian Signals Directorate’s Australian Cyber Security Centre, co-sealed by CISA, NSA, FBI and agencies from seven other countries. Principle 2 asks that a process engineer be involved both when creating plans and playbooks and during investigation, containment or recovery. It also asks that plans and playbooks be regularly exercised, updated and protected because of their value to adversaries, which bears on who writes these manual procedures and how they are kept.
