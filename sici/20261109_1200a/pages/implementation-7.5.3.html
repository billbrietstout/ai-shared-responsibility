Implementation 7.5.3: Integrate Privacy Impact Assessments (PIA) into Update Cycles

Repeating a privacy impact assessment as an SI system is updated helps the organization notice when a change creates new privacy risk. Conduct a PIA before initial deployment, where one was not conducted when the SI system was developed or procured, and for all significant model or system updates to identify risks related to data minimization, re-identification, and inference. Check that updates do not bypass existing privacy controls. For agents, privacy risk can also build up within a session, where material carried across steps combines into a disclosure that no single tool call would make; see Implementation 5.3.4: Limit the Context Carried Forward Between Steps.

Updates that may warrant a PIA include adding data sources or fields to training, fine-tuning or retrieval, extending retention, giving the SI system tools that reach customer or employee records, and changing who can query it. Operational data can be personal data indirectly. For example, interval data from smart meters can indicate when a household is occupied, and camera, badge or location telemetry collected for safety monitoring can reveal the movements of individual workers. For learned systems, the assessment can include whether the updated SI system can reproduce personal data from its training or retrieval data, and whether its outputs or logs now contain information that the previous version’s did not. Record the outcome with the version identifier of the update in the change record under Implementation 7.4.1: Define and Implement Requirements for Approvals and Logging of Updates and Changes to SI Systems. A privacy finding from the PIA should be resolved or formally accepted before the update is deployed. A finding that the SI system can reproduce personal data also tells an attacker where to attempt extraction. Until it is resolved, protecting it is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which updates count as significant enough to call for a PIA?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control RA-8, Privacy Impact Assessments, calls for a privacy impact assessment before developing or procuring information technology that processes personally identifiable information, and its discussion describes the assessment as a living document that is updated whenever changes to the technology or the organization’s practices alter the privacy risks, which supports repeating it on each significant model or system update. The Personally Identifiable Information Processing and Transparency (PT) family covers the related processing authority and purpose controls.
  • Guidance NIST AI 100-2e2025, Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations, March 2025, from NIST. Section 3.3.2, Information Extraction, describes training data extraction attacks on generative language models and observes that verbatim memorization of sensitive information, such as email addresses, phone numbers and locations, has been seen in more recent transformer models. This bears on checking whether an updated SI system can reproduce personal data.
