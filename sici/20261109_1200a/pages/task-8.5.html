Task 8.5: Pre-Arrange Specialized Resources for Investigating and Recovering from SI Incidents

Identifying specialist help before an incident can shorten investigation and recovery once one occurs. Pre-identify the specialized personnel and budgetary resources needed for complex investigations, such as third-party AI forensic auditors or specialized legal counsel. Whether to go further and pre-arrange that access is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations: pre-arranged access shortens response when an incident occurs, and costs money whether or not one does. Record the level chosen and the reason for it. The market for third-party AI forensic auditors is currently nascent. Interim approaches include developing internal capability through targeted training, establishing sector-level mutual aid via ISACs and Sector Coordinating Councils, and forming partnerships with academic centers conducting AI safety, reliability, security and forensics research. This Task addresses potential internal skill gaps and helps ensure that the investigation of and recovery from non-deterministic AI failures is adequately funded and supported. In a sector-wide event, such as a flaw in a widely used foundation model, many affected operators may seek the same few specialists at once. Where an SI system is supplied or hosted by a vendor, the access that investigators will need during an incident can be agreed in the procurement terms in advance, such as access to model version histories, inference logs and vendor engineering staff, and whether a third party may examine them. Where a model was obtained outside procurement, for example downloaded directly, no such terms exist. What investigators can learn about its versions then depends on what the organization recorded itself (see Task 3.7 and Task 7.1). Practice 6: Integrate Visibility of the External Supply Chain of SI into Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management covers procurement terms more broadly.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Accountable: Contributes access, identified and ideally arranged before an incident, to the forensic and legal expertise needed to establish what a non-deterministic AI system did, with interim routes while that market is nascent: internal training, sector mutual aid and academic partnerships. This Task does not cover the records those experts will work from, which Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities covers.
  • Resilient: Contributes priority access to specialized responders through retainer arrangements (Implementation 8.5.2: Establish Formal Retainer-Style Relationships with Specialized SI Incident Responders and Auditors), so recovery does not wait on finding help during a live incident. This Task does not cover securing the operational resources that keep the function running in the meantime, which Task 11.3: Pre-Allocate Operational Resources for Alternative Service Delivery covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: Bringing in third-party forensic auditors and counsel gives people outside the organization access to logs, prompt history, model details and incident evidence. In the wrong hands, that material shows how the organization’s SI systems work and where they failed. This Task does not address the terms of that access, which are a matter for the retainer agreements (Implementation 8.5.2: Establish Formal Retainer-Style Relationships with Specialized SI Incident Responders and Auditors) and the organization’s existing third-party access controls.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MANAGE 2.1 (on allocating resources): This Task covers the budget and pre-arranged specialist access that investigating and recovering from non-deterministic AI failures need; it does not cover the resources for operating through non-AI alternatives. For that, see for example Task 11.3: Pre-Allocate Operational Resources for Alternative Service Delivery.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is access to specialized personnel and budget only pre-identified, or also pre-arranged?
  • Who holds the budget for specialist incident support, and what is the fallback if pre-arranged specialists are unavailable during a sector-wide event?
