Implementation 12.1.3: Decision Authority for Promoting Machine-Authored Logic

Deciding in advance who may put SI-generated logic into service helps an organization keep generated artifacts from entering service without an informed human approval. Define which roles (e.g., Lead Engineer, Site Supervisor) are authorized to “promote” AI-generated logic from a draft state to a production-ready operational artifact. Whether the person who promotes an artifact is someone other than the person who requested or prompted its generation is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Separating the two guards against a requester accepting their own result unexamined, and also adds a step and needs a second qualified person, whom a small site or a single-operator shift may not have. Record the arrangement and the reason for it. For the highest-consequence artifacts, the approval required should be no less than for equivalent human-authored logic, including any approval by a licensed or otherwise qualified person that existing regimes call for. These roles should sit within the decision authority documented under Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers, rather than being defined only for this artifact class. Where an organization already approves configuration changes through a body such as a Change Advisory Board, the promotion of SI-generated logic can follow that same route. Automated mechanisms can hold a generated artifact in its draft state until the designated approval is recorded. Where a promoted artifact is later executed by an automated or agentic process, action-level authorization under Implementation 5.2.2: Action-Level, Context-Aware Authorization Policies still applies at the point of execution.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which roles may promote SI-generated logic, and does promotion follow an existing change-approval route?
  • Is the person who promotes an artifact separate from the person who requested it, and what applies where a second qualified person is not available?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CM-3, Configuration Change Control, has proposed configuration-controlled changes reviewed and approved or disapproved. Its discussion counts operational procedures among those changes and names Configuration Control Boards and Change Advisory Boards as the bodies that approve them. Control enhancement CM-3(1) uses automated mechanisms to prohibit changes until designated approvals are received. These are existing structures that this Implementation’s promotion roles can sit within. Control AC-5, Separation of Duties, divides functions among different individuals or roles to reduce the risk of abuse of authorized privileges without collusion. Separating the person who requests an artifact from the person who promotes it is one such division, and it also means the artifact is reviewed by someone who did not frame the request.
