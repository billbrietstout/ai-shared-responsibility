Implementation 12.2.2: Subject Matter Expert (SME) Review of Procedures

Review by people who know the process helps an organization find plausible errors and missing warnings in a generated procedure that automated checks cannot see. For non-executable but safety-critical artifacts (like procedural summaries), conduct a “red-team” review by SMEs to identify any “plausible hallucinations” that may have introduced safety risks and any critical warnings that may have been omitted (as discussed in Implementation 2.4.3: Technical Accuracy and “Plausible Hallucination” Audits). Compare the artifact against its source material for missing constraints, exceptions and warnings, in addition to reading it for correctness. Where the artifact follows a set format, mandatory fields for warnings and limits can make an omission visible to the reviewer.

Safety-critical generated procedures can also be checked by a walkdown in the field or a run on a simulator, covering abnormal and failure conditions as well as normal operation. Many facilities already validate new or revised procedures in these ways, and this Implementation adjusts that practice for procedures an SI system drafts.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which generated procedures are also checked by a walkdown or a simulator run?
  • Which SMEs review generated procedures, how much of their time does this take, and what happens to generation when they are unavailable?

References:

  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications, Version 2026, from the OWASP GenAI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project. LLM07:2026, Misinformation, lists misleading summaries and critical omissions among its examples, where summaries omit key constraints, exceptions, timestamps or risks. Among its mitigations it asks for structured outputs with mandatory fields to detect omission failures. This bears on the omission check that this Implementation adds to SME review.
  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). Chapter 5, under Report Generation and Procedure Drafting, describes large language models drafting operating procedures from technical details that experts supply. Chapter 3, under Training for AI/ML System Use, lists validating generated content for inappropriate assumptions, conclusions and hallucinations among the skills to train. Written for electric grid operations, it is a sector example of the procedures this Implementation reviews.
  • Research Schiller, C. A., The Human Factor in Detecting Errors of Large Language Models: A Systematic Literature Review and Future Research Directions, preprint arXiv:2403.09743v1, March 2024, from the University of Duisburg-Essen. Section 6 finds that 16 of the 21 papers reviewed discuss hallucinations but only 8 discuss omissions, and that error detection in those studies relied on two or three senior domain experts. Section 7.1 calls omissions under-researched for complex processes where a missing step could be harmful. This bears on the omission check and on the reliance on expert reviewers in this Implementation.
