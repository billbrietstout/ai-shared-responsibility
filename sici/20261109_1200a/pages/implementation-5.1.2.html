Implementation 5.1.2: Establish Unique Identities for SI Service Accounts and Agents

Unique identities help keep each SI agent’s actions attributable throughout its service, including across changes to its model, configuration, or tools. Assign each AI service account or agent a unique, non-shared identifier at deployment, distinct from any human user account it may act on behalf of. For example, a grid-optimization agent and a chatbot summarizer running on the same server should never share a single service account, since doing so makes it hard to attribute an anomalous action to a specific system during a review. For the same reason, retire an identity with the agent it was issued for, rather than reassigning it, so that past records stay attributable to what produced them. A change to the model, its configuration or its tools also affects attribution. One option is to keep a persistent identity and record the model, configuration and tool versions as attributes of it, as in CoSAI’s Agentic Identity and Access Management (their Section 3.1). Another option is to issue a new identity when such a change is material. Record which option applies to each SI entity, and why.

Similarly, an AI assistant working on behalf of a person should be assigned a separate identity that allows its behavior to be logged and permissions managed separately from the person. The same applies to agents that a third party operates on the organization’s systems or data: procurement terms can require that such agents act under identities the organization can see, log and revoke, rather than under a vendor’s shared credentials. Where such an agent cannot be registered in advance, verifiable, time-bound credentials combined with conservative default permissions, such as read-only access, are one option.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which changes to a model, its configuration or its tools count as material enough to issue a new identity?
  • What do procurement terms require of agents that a third party operates, and what default permissions apply to an agent that cannot be registered in advance?

References:

  • Working Document Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization, concept paper (draft, February 2026; public comment period February 5 to April 2, 2026) from the National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence at NIST. Under Areas of Interest in Section 1, ‘Identification of AI and Software Systems’ proposes exploring means to identify software and AI agents so that access management systems can distinguish agent from human identities, and ‘Access Delegation’ proposes linking specific user identities to AI agents to maintain accountability for their actions. These describe the separation this Implementation calls for, as a project NIST is scoping rather than settled guidance.
  • Guidance Agentic Identity and Access Management, Workstream 4: Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems, version 1.0, 20 March 2026, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Section 3.1, under Unknown and third-party agents, contrasts pre-onboarding agents with assigned identities and keys with validating time-bound, verifiable credentials per request. It recommends that unknown or third-party agents be handled with verifiable, time-bound credentials plus conservative default policies such as read-only access and restricted scopes. This bears on agents that a third party operates on the organization’s systems.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control IA-4, Identifier Management, covers assigning identifiers to individuals, groups, roles, services and devices, and preventing reuse of identifiers for an organization-defined period. Its discussion explains that this means not assigning a previously used identifier to a different entity. This bears on retiring an SI entity’s identity rather than reassigning it.
