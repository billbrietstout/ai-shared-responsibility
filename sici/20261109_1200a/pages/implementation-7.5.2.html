Implementation 7.5.2: Perform Automated “Champion-Challenger” Assessment

Seeing how a prospective update behaves on the same live inputs as the production system, before its outputs are used, helps the organization decide whether to switch over. The comparison should be done by concurrently running existing production AI systems (“champion”) alongside the prospective updated AI system (“challenger”) in a “shadow mode” to determine the differences in behavior. The “challenger” system should demonstrate appropriate improvements in performance, as measured in Practice 1 and Practice 2, utilizing evaluation metrics that align with Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle, and mitigating controls established, prior to switch-over to the new system. Where actual outcomes become known, compare the outputs of both systems with those outcomes as well as with each other. A difference between the two does not by itself show which one is in error, since the champion’s output is also a prediction. A review of shadow-mode (“silent”) evaluations of medical AI found that far fewer studies verified outputs against ground truth in the setting of use than reported technical performance metrics. This process also supports Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust in training those interacting with the system, should this be required by any change in the behavior of the new system.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How long does the challenger run alongside the champion, and what improvement is needed before it replaces it?
  • Who provides the compute and reviewer time to run both systems, and what comparison is used where running both is not practical?

References:

  • Guidance SR 11-7, Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, written for banks. Section V, Model Validation, describes parallel outcomes analysis, in which the original and adjusted models’ forecasts are both tested against realized outcomes, and states that an adjusted model that does not outperform the original needs further change before it replaces it. Its discussion of benchmarking adds that a difference from a benchmark does not by itself mean the model is in error. Both bear on the champion-challenger comparison in this Implementation.
  • Research Tikhomirov, L. et al., “A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence”, Nature Health, 2026. Reviews 75 studies of “silent” evaluation, in which an ML model runs prospectively in its intended clinical setting without affecting care. It finds that most reported area under the curve and similar technical metrics, and that far fewer verified outputs against ground truth in the setting of use. This bears on what a shadow-mode comparison in this Implementation measures.
