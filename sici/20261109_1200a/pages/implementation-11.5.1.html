Implementation 11.5.1: Conduct “Fail-to-Manual” Tabletop Exercises for Key Personnel

A tabletop exercise helps an organization check its continuity plans without disturbing live operations. Run tabletop exercises at least annually, or at another frequency aligned with organizational failover testing policies, walking key personnel through a simulated AI outage, testing whether the procedures in Task 11.2: Define Manual and Alternative Operating Procedures for Long-Term Mission Continuity and the standby resources in Task 11.3: Pre-Allocate Operational Resources for Alternative Service Delivery actually work as documented, and updating the plans based on what the exercise reveals. Set the evaluation criteria before the exercise, record observations in an after action report, and assign the resulting plan updates to specific personnel. Senior decision-makers and operational staff can be exercised separately at first, then together to test coordination between them. Some elements, such as notification and communications, can be tested more often than the full exercise.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often are tabletop exercises run, and which elements, such as notification, are tested more often?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CP-4, Contingency Plan Testing, tests the contingency plan at a defined frequency to determine its effectiveness and the readiness to execute it, names walk-through and tabletop exercises among the methods, and calls for review of results and corrective action, which is the cycle this Implementation applies to fail-to-manual procedures.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-84, Guide to Test, Training, and Exercise Programs for IT Plans and Capabilities, September 2006, from NIST. Section 4 covers tabletop exercises. Section 4.2.2 has senior-level and operational-level teams exercised separately at first and then together to validate coordination, Section 4.3 asks that evaluation criteria be developed before the exercise, and Section 4.5 records the debrief and lessons learned in an after action report from which action items to update the plan are assigned, which bears on how these fail-to-manual exercises are run and followed up.
  • Guidance Cybersecurity Incident & Vulnerability Response Playbooks, November 2021, from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA). Written for Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies, its Preparation Activities ask for regular recovery exercises that test the full continuity of operations plan and failover, backup and recovery systems. They also suggest organization-specific continuity test, training and exercise activities annually, with alert, notification and communications testing quarterly, which bears on testing parts of the plan more often than the whole.
