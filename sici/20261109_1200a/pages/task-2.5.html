Task 2.5: Verify that Learned Behavior Is Robust to Component, Hardware, and Data Schema Variance

An SI system whose learned behavior depends only on what the specification guarantees is more likely to keep working when parts are replaced or a data platform changes. Check that learning systems that optimize their behavior, such as reinforcement learning systems, have not learned to rely on undocumented, unspecified, or beyond-specification behavior of their target system. Such dependence may cause unexpected suboptimal system behavior, failure, damage, or otherwise increase the attack and risk surface (see Task 3.8: Scope and Map the Expanded SI Attack Surface and Risk Surface) if the AI system is subsequently deployed on, uses, depends on, or interacts with, other systems that have identical specifications but behave differently outside of their specified parameters. The same dependence can form on a data source, where a learned behavior relies on details of a feed that its specification does not fix, such as tag names, units, sampling rates or timestamp handling, and these can change when a historian or data platform is migrated. Numerical results can also differ slightly between processors or accelerators. Where results cannot be reproduced exactly on different hardware, stating a tolerance can define what counts as the same behavior.

For example, an AI system trained to optimize pressure levels might learn to rely on a training-system valve that can physically open wider than its specification requires, where the specification defines only a minimum flow rate. If this AI system is subsequently deployed to control a different system with the same specifications, but where the valve just meets specifications, the system may behave in an undesirable manner. This Task also supports Implementation 7.5.4: Audit Interoperability for Hardware or Data Substrate Substitution.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Robust: Contributes invariance across the specification range rather than across one installation, by cycling environmental, digital and physical parameters to their full allowed limits and testing functionally equivalent parts from different manufacturers at differing states of wear (Implementation 2.5.1 and Implementation 2.5.2). This Task does not cover triggering re-testing when a component or data feed is substituted in service, which Implementation 7.5.4: Audit Interoperability for Hardware or Data Substrate Substitution covers.
  • Valid: Contributes a check that an AI system proven in one deployment is the right system for another built to the same specification, by finding learned dependence on behavior that the specification permits but does not require. This Task does not cover confirming fit against the receiving deployment’s own requirements, which Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle covers.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Robust versus Accurate: Where the assets an AI system will meet over its life genuinely span the specification range, a behavior learned across that range performs better in service. Where one installation sits at a stable point within the range, cycling parameters across the whole of it spends learning capacity on configurations that will not be met, and the resulting learned behavior may do less well on the one that is. One way to balance this is to scope the parameter cycling in Implementation 2.5.1: Train Across the Full Operating Range to the range the AI system may actually be deployed across, including planned replacements and multi-vendor substitution. Others include keeping the narrower learned behavior and treating any substitution as a re-qualification trigger under Implementation 7.5.4: Audit Interoperability for Hardware or Data Substrate Substitution.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MEASURE 2.5 (on measuring validity and reliability): This Task covers the generalizability limit that matters most in cyber-physical deployment, where a learned behavior depends on out-of-specification component behavior that was present during training; it does not cover testing generalization to unfamiliar language and terminology. For that, see for example Task 2.4: Perform Linguistic and Domain-Context Robustness Testing.
  • MAP 2.3 (on applying scientific methodologies): This Task covers representativeness as a property of the hardware and parameter space rather than of the data alone, by cycling parameters across full specification limits and testing multi-vendor parts at varying wear; it does not cover the wider TEVV design that MAP 2.3 also names. For that, see for example Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where results differ between processors or accelerators, what tolerance counts as the same behavior, and who sets it?
  • Which substitutions of parts, vendors, or data platforms count as changes that call for re-testing?
