Implementation 1.3.4: Generate and Maintain Audit-Ready TEVV Evidence

Keeping test evidence in a form others can check helps the organization show later, even without the original team, what was tested and what was found, for audits, lifecycle reviews, and incident investigations. TEVV activities should produce structured, verifiable evidence suitable for regulatory audit, lifecycle review, and post-incident evaluation.

Where evidence includes a verification result, state what the property was proven over: the AI system’s own internal model or records, or over the whole end-to-end system, including the physical process and its ground truth. A property proven over a model holds for the world only as far as the model is faithful to it.

Where the AI system’s behavior depends on its configuration, the evidence should also record the configuration tested, such as prompts, tool settings and the settings of any safety wrapper, and the test environment. Linking each result to the requirement it verifies in Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping can help show which requirements are supported by evidence. Recording who reviewed the results, who accepted them and the basis for that decision can help a later reviewer reconstruct the test without the original team.

To establish operational trust, evidence packages should pair governance evidence (test plans, dataset provenance documentation, model cards) with technical evidence (empirical evaluation results, simulation logs, boundary test receipts). Record with each result the version of the AI system, its data and its test datasets, and how each has changed since earlier testing, so that the evidence remains traceable to the system actually in service. Keeping evidence packages, configurations, and test datasets for each version has a storage and curation cost, which bears on how long they are kept. Records about a version can lose their meaning once that version is updated or retired, unless what they refer to is kept with them (see Task 10.1 and Practice 13).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How long is TEVV evidence kept, and which configuration details are recorded with it?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53Ar5, Assessing Security and Privacy Controls in Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Section 2.3, Building an Effective Assurance Case, describes compiling evidence from development artifacts and assessments, and from operational records such as remediation actions, incident reports and continuous monitoring results, into a form decision makers can use, and defines an assurance case as a body of evidence organized into an argument that a claim about a system holds, needed for properties such as safety or reliability. That parallels this Implementation’s pairing of governance evidence with technical evaluation evidence.
  • Research Mitchell, M. et al., “Model Cards for Model Reporting”, FAT* ’19: Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency, January 2019. The abstract proposes model cards: short documents that accompany trained machine learning models and give benchmarked evaluation across relevant conditions, the context in which the models are intended to be used, and details of the performance evaluation procedures. This is the source of the model cards that this Implementation lists among governance evidence.
  • Regulation DoD Manual 5000.101, “Operational Test and Evaluation and Live Fire Test and Evaluation of Artificial Intelligence-Enabled and Autonomous Systems,” December 9, 2024, a policy issuance of the United States Department of Defense, binding on DoD components. Section 4.1.b, OT\&E and LFT\&E Plans, requires plans to identify how inputs or outputs to AI components have been modified over time through data and model versioning, and how changing aspects of system performance relate to prior testing. This Implementation suggests the same versioning record for evidence outside that setting.
  • Guidance AI Shared Responsibility Framework, version 1.0, Workstream 2: Preparing Defenders for a Changing Cybersecurity Landscape, 26 May 2026, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Appendix A.7, Evidence Requirements, sorts evidence into governance, technical, operational, assessment and attestation categories. It lists by layer the artifacts each accountable party is expected to hold, such as model cards, training data documentation and red team reports from the AI model provider. This bears on what an operator assembling a TEVV evidence package can ask its suppliers for.
