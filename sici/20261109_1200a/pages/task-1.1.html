Task 1.1: Identify Current Requirements

Knowing what the current process actually has to achieve gives the organization the reference against which any SI system is judged. Catalog the functional, safety, and performance requirements for the operational processes the SI system is intended to support or augment. Include requirements that are met today only because a person is present to notice something, such as an implausible reading, or that rest on informal checks and unwritten limits. These are often absent from written procedures, and an SI system that takes over part of the task will not meet them unless they are stated. Distinguish requirements that apply regardless of who or what performs the task from those that assume a human performer, such as a licensed operator’s duties or a human-in-the-loop clause in a safety case. The latter can limit what an SI system may be permitted to do at all. Organizations with an established requirements capture process for new systems, or a management of change process, can build on that process here, with particular attention to what people currently do that is not written down. Organizations without one can start from the documentation review and walk-throughs in Implementation 1.1.1: Operational Baseline Review.

This Task helps ground SI-specific objectives in established mission-critical benchmarks, and contributes the reference for system acceptance and ongoing oversight. It also helps ensure that visibility is maintained into the expected benefits to be gained relative to the trade-offs elsewhere, and highlights when those benefits might no longer be present. Establishing operational success criteria also involves evaluating the initial Risk Surface under Task 3.8: Scope and Map the Expanded SI Attack Surface and Risk Surface, to help keep optimization gains from introducing unmitigated exposure elsewhere.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Valid: Contributes the specification of expected conditions and use: mission scope, operating envelopes, environmental parameters and functional dependencies across sensors, networks, control software, actuators and operators, recorded as a structured requirements matrix that remains auditable (Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping). This Task does not cover testing the delivered system against that specification. For that, see Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle.
  • Safe: Contributes classification by consequence: requirements that reach physical machinery, environmental safety or human life are classified as such, which is what raises verification rigor and calls for deterministic boundary constraints (Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping). This Task does not cover defining those constraints or the states entered when one is exceeded. For those, see Implementation 3.2.2 and Implementation 3.2.3.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Fair: Requirements are elicited through internal functions such as engineering, operations, safety, compliance and legal (Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping), so obligations toward the people a service reaches enter through the roles that already hold them. Because this Task is the authoritative list that later testing works from, a service obligation absent here is absent from acceptance. Task 1.4: Establish the SI System’s Domain Knowledge and Linguistic Alignment Requirements carries part of this for systems that work in natural language, by making comprehension of community language variation a success criterion. >TBD - Whether evenness of service across the populations an operator serves belongs in the requirements matrix for SI systems that allocate, prioritize or triage rather than communicate. This entry is incomplete until that is settled. Suggestions welcome.
  • Secure: The requirements matrix records operating envelopes, the adverse and adversarial conditions the system is designed to withstand, and which requirements reach physical machinery, environmental safety, or human life (Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping). An attacker could use it to find the limits the system is built around, and the functions where a failure would do most harm. Protecting the matrix is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MAP 1.1 (on defining operating context): This Task documents the operational processes, deployment settings, operating envelopes and environmental conditions that the SI system will meet, drawn from safety manuals, SOPs and SLAs that already exist; it does not cover deriving the adverse and adversarial conditions that it catalogs. For those, see for example Task 3.8: Scope and Map the Expanded SI Attack Surface and Risk Surface.
  • MAP 1.6 (on defining and communicating user requirements): This Task elicits requirements from engineering, operations, safety, compliance and legal subject matter experts and records them as a structured requirements matrix that remains auditable later; it does not cover testing the delivered system against them. For that, see for example Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle.
  • MAP 2.1 (on defining SI system task and methodology): This Task defines the operational tasks and mission scope that the SI system and the wider system are expected to perform, together with their dependencies on sensors, networks, control software, actuators and human operators; it does not cover selecting the model class or the method by which those tasks are implemented.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which expected benefits are recorded alongside the requirements, so that their loss can be noticed later?

References:

  • Guidance NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Suggested actions MP-1.1-001 and MP-1.1-002, under MAP 1.1, cover identifying the intended purposes of a generative SI system, including whether it is fine-tuned or draws on retrieval-augmented data, and documenting its expected and acceptable context of use with domain experts: assumptions and limitations, direct value to the organization, and the intended operational environment. These are the generative-AI-specific counterparts of the operational requirements this Task catalogs.
