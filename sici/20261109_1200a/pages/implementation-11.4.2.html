Implementation 11.4.2: Implement “Shadow Mode” Validation and Independent Safety Protection Checks for SI Re-Commissioning

A trial period in shadow mode helps an organization learn how the SI system behaves on the current process before control returns to it, including after changes to the system or the process since the incident. Before fully returning control to the AI system, run it in shadow mode alongside the manual process to compare outputs, and separately confirm that independent safety protections respond correctly, catching problems before the AI system resumes live authority. Compare the shadow outputs against what actually happened or what the manual process decided, not only against the SI system’s own performance metrics. A review of silent trials of medical AI found that far fewer studies checked outputs against ground truth in the live setting than reported technical performance metrics (see Tikhomirov et al. 2026). Set the length of the shadow period, or the number of operating events it should cover, and the acceptance criteria before the period begins. A shadow period exercises only the conditions that occur during it, so rare or hazardous conditions may need separate testing, for example in the digital twins of Implementation 2.3.3: Use Digital Twins to Regularly Test SI System Behavior.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What length of shadow period, or number of operating events, and what acceptance criteria are set before it begins?
  • Who compares shadow outputs with the manual process, and what extra staff time does that need while personnel are also running the process manually?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-34r1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, May 2010, from NIST. Their Section 4.4, Reconstitution Phase, describes concurrent processing: running a system at two separate locations concurrently until there is a level of assurance that the recovered system is operating correctly and securely. It is the information-system counterpart of the shadow-mode comparison in this Implementation.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 2.3.7, Safety Systems, describes a safety instrumented function as bringing a system to a safe state when predetermined thresholds are violated, and a safety instrumented system as often independent of other control systems so that a failure of the basic process control system does not impair it, which bears on confirming those protections separately from the SI system’s own behavior.
  • Research Tikhomirov, L. et al., “A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence”, Nature Health volume 1, May 2026, pages 532 to 554. A silent trial runs an AI model in its intended setting with outputs produced in parallel to, and separate from, the standard of care, a practice also called shadow evaluation. Of 75 studies reviewed, most reported area under the curve and similar technical metrics, and far fewer verified outputs against an in situ clinical ground truth. Written for pre-deployment evaluation in health care, it bears on what the shadow-mode comparison in this Implementation measures before re-commissioning.
