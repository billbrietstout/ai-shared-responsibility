Task 11.1: Identify and Prioritize SI-Dependent Essential Functions

Knowing which services depend on SI components, including as reliance grows with wider use, helps an organization plan its recovery in advance rather than decide under pressure. Catalog all infrastructure services that rely on SI components and rank them based on their impact on mission continuity and public safety. Organizations with an established business impact analysis can build on it by adding each essential function’s dependence on SI components. This Task helps ensure that restoration priorities are set as a matter of policy, so that limited recovery resources go where the organization judges them most needed. What the ranking weighs, such as life safety, service delivery and who bears a longer wait, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations that set restoration priorities; record the criteria and the reasons for them. The ranking also guides where resources go to maintain non-AI backup systems for critical functions. A function can tolerate one failure mode far better than the other, so rank against both: an SI component that keeps running but produces incorrect output, and one that becomes unavailable. The ranking can also change with the length of an outage, for example as backlogs accumulate or as personnel covering manual work tire, so consider consequence at more than one outage duration.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Resilient: Contributes the ranked list of AI-dependent functions, scored by the consequence of losing the AI component, that tells recovery where to start and which functions need non-AI backups maintained. This Task does not cover defining how a ranked function is run without the AI system, which Task 11.2: Define Manual and Alternative Operating Procedures for Long-Term Mission Continuity sets out.
  • Transparent: Contributes the map of which sector-critical functions rely on which AI components, directly or indirectly, so that the downstream reach of a single AI failure is known before an incident (Implementation 11.1.2: Conduct Comprehensive SI Dependency Mapping Across All Sector-Critical Functions). This Task does not cover recording the AI systems themselves, which Task 7.1: Maintain an Internal Registry of SI Deployments covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Fair: A ranking that directs limited recovery resources to life-safety and service-delivery functions first leaves those served by lower-ranked functions in an outage for longer. The scoring in Implementation 11.1.1: Rank Operational Processes Based on the Impact of SI Failure or Unavailability is by severity of consequence. This Task leaves to the organization whether the ranking also weighs who bears the longer wait, and asks that the criteria be recorded.
  • Secure: The map of which essential functions depend on which SI components shows where a single failure would reach furthest. An adversary who obtains it could use it to choose the SI component whose failure would disrupt the most essential functions at once. Protecting the map, and the restoration ranking with it, is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 1.6 (on building the AI inventory): This Task covers the dependent side of the inventory, recording which essential functions rely on each AI component and how directly; it does not cover recording the AI systems, versions and owners themselves. For that, see for example Task 7.1: Maintain an Internal Registry of SI Deployments.
  • MAP 5.1 (on identifying risk likelihood and impacts): This Task covers a magnitude scale for one impact, the loss or unavailability of an AI component, ranged from life-safety consequences down to administrative inconvenience; it does not cover estimating how likely that loss is.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What does the restoration ranking weigh, and which regulatory or contractual obligations set restoration priorities?
  • How is the dependency map protected, and who may see it?
  • Who maintains the ranking and the dependency map, and what prompts a review, such as an SI system taking on a new function?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CP-2, Contingency Plan, calls for a contingency plan that identifies essential mission and business functions and their contingency requirements, provides recovery objectives, restoration priorities and metrics, and addresses maintaining essential functions despite a system failure, which is the plan into which this Task’s ranking of AI-dependent functions and restoration priorities is written.
  • Guidance Principles of operational technology cyber security, 2024, from the Australian Signals Directorate’s Australian Cyber Security Centre, co-sealed by CISA, NSA, FBI and agencies from seven other countries. Principle 2, Knowledge of the business is crucial, asks critical infrastructure organizations to identify the vital systems they need to continue to provide their crucial services, and to understand the dependencies those systems have. It suggests bottom-up thinking to discover the minimal set of equipment a critical function requires, and calls understanding of the business context vital to determining recovery priorities during a critical incident, which bears on the identification and ranking this Task performs.
