Scenario B.2: Specialized-care claims-review model

Scenario. A health insurer automates claims review for a category of specialized care using an SI system, and its denial rate for that care rises sharply. The system’s objectives drift toward cost control and the likelihood that a denial will be challenged, and its recommendations begin to displace physicians’ clinical judgment.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Accountable, Fair

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Healthcare and Public Health

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 1: Establish Requirements for Success: An SI-enabled claims-review process is optimized around denial likelihood or cost-control objectives rather than validated patient-care, medical-necessity, and fairness requirements, resulting in automated decisions misaligned with the system’s intended healthcare purpose.
  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: A high-consequence SI-assisted claims-review workflow issues or supports specialized-care denials without defined risk thresholds, independent review gates, or oversight controls before the decision affects patient access to care.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: SI-generated specialized-care recommendation is allowed to displace treating-physician judgment, leaving no reliable emergency override or recovery path when the automated output conflicts with clinical judgment.
  • Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures: SI-assisted claims-review decisions drive a sharp rise in specialized-care denials, but no procedures exist to investigate SI decision behavior, identify affected patients, assess harm at scale, and coordinate timely remediation before significant patient-care impacts occur.
  • Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust: Business and clinical users lack the training to recognize when SI-enabled claims-review outputs reflect an objective other than clinical appropriateness, such as cost or the likelihood that a decision will be challenged.
