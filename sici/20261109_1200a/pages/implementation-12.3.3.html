Implementation 12.3.3: Monitoring for Latent Failures

Watching generated artifacts after they enter service helps an organization find errors that initial validation missed. Establish monitoring for systems running on AI-generated configurations to detect configuration drift, where the running configuration departs from the approved one, and latent errors that may not be apparent during the initial validation phase. Latent errors give no immediate sign when they are introduced, so controls for them rely more on detection than on prevention (see DOE-HDBK-1028-2009). Compare the running configuration against the approved, human-verified baseline at intervals. Reconcile each difference found against an approved change, so that changes made after validation are visible. Drift can also come from the supply chain, such as a vendor update to a library, firmware, or service that a generated artifact relies on. SI-generated code, documentation and procedures can also drift from, or come to misrepresent, the behavior of the system they describe as that system changes. Periodically reconcile them against current behavior, as Implementation 13.1.2: Maintain a Current Account of System Behavior, Not Only Its Specification suggests for the SI system itself. Signals worth watching include previously unexercised code paths becoming active, control responses that depart from those expected, and alarms or trips that cluster after a change. A finding can be traced to the artifact behind it through the record kept under Implementation 12.1.2: Provenance and Traceability.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often is the running configuration compared against the approved baseline, and who reconciles the differences?

References:

  • Guidance DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 1: Concepts and Principles, June 2009, from the U.S. Department of Energy. Chapter 2, Reducing Error, under Latent Errors, describes errors that go unnoticed when they occur and lie dormant, including inaccuracies embedded in procedures, and cites an NRC-sponsored study in which 81 percent of 270 errors behind 35 nuclear power events were latent. It states that methods of controlling latent errors are designed more to catch them than to stop them occurring. This bears on monitoring after initial validation.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-128, Guide for Security-Focused Configuration Management of Information Systems, August 2011, with updates as of October 2019, from NIST. Section 3.4.1, Assessment and Reporting, lists scanning to identify disparities between the approved baseline configuration and the actual configuration of a system. It reconciles changes found by monitoring with approved changes, asking who made each change and whether it corresponds to an approved change request. This is the configuration-side form of the monitoring this Implementation asks for.
