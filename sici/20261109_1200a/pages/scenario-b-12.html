Scenario B.12: Health-system ransomware and SI-dependent clinical services

Scenario. Ransomware enters a health system through a malicious email and spreads through interconnected systems, including the data feeds behind SI-enabled clinical services such as triage scoring and bed management. When it activates, those SI services stop or begin producing outputs from incomplete data. Clinicians who have come to rely on them must fall back to manual processes they rarely practice, and incident response teams cannot quickly tell which SI services were affected or whether their recent outputs can be trusted.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure, Resilient

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Healthcare and Public Health

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: SI-enabled clinical services have no defined behavior for when their data feeds are compromised or incomplete, so they either stop without warning or continue producing outputs that clinicians cannot tell are unreliable.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: A compromised identity, or an SI agent with broad standing access to clinical data feeds, lets the ransomware escalate and move laterally across interconnected systems.
  • Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures: Responders lack procedures to identify which SI services were affected, determine whether outputs produced during the compromise can be trusted, assess impacts to patient-care functions, and investigate how the disruption spread.
  • Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning: Manual fallbacks for triage, scheduling and bed management have not been rehearsed since SI-enabled services took over, so clinical operations degrade sharply while those services are down.
