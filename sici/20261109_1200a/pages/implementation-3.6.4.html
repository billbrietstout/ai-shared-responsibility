Implementation 3.6.4: Monitor Aggregate Engagement Indicators

Aggregate engagement indicators can show an organization where its interface and workload are wearing down operators’ attention, before a drill or an incident does. Consider tracking aggregate indicators of engagement at the review and approval points designed under Implementation 3.6.3: Cognitive Engagement by Design, such as the share of recommendations accepted without modification and the time spent reviewing before approval. Many of these can be drawn from the decision-level records kept under Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities. Use them to adjust the interface and workload, rather than to assess individual operators, and agree their scope with the personnel concerned. A sustained shift, such as acceptance rising toward every recommendation or review times falling, can also prompt an earlier drill under Implementation 3.6.2: Scheduled “Manual Control” Drills or a check of the skills identified under Implementation 3.6.1: Baseline Human Competency Retention.

Whether such indicators are collected, who may see them and how the resulting record is governed is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations, such as privacy law and labor agreements. Aggregate indicators can show where the design is not keeping people engaged. They also produce a record drawn from how individuals work, which can discourage candid use if its scope is not bounded. Record the scope agreed and who may see the indicators.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are aggregate engagement indicators collected, who may see them, and how is the record governed?
