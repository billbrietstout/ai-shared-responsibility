Implementation 8.2.1: Identify Applications and Situations for Which Root Cause Analysis Requirements May Be Relaxed

This Implementation helps the organization decide, and record, when a root cause requirement can be relaxed without losing sight of the risk that relaxing it accepts. It applies where Task 8.1: Identify Scenarios Requiring Deterministic Root Cause Analysis and Incident Reconstruction has identified a need for forensic root cause analysis. Many applications, SI or otherwise, carry no such requirement, and nothing here creates one. Whether to relax these requirements, and which other risk management controls are enough to do so, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Relaxing them can let a useful application proceed, and also accepts that some failures will not be traced to a cause. For example, a load-balancing optimization application might typically require forensic root-cause analysis for all actions. It may be acceptable to revise this so that it does not require forensic root-cause analysis for actions that differ by no more than a margin the organization defines and documents from a verified-safe (but sub-optimal) legacy system. For each relaxation, document why the requirement cannot be met, how those controls reduce risk, and the residual risk that is accepted. Inform those who decide on the use of the SI system of the limitation that the relaxation accepts. Revisit each relaxation when the SI system’s scope, level of autonomy or operating context changes.

For advisory uses, a qualified person can review each output before it is acted on. The reviewer needs the expertise, time and independent information to judge the output rather than simply accept it. This review can catch an unacceptable output. It does not show why the SI system produced that output. If an error gets past the reviewer, its root cause may still not be found. Record the review as one of the controls the relaxation relies on. Also record that some errors may not be traced to a cause, and who has accepted that.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which applications or situations have a relaxed root cause requirement, and which other controls does each rely on?
  • For advisory uses, does the relaxation rely on a person reviewing each output before it is acted on? If so, who accepts that some errors may still not be traced to a cause?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix F.4, Tailoring Considerations, holds that each use of compensating controls should involve a risk-based determination of how much residual risk to accept, and asks organizations to document why the baseline control could not be implemented, how the compensating controls provide equivalent capability, and the risk acceptance for any residual risk. Written for security controls in OT, it gives the record this Implementation asks for when a root cause analysis requirement is relaxed.
  • Guidance SR Letter 11-7 Attachment, Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. Section V, Model Validation, notes that banks may at times have a limited ability to use key validation tools such as back-testing. In those cases it asks for more attention to the model’s limitations when considering whether to use it, with senior management fully informed of those limitations. Written for bank models, it is a sector example of accepting a gap in analysis while keeping decision makers informed.
