Implementation 3.1.2: Evaluate Misclassification Trade-offs

To weigh one type of error against another and decide which to tolerate, assess or estimate the operational cost (financial and otherwise) and likelihoods of errors identified in Implementation 3.1.1: Identify SI System Decision Points and Potential Outcomes of SI System Errors, across normal, degraded, and emergency operating conditions. Examples include production outages, reduced safety margins, injury to people, and damage to equipment or the environment. For each decision point, record the expected rates of false positives and false negatives, and how the false positives compare with the base rate of true positives. Where the condition being detected is rare, false positives can outnumber true detections even at a low error rate. Include the cost of investigating them in the estimate.

References:

  • Research The state of artificial intelligence in public audit: Evidence from selected countries and the European Union, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 58, May 2026, from the OECD Directorate for Public Governance. Section 3, under ‘Anomaly detection and risk assessments’, sets the cost of false positives in financial audit (investigative effort spent on legitimate transactions) against that of false negatives (fraud left undetected) and calls for models to be calibrated to the trade-off. Box 3 describes a distress-prediction model that achieved a recall of 1, identifying every municipality in distress, but produced a notable number of false positives needing further investigation.
  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Section 4.1, under understanding the correctness of AI system results, asks owners and operators to understand the states in which an AI system can fail to produce accurate and reliable results, including the expected false positives and false negatives and how the false positives compare to the base rate of true positives. This is the comparison this Implementation asks for at each decision point.
