Task 8.4: Define External Reporting and Regulatory Disclosure Protocols Following SI Incidents

Establish formal procedures and relationships with external organizations for notifying sector-specific regulators and the public following an AI-related anomaly. This Task helps ensure that the organization meets its legal and transparency obligations, helping to maintain community trust and contribute to broader sector-wide knowledge of how SI systems fail, whether through attack or otherwise. Reporting timelines can start before the role of an SI system in an incident is established. The decision to notify should not wait for that role to be proven, and an initial notification can state what is not yet known.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Transparent: Contributes notification of AI incidents to sector regulators and the public, with triggers that name AI-driven causes such as model drift or automated decision error so these are reported as completely as a cybersecurity incident (Implementation 8.4.1: Integrate SI-Specific Triggers into Existing Incident Disclosure and Reporting Protocols), an automated initial alert where timelines are short, and a detailed impact disclosure after it. This Task does not cover voluntary sharing of failure modes with peer operators, which Implementation 8.6.4: Share De-Identified Incident Records for Collective Learning covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Accurate: An initial alert automated from triggers is issued before the cause is established, so it may attribute an incident to drift that later proves to be an exploit, or the reverse. Implementation 8.4.2: Establish Automated Notification and Detailed Impact Disclosure for Sector-Specific Authorities follows it with a detailed impact disclosure prepared by the incident response team, which can correct it once the triage in Implementation 8.1.6: Incident Triage Across the Dual Attack/Risk Surface has run.
  • Secure: A notification or impact disclosure describes how an SI system failed and which control did not hold. An attacker could use it to repeat the attack here or against peers running the same SI system. What a public disclosure includes, within any legal or regulatory obligation to disclose, can draw on the de-identification considerations in Implementation 8.6.4: Share De-Identified Incident Records for Collective Learning.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MANAGE 4.3 (on incident response): This Task covers the communication of AI incidents to regulators and the public, with AI-specific triggers, an automated initial alert and a detailed follow-up disclosure; it does not cover the internal processes for responding to and recovering from incidents. For those, see for example Task 8.1 and Practice 11.
  • GOVERN 1.1 (on legal and regulatory): This Task covers AI-specific triggers within the organization’s existing regulatory reporting obligations; it does not cover identifying the legal and regulatory requirements that apply to the AI system more broadly.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Beyond legal and regulatory obligations, whether, when and how is the public notified?
  • When is a notification made, relative to establishing the SI system’s role in the incident?
  • What detail about how the SI system failed is withheld from public disclosure, and on whose judgment?

References:

  • Regulation Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act, 13 June 2024, from the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union. Article 73(2) has providers of high-risk AI systems report a serious incident once a causal link with the AI system, or the reasonable likelihood of such a link, is established. Article 73(5) allows an initial report that is incomplete, followed by a complete report. It applies only to high-risk AI systems in the EU, and is one model for notifying before an SI system’s role is proven.
