Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control

Knowing in advance what could force a system’s retirement helps an organization act on a prior decision rather than improvise. Record the conditions that would initiate retirement of each AI capability, and who holds the authority to decide, under Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers. Internal triggers include a sponsor decision, a policy change, or an incident. External triggers are the ones most easily overlooked: deprecation of a foundation model, withdrawal of a third-party interface, insolvency of a vendor, a regulatory prohibition, or a trade restriction that ends access to a model or service hosted in another jurisdiction. An externally imposed trigger compresses the timeline and may remove the option of an orderly shutdown. For each external trigger, the expected notice, where a supplier’s published deprecation policy or contract states one, can be compared with the time needed to validate a replacement for the same use. Where the notice is shorter, or none is stated, record the gap: an imposed retirement would then mean a move to an unvalidated substitute or to the fallback in Implementation 11.2.2: Develop a Validated “Safe Operating Mode” that Depends Minimally on SI Infrastructure. Assign responsibility for watching for external triggers, such as supplier deprecation notices and regulatory announcements, and for bringing them to the authority that decides on retirement. The trigger list can be revisited when a supplier contract is renewed. Where processes or products have been built around one provider’s model or platform, moving to an alternative may require re-engineering them, which adds to the work an externally imposed retirement demands.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who watches for external triggers, such as supplier deprecation notices, and brings them to the authority that decides on retirement?
  • Where a supplier’s notice is shorter than the time needed to validate a replacement, is the gap accepted, covered by a fallback, or addressed in the contract?

References:

  • Research AI meets trade: Global linkages and the cross-country distribution of the gains from AI, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 57, March 2026, from the OECD Economics Department. Section 1 notes that continued access to AI models developed in other countries depends on low barriers to digital services trade, and that restrictive trade policies could fragment the global market for AI models and applications. This bears on loss of cross-border access as an external retirement trigger.
  • Research Competition in the age of AI: Initial evidence from microdata, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 62, 2026, from the OECD. Section 2 describes the switching costs faced by firms that build their operations on a particular foundation model, which would need costly re-engineering of products and processes to move, and by customers of bundled cloud AI services; note 12 describes the resulting reliance on specific providers. This bears on the external triggers this Implementation records.
