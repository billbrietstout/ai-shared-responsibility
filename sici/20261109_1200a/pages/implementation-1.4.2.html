Implementation 1.4.2: Administrative & Support

For report summarization or customer service systems, establish “Contextual Accuracy” benchmarks to verify that LLM-based tools correctly interpret industry-specific jargon without introducing “hallucinations” or misprioritizing critical alerts. Such benchmarks can include explicit checks that a summary preserves the quantities, units, dates and obligations stated in its source. Scoring such benchmarks generally depends on reviewers with domain expertise. Where reviewers disagree on whether an output is in error, recording the disagreement, rather than only a consolidated rating, can help identify the items that are hardest to judge. For testing against these benchmarks, see Implementation 2.4.3: Technical Accuracy and “Plausible Hallucination” Audits.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who scores the contextual accuracy benchmarks, and with what domain expertise?

References:

  • Guidance NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Section 2.2, Confabulation, describes confidently presented but erroneous or false content, colloquially called hallucinations. It notes that this is particularly relevant for open-ended prompts for long-form responses and in domains that require highly contextual or domain expertise, and it gives the example of a confabulated summary of patient information leading to an incorrect diagnosis. This bears on the summarization benchmarks in this Implementation.
  • Research Schiller, C. A., “The Human Factor in Detecting Errors of Large Language Models: A Systematic Literature Review and Future Research Directions”, preprint, March 2024. Section 4, Human factors in LLM error detection, finds that the reviewed studies relied on a very small number of evaluators with domain expertise, typically two or three. Where evaluators disagreed, the studies consolidated their ratings into one rather than examining why they disagreed. This bears on how contextual accuracy benchmarks are scored.
