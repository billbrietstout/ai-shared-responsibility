Scenario B.16: Load-management agent misled into blacking out a city

Scenario. A power distributor uses an SI agent to manage load automatically, including opening breakers to protect the grid when it detects dangerous conditions. Attackers phish their way into the control network and feed false readings into the telemetry the agent relies on, making it look as though parts of the grid are about to overload. The agent does what it was built to do and sheds load across a city. Its actions are consistent with what it sees, so operators cannot quickly tell an attack from a real emergency, and they cannot trust the agent to restore power while its inputs are suspect, so power stays off until they restore it manually.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure, Resilient

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Energy

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: The SI agent can open breakers across a whole city on its own judgment, without limits on how much load it may shed, how quickly, or when a human must confirm, so a manipulated picture of the grid turns directly into a blackout.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: The SI agent’s actions look justified by the telemetry it sees, so operators need an independent view of grid conditions, and a way to override the agent, to tell manipulation from a real emergency.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: Phished credentials give attackers access to telemetry sources the SI agent trusts, and the agent does not distinguish authenticated sources from injected readings.
  • Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning: With the SI agent and its inputs untrusted, grid operation must continue manually while the system is investigated, which requires rehearsed manual restoration procedures and staff who can perform them.
