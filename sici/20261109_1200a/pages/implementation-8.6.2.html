Implementation 8.6.2: Protect Reporter Identity Through an Independent Reporting Route

A route that protects reporter identity helps personnel raise concerns about an SI deployment with less fear for their role or standing. Provide a reporting route that does not require the reporter to disclose their identity to the function responsible for the deployment. Where internal anonymity is impractical, or where the deployment function has influence over the reporter’s role, work or standing, route reporting to an independent recipient outside the organization that can receive it in confidence. The sector-level pooling described in Task 8.5: Pre-Arrange Specialized Resources for Investigating and Recovering from SI Incidents may provide such a recipient, but a body that supports investigation is not by that fact an independent or confidential one. Make clear to potential reporters, in advance, how a report will be handled, who will see it, and what it will and will not be used for, so that the route can be used with confidence. In a small workforce, such as a single control room or crew, details in a report such as shift times, unit numbers or wording can identify the reporter. These details can be generalized before a report reaches the deployment function. Where follow-up with the reporter is needed, the recipient can hold the reporter’s identity separately from the report and discard the link once follow-up is complete.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Do reports go to an independent recipient outside the organization, and if so, which one?

References:

  • Guidance DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 1: Concepts and Principles, June 2009, from the U.S. Department of Energy. Chapter 4, Culture & Leadership, lists among the attributes of a strong safety culture that a variety of methods are available for personnel to raise safety issues without fear of retribution. It notes that if individuals believe their errors will be punished, information about those errors will likely remain obscure. Written for DOE facility operations, it bears on offering a route besides the deployment function.
  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). In Chapter 3, under Organizational Level, Vendor Transparency compares disclosure of SI issues to near-miss database systems, which can require well-aligned key performance indicators, anonymity of reporter and vendor, and a third party, such as NASA for the Aviation Safety Reporting System, for the information to keep flowing. That is a precedent for the independent recipient in this Implementation.
