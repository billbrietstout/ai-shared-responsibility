Implementation 13.3.2: Treat the Retirement of a Protective SI Capability as a Change to Controls

Treating the retirement of a protective SI capability as a change to controls helps an organization keep the protection in place, or know where a gap remains. Where an AI system performs a detection, monitoring, screening or filtering function, its retirement removes a control and should be processed as a control change as well as an asset disposal. Establish what the system was detecting, including behavior it had learned to detect that is not documented elsewhere, and what will perform that function afterward. Before the capability is withdrawn, verify through defined testing that the successor or compensating control covers what is required, for example by running the two in parallel and comparing their outputs, and retain the evidence. Where equivalent coverage cannot be established first, record the residual risk, the interim compensating measures, the role with authority to accept that risk, and a date for review. Some frameworks and obligations, such as SP 800-37, limit the delegation of risk acceptance. A replacement that begins without this information starts without the accumulated basis of the one it replaces, and the resulting gap may not be visible from either system’s records. The operators and analysts who acted on the retired system’s alerts should be told that the absence of an alert no longer carries the meaning it did. Where a successor takes over, its alerts can differ in which events they flag, how often they occur and how often they are false, and the people receiving them should be told what will change. Documents that cite the capability as a control, such as a system security plan, a safety case or hazard analysis, or compliance evidence provided to a regulator, should be updated to record its removal and what replaces it.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What testing shows that a successor or compensating control provides the required coverage, and who accepts any gap until it does?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-37r2, Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy, from NIST. Task M-7, System Disposal, calls for any control inheritance relationships to be reviewed and assessed for impact when a system is removed from operation. Where other systems inherit controls from the disposed system, this review shows what protection they lose. A detection, monitoring or filtering AI system can play that role whether or not it is formally recorded as a control provider, and this Implementation extends the review to behavior it had learned to detect that is documented nowhere else. Their Task R-4 and Appendix D also state that the explicit acceptance of risk is the responsibility of the authorizing official and cannot be delegated.
