Task 8.3: Designate Roles for SI Incident Governance

Settling incident roles before an incident, rather than during one, helps an organization act quickly when an SI system is involved. How AI incident roles are organized, whether as a standing cross-functional AI Incident Response Team (AIRT), an extension of an existing incident response team or another arrangement, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Decisions during an AI incident can turn on legal, technical, risk management and communications considerations, which argues for drawing those perspectives in; a separate team can also run in parallel to existing incident command and add coordination cost. Record the arrangement and how each perspective is represented. This Task helps ensure that the high-level management of an incident is supported by a “digital chain of command” capable of making informed decisions regarding liability, public safety, and mission continuity. Where the organization already uses a standardized incident management structure, such as the Incident Command System within the National Incident Management System, the AIRT can work within it, with expertise in the SI systems involved available to the incident commander, so that the digital chain of command does not run in parallel to it. Its decision rights during response should be defined so that they do not overlap with the incident commander’s.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Accountable: Contributes a named point of contact for every AI system that can affect the mission, purchased-as-a-service systems included, who keeps its criticality, impacts, fail-over options and dependencies documented before an incident (Implementation 8.3.1: Identify Internal Incident Management Points of Contact), and a standing cross-functional team able to decide on liability, public safety and continuity. This Task does not cover establishing ownership of the capability’s governance decisions outside an incident, which Implementation 3.9.1: Establish Accountable Ownership covers.
  • Resilient: Contributes leadership of recovery: evidence-based prioritization to restore the most mission-critical functions first, and oversight of recovery and re-commissioning (Implementation 8.3.2: Form an SI Incident Governance Team). This Task does not cover setting the restoration priorities or the re-validation tests, which Task 11.1 and Task 11.4 set out.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: The documentation that each point of contact keeps under Implementation 8.3.1: Identify Internal Incident Management Points of Contact, covering mission-criticality, impacts, fail-over options, and dependencies, maps which SI systems matter most and what the organization falls back on. An attacker could use it to choose targets, or to time an attack for when the fallback is also unavailable. Contact lists and decision rights also show whom to impersonate to have an SI system taken offline. Protecting this material is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Resilient versus Accountable: Where the state of the AI system at the time of the incident is already held independently of it, restoring the most critical functions first costs little evidence. Where restoration means a rollback, restart or model change that overwrites the context, prompts or version in use, or the system is remote-hosted and its state is not the operator’s to keep, restoring first can remove what reconstruction needs. One way to balance this is to make documenting the state of the AI systems at the time of the incident, which Implementation 8.1.3: Update Existing Incident Response Policies and Playbooks adds to incident playbooks, an early response step; others include relying on records protected under Task 10.2: Establish Data Integrity and Tamper-Evidence Controls for Telemetry and Logging so that restoration need not wait.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 2.1 (on establishing roles and responsibilities): This Task covers the incident roles: a point of contact for each AI system and a standing governance team spanning legal, technical, risk and communications; it does not cover roles for AI risk outside an incident. For accountable ownership of the capability, see Implementation 3.9.1: Establish Accountable Ownership.
  • MANAGE 4.3 (on incident response): This Task covers the people who lead tracking, response and recovery for each AI system, including purchased-as-a-service systems; it does not cover communicating incidents to regulators and the public. For that, see for example Task 8.4: Define External Reporting and Regulatory Disclosure Protocols Following SI Incidents.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are AI incident roles organized as a standing AIRT, an extension of an existing incident response team or another arrangement, and how do they fit any existing incident command structure?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.4.2, Response Communications, notes that the FEMA National Incident Management System (NIMS) can be used to standardize common terminology and roles for incident response. Its OT-specific guidance assigns roles that could include incident commander and lists a designated incident commander among internal resources. Written for cybersecurity incidents in OT, it bears on placing the AIRT within an existing incident management structure.
