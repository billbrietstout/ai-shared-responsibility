Task 7.6: Establish Processes for Internal SI Component Deprecation and Decommissioning

Establish secure, structured protocols for the sunsetting, deprecation, and decommissioning of internally developed AI agents, systems, tools, models, training datasets, and associated software dependencies. This Task helps ensure that when internal AI assets reach end-of-life, they are safely removed from production environments without breaking legacy system dependencies or leaving behind unmonitored attack surfaces. This Task covers the artifacts: the models, datasets, pipelines and tools themselves. Where the organization has system disposal and media sanitization practice, this Task can build on it. The withdrawal of an operating AI system’s authority, the preservation of what it knew, and the decisions recorded for everything that depended on it are in Practice 13: Establish Procedures for Retiring SI Systems and Withdrawing Their Authority.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Secure: Contributes destruction or secure archiving of retired models, training pipelines and datasets so that “zombie models” are not exfiltrated. This Task does not cover withdrawing the credentials, triggers and delegated authority of a retiring system, which Task 13.2: Withdraw All Authority and Confirm the Result covers.
  • Safe: Contributes destruction or controlled archiving of retired models so that one retired for inappropriate behavior is not accidentally put back into use, and isolation of deprecated systems that cannot yet be removed so that their faults are contained. This Task does not cover deciding when a model should be retired, which Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control covers.
  • Resilient: Contributes the dependency audit and deprecation timeline that allow downstream IT, OT and ICS systems to be refactored or reverted to deterministic fallbacks before a model is turned off. This Task does not cover recording a decision for each dependent, which Implementation 13.3.3: Determine and Record What Happens to Everything that Depended on the System adds.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Secure and Safe versus Accountable: Where a retired model is destroyed, nothing remains to be exfiltrated, or to be put back into use by mistake. Where it was retired because it behaved inappropriately, destruction also removes what an investigation or a later claim may need to examine. One way to balance this is the choice Implementation 7.6.2: Secure Archiving and Destruction of Model and Other SI Artifacts already offers between destruction and secure archiving, made artifact by artifact; others include capturing the evidence identified under Task 13.3: Preserve What Is Needed and Hand Over What Depended on the System before anything is destroyed.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 1.7 (on establishing decommissioning processes): This Task covers the artifact side of phasing out safely: dependency mapping before turn-off, secure archiving or destruction, and isolation for what cannot yet be retired; it does not cover withdrawing the authority of an AI system that is still operating. For that, see for example Task 13.2: Withdraw All Authority and Confirm the Result.
  • MANAGE 2.4 (on decommissioning or superseding AI systems): This Task covers disposal of superseded models, including those found to behave inappropriately, so that they are not accidentally resurrected; it does not cover the decision to deactivate or the conditions that prompt it. For that, see for example Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is each retired artifact destroyed or archived? (see the Secure and Safe versus Accountable tension in Task 7.6: Establish Processes for Internal SI Component Deprecation and Decommissioning)
  • Who owns retiring an internal artifact whose original developer or team has moved on or left?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-37r2, Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy, from NIST. Task M-7, System Disposal, calls for a disposal strategy when a system is removed from operation, implementation of disposal controls such as media sanitization, configuration management and record retention, updates to inventory and tracking systems, notification of users and application owners hosted on the system, and review of any control inheritance relationships. It applies equally to system elements removed from operation, which is the level at which individual models, datasets and pipelines are retired.
