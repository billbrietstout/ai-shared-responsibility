Scenario B.13: Shared SI claims-adjudication service compromise

Scenario. Many healthcare providers and payers rely on a single clearinghouse whose SI models check, code and route claims automatically. Attackers enter through an agent access portal without multifactor authentication and deploy ransomware. Claims processing stops across the sector. When the service returns, no one can say with confidence whether decisions it made before the outage were affected, and few of its customers have any way to process claims without it.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Resilient, Secure

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Healthcare and Public Health

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: A single SI claims-adjudication service is relied on by much of the sector without validated availability and integrity expectations, so its failure halts claims processing everywhere at once, and the integrity of its earlier decisions is uncertain.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: An agent access portal to the clearinghouse lacks multifactor authentication and least privilege, and is compromised.
  • Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures: Responders lack procedures to determine whether SI decisions made before and during the compromise can be trusted, assess sector-wide impacts, prioritize restoration, and communicate consequences to the many organizations that depend on the service.
  • Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning: Dependent providers and payers have no alternative way to process claims without the shared SI service, and its decisions cannot easily be reproduced elsewhere, so the outage becomes a prolonged financial disruption.
