Implementation 7.6.2: Secure Archiving and Destruction of Model and Other SI Artifacts

Define destruction (cryptographically secure as appropriate) or secure archiving standards for retired models, training pipelines, and datasets. This helps prevent “zombie models” from being accidentally used, resurrected or exfiltrated. This may be particularly important for models that were found to behave inappropriately, do not comply with regulations, are found to contain illegal or otherwise undesirable content, or found to be susceptible to reverse engineering and leakage of confidential or proprietary information. Archived models can be kept as encrypted copies with recorded hashes in a location protected against tampering, with the keys and hash values stored apart from the encrypted copies. Before storage media that held data for SI systems are repurposed or disposed of, they can be sanitized by a method such as cryptographic erase, block erase or overwrite. An archived model can also serve later as a challenger or baseline under Implementation 7.5.2: Perform Automated “Champion-Challenger” Assessment. Before destruction or archiving, identify the artifacts and copies concerned, which can include weights and checkpoints, adapters, embeddings and retrieval indexes, prompts, safety-wrapper configurations, and copies held in backups, on edge devices or on developer workstations. An archive intended to allow a model to be examined or run again should keep the runtime, dependency versions and configuration with the weights. The weights alone may not reproduce its behavior. Weights and embeddings can be classified as at least as sensitive as the data they were derived from. Training data can sometimes be extracted from a model, and stored embeddings can be inverted to recover source text.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How long are archives kept, and are they kept in a form that can be run again?

References:

  • Research Supervision of Artificial Intelligence in Finance: Challenges, Policies and Practices, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 54, January 2026, from the OECD Directorate for Financial and Enterprise Affairs. Section 2.2 observes that robust governance calls for retaining each model instance rather than only the latest version, because a problem such as biased output can surface after a model is fixed and cannot then be investigated from the latest version alone. This bears on this Implementation’s choice between destruction and secure archiving for models found to behave inappropriately.
  • Guidance Deploying AI Systems Securely: Best Practices for Deploying Secure and Resilient AI Systems, Version 1.0, April 2024, from the National Security Agency’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center with CISA, the FBI and partner agencies. Under Validate the AI system before and during use, it asks that hashes and encrypted copies of each release of the model and system be archived in a tamper-proof location, with hash values or keys kept apart from the encrypted copies. This is a concrete form of the secure archiving this Implementation asks for.
  • Guidance AI Data Security: Best Practices for Securing Data Used to Train & Operate AI Systems, Version 1.0, May 2025, from the National Security Agency’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center with CISA, the FBI and partner agencies. Best practice 9, Delete data securely, asks that drives used to store and process data for SI systems be erased before they are repurposed or decommissioned, by cryptographic erase, block erase or data overwrite, and points to NIST SP 800-88. This is the media side of the destruction this Implementation asks for.
  • Guidance Supervisory Statement SS1/23, Model risk management principles for banks, May 2023, updated 16 April 2026, from the Prudential Regulation Authority of the Bank of England, which sets its expectations for UK banks with internal model approval. A footnote to Principle 1.2 notes that the rationale for decommissioning a model can inform later model development, and that the decommissioned model may be used as a challenger model itself. This is a further reason to archive rather than destroy.
  • Guidance NIST AI 100-2e2025, Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations, March 2025, from NIST. Section 3.3.2, Information Extraction, describes training data extraction attacks on generative language models and observes that verbatim memorization of sensitive information has been seen in more recent transformer models. This bears on handling retired weights at the sensitivity of their training data.
  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications, Version 2026, August 4, 2026, from the OWASP GenAI Security Project, a volunteer community project. LLM09:2026, Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, states under Embedding Inversion that stored embeddings can be inverted to recover source text, and that vector-database backups and exported embeddings should be treated as equivalent to a leak of the underlying documents. This bears on archiving or destroying retrieval indexes under this Implementation.
