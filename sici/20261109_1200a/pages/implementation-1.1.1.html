Implementation 1.1.1: Operational Baseline Review

A baseline review shows the organization what the targeted process is required to achieve today, including what is done in practice but not written down. Perform a baseline review of existing operational documentation, such as safety manuals, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and service level agreements (SLAs), for the targeted process. Compare the written procedures with how the process is actually run, through walk-throughs, shift observation or interviews with operators, and record where the two differ, including workarounds. Recent incident and near-miss reports, permits and contracts can also hold requirements that the procedures do not state.

Distill these into a “Requirements Summary” that explicitly lists measurable success criteria (e.g., latency or throughput targets) along with minimum and ideal thresholds for performance. Where the SI system is later developed or supplied by one party and validated by another, both can agree on these thresholds before testing, so that the same thresholds serve validation, pre-deployment checks and monitoring. This implementation differs from Task 1.2: Create Quantitative Baselines for Existing Systems in Both Normal and Challenging Conditions by focusing on establishing a baseline from which improvements can be measured.

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 2.1 closes with a fictional example assessment of SI for predictive maintenance on an industrial generator. It states requirements, corresponding safety and security requirements and success metrics as measurable targets, such as a 25% reduction in downtime, a 15% decrease in maintenance costs and a cap of 80% on network bandwidth for data aggregation. It is a worked example of the measurable success criteria and thresholds that a Requirements Summary lists.
  • Commentary Artificial Intelligence Model Risk Management: Observations from a Thematic Review, Information Paper, December 2024, from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, drawn from a review of banks. Paragraph 6.3.8 reports that most banks set criteria or thresholds that define acceptable performance, and that these thresholds need to be clearly defined, documented and agreed by developers and validators. It adds that such thresholds were usually specific to the use case and could later support validation, pre-deployment checks, monitoring and change management. This is a financial-sector example of the agreed thresholds this Implementation asks for.
