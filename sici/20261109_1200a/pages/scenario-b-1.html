Scenario B.1: Clinical risk-prediction model

Scenario. A widely used SI model for predicting a medical condition misses many positive cases in independent validation, while generating enough false alarms to cause alert fatigue.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Accurate, Valid

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Healthcare and Public Health

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 1: Establish Requirements for Success: An SI clinical decision-support model is deployed for risk prediction without verified, real-world performance acceptance criteria, so clinicians and hospitals rely on vendor-reported accuracy that does not hold under operational conditions.
  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: The SI prediction model misses many positive cases and performs inconsistently across hospitals and care-timing contexts, because robustness, calibration, and site-specific quality-of-service expectations are not validated before scaled clinical use.
  • Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust: SI-generated alerts have low positive predictive value, creating alert fatigue and reducing appropriate trust in the model’s outputs during high-consequence clinical decision-making.
