Task 10.3: Implement Systematic Audit Review and Performance Trend Analysis

Reviewing the accumulated record regularly helps an organization notice problems that no single output reveals, such as slow degradation. Define formal procedures for the periodic review of AI system logs to identify model drift, emerging biases, or performance anomalies. Organizations that already review audit logs on a schedule can extend that review to look for these signs in an SI system’s record. This Task turns raw telemetry into findings that someone can act on, allowing those the organization designates to receive review findings to detect degrading model reliability or “silent” failures before they escalate into mission-critical incidents or cause quality of service issues. Those who receive the findings need the authority, time, and resources to act on them, or a defined route to someone who has. Operator override and rejection rates are one signal to review alongside measures of drift. A rising rate can indicate degrading outputs, and a falling rate can indicate either improved outputs or reduced scrutiny by personnel (Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation). Drift can also come from the supply chain, for example when a provider changes a hosted model or a data source changes, and not only from changes in inputs (see Task 6.2: Align SI and Existing CI Asset Lifecycle Management).

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Reliable: Contributes periodic review of AI system logs, manually at a defined cadence by qualified personnel and automatically through trend dashboards, to catch slow degradation and “silent” failures, including those the manual review looks for because automated monitoring may not yet be tuned to catch them. This Task does not cover detecting acute anomalies as they occur, which Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior covers.
  • Bias Managed: Contributes review of the accumulated record for biases that emerge over time in operation, rather than only at evaluation. This Task does not set out how a bias found this way is to be removed, compensated for, or bounded.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MEASURE 2.4 (on monitoring deployed AI systems): This Task covers periodic and trend-based review of production logs for drift and slow-developing degradation between formal audit cycles; it does not cover real-time detection. For that, see for example Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior.
  • MEASURE 3.1 (on tracking known and emerging risks): This Task supports tracking of emergent risk by turning accumulated telemetry into trends that surface drift, emerging biases and performance anomalies; it does not cover setting the thresholds at which a trend requires mitigation. For that, see for example Implementation 3.3.2: Establish Maximum Drift Thresholds.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who receives review findings?
  • What change in override or rejection rates prompts a closer look?
  • Who performs the manual review, with what qualifications, and how is their time set aside from other duties?
  • Who decides what follows a finding, and by when?

References:

  • Commentary Artificial Intelligence Model Risk Management: Observations from a Thematic Review, Information Paper, December 2024, from the Monetary Authority of Singapore. Paragraph 6.5.7 reports banks conducting periodic reviews of their AI portfolio, covering changes in materiality, risks, scope and usage, performance, assumptions and limitations. Paragraph 6.5.8 reports revalidation of AI in production at an intensity and frequency based on its materiality. Written as observed good practice at selected banks, it is a sector example of the periodic review procedures this Task defines.
