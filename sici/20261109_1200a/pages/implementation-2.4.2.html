Implementation 2.4.2: Localization and Community Validation

For customer-facing or public-sector systems, test the AI’s robustness against regional dialects and community-specific terminology to help ensure consistent and accurate delivery of information across the service area. For public alerts and customer notices, testing can also cover whether outputs can be understood across the range of reading levels in the service area. Report results separately for each language variety in scope, since an aggregate score can hide underperformance for one community. Where feasible, involve members of the communities served in preparing and reviewing test material.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which communities and language varieties are included in validation, and how are their members involved?

References:

  • Guidance NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, from NIST. Section 2.6, Harmful Bias and Homogenization, notes that a large language model may perform less well for non-English languages or certain dialects, and that such a system may be inappropriately trusted to perform similarly across all subgroups. Action MS-2.11-003 identifies affected groups through direct engagement with potentially impacted communities, which bears on testing by dialect and validating with the communities served.
  • Commentary Artificial Intelligence Model Risk Management: Observations from a Thematic Review, Information Paper, December 2024, from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, drawn from a review of banks. Paragraph 6.3.7 lists sub-population analysis among the robustness tests banks used: evaluating how AI models perform across subsets of the data, to find issues that might not be obvious in the aggregated testing dataset. This is a financial-sector example of reporting results separately for each language variety.
