Implementation 8.2.2: Identify Opaque Systems and Appropriate Safety Margins

Where an SI system’s failures cannot be traced afterward, safety margins and a planned hand-off help an organization reduce its exposure to failures it could not investigate. Identify opaque systems and the safety margins within which they will operate to acceptably minimize the likelihood of failures for which root-cause analysis cannot be performed. Such margins can take forms such as operating limits set back from equipment ratings or a lower level of autonomy, and can be checked against the most severe conditions recorded in operation. These margins may be relative to other systems, as per Implementation 8.2.1: Identify Applications and Situations for Which Root Cause Analysis Requirements May Be Relaxed. Beyond these safety margins, establish appropriate procedures for hand-off to manual oversight or to a system whose own failures can be traced, such as a verified-safe legacy system. That system should share as few of the SI system’s sources of error as practical, and those it does share, such as common sensors, should be identified. Where control passes to manual oversight, personnel who were not actively involved in the task can take longer to take over after a failure, so the margins and the hand-off procedure can allow for that time. Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation covers keeping those capabilities exercised.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What form do the safety margins take, and how much time does the hand-off to manual oversight allow?

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 4.1 asks operators to establish safe operating bounds for OT devices that detect AI drift and model changes affecting safety and performance, and to maintain a known good state or thresholds for safe behavior. Section 4.2 asks for failsafe mechanisms that let an SI system fail gracefully, including how to bypass or replace it. These correspond to the margins and the hand-off this Implementation defines.
  • Research The Out-of-the-Loop Performance Problem and Level of Control in Automation, by M. R. Endsley and E. O. Kiris, Human Factors 37(2), pages 381 to 394, 1995, from the journal Human Factors. Describes how operators of automated systems are handicapped in taking over manual operations when automation fails, and reports a study in which lower situation awareness went with slower decisions after an expert system failed. The level of operator control moderated that loss. It bears on the time that a hand-off to manual oversight in this Implementation can need.
