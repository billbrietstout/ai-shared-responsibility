Implementation 3.8.3: Dynamic Risk Surface Coupling Analysis

Because an SI system, its uses, and its operating environment can change after deployment, regular review helps an organization notice when the system’s risk surface has grown or shrunk. Conduct periodic evaluations of how changes in the operating environment, data distribution drift, increased agentic autonomy, or multi-agent chaining expand or contract the system’s risk surface over time. Include interactions between the attack surface and the non-adversarial risk surface. An adversary can steer inputs toward conditions in which the SI system is known to perform poorly. A non-adversarial change, such as a new operating mode, a replaced sensor or an updated model, can open or widen an attack path. Adding a tool, a permission or a data source to an agent can enlarge both surfaces at once. Have risk surface expansion trigger re-evaluation of independent guardrails, automatically where practical and otherwise through the reassessment triggers in Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers, (Task 3.2: Implement Independent Guardrails for SI Outputs) and of the oversight thresholds established under Implementation 4.5.1: Define Engineering Time-Constant Thresholds, since a widened risk surface can move a path across a threshold that was previously satisfied. Autonomy can also widen without a deliberate decision. For example, oversight can relax gradually after a run of successes, through bulk approvals or relaxed policies, so include changes in how oversight is exercised in practice among the changes evaluated.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often is the coupling analysis repeated, and how long does a containment claim stand without re-confirmation? (see the Safe versus Safe tension in Task 3.8: Scope and Map the Expanded SI Attack Surface and Risk Surface)

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-160v2r1, Developing Cyber-Resilient Systems: A Systems Security Engineering Approach, from NIST. Table 2 discusses, under the Adapt goal, changes in the technical environment, naming artificial intelligence among them, and in the operational environment, and states that analyses of such changes and of interactions between them can reveal how they modify the attack surface or introduce fragility, which is the coupling analysis this Implementation performs as the AI system’s environment and autonomy change.
  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, Version 2026, December 2025, from the Agentic Security Initiative of the OWASP Gen AI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project. ASI08, Cascading Failures, describes a single fault propagating across agents, tools and workflows. Its observable symptoms include rapid fan-out and feedback loops between agents. Among its examples is a governance drift cascade, in which human oversight weakens after repeated success and bulk approvals or policy relaxations propagate configuration drift across agents. These bear on multi-agent chaining and on the gradual widening of autonomy this Implementation evaluates.
