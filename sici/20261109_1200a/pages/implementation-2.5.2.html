Implementation 2.5.2: Multi-Vendor Testing

For high-consequence systems, the AI system’s validation should include testing in simulation environments, digital twins, and/or dedicated test-rigs. These should include equivalent parts from different manufacturers and at different levels of wear and tear that are representative of what may appear in the environments that the AI system would be deployed in over time. Variation between vendors can also appear in the data path, such as differences in data formats, scaling, timestamp handling, quality flags and protocol implementations, and these can be included among the variants tested. This is necessary to detect hidden, non-generalized model behavioral strategies (learned policies), such as a hidden reliance on capabilities that may be outside of the physical parts’ specifications. Under the conditions the AI system was trained in, an intended strategy and a hidden one can perform equally well, so testing only under those conditions may not tell them apart. Conditions that differ systematically from training, such as parts at a different point within their specification, can reveal the difference. For time-critical systems, testing should be performed on non-production environments as specified in Implementation 1.3.2: Categorization of Testing Environments.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which equivalent parts and vendors are included in testing, and which substitutions trigger re-testing?

References:

  • Research Shortcut Learning in Deep Neural Networks, by R. Geirhos, J.-H. Jacobsen, C. Michaelis, R. Zemel, W. Brendel, M. Bethge and F. A. Wichmann, 2020, preprint of an article published in Nature Machine Intelligence. Section 3 defines shortcuts as decision rules that perform well on test data like the training data but fail on out-of-distribution tests, and notes that tests on such data alone cannot tell an intended solution from a shortcut. Section 6.2 argues for out-of-distribution tests as standard practice, which bears on testing with parts and wear states unlike those met in training.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing, a commission initiated by the Special Competitive Studies Project. Its Finding 9 discussion of robotics data notes that assembly tasks are vulnerable to equipment wear over time, and that a simulation is useful only if it matches real equipment. It adds that physical testing remains necessary to validate performance and safety under actual operating conditions, which bears on including test-rigs with worn parts alongside simulation.
