Implementation 8.1.4: Conduct Incident Reconstruction Drills

Such drills should be conducted within digital twins or high fidelity simulators to help ensure that the resulting root cause analysis process and outputs are both accurate and accurately understood by responding personnel. A simulator that omits behavior that responders will meet in real operations can leave gaps in their preparation. Regular drills also detect shifts in the capabilities of underlying systems to enable root cause analysis, particularly where AI functionality is provided by external parties. Where feasible, include those external parties in the drills. Where they cannot take part, a member of the exercise staff can play their role. This is particularly important in applications where such analysis is required in real-time to inform ongoing, time-sensitive incident response activities. Record the findings of each drill in an after action report, and use its recommendations to update the reconstruction procedures and the playbooks in Implementation 8.1.3: Update Existing Incident Response Policies and Playbooks. Drills can work from the records that production systems actually retain, testing whether responders can identify the SI system version in use, reproduce or account for its output, and place it in the timeline alongside historian, alarm and other operational records.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which external parties take part in reconstruction drills, and who plays the role of those that cannot?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-84, Guide to Test, Training, and Exercise Programs for IT Plans and Capabilities, September 2006, from NIST. Section 2 describes functional exercises, in which personnel validate their operational readiness in a simulated operational environment. Section 5.4 has exercise staff play the roles of internal and external entities that are not participating, and Section 5.5 documents findings and recommendations for enhancing the exercised plan in an after action report. Written for IT plans in general, it gives a structure for the drills this Implementation describes.
  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). In Chapter 3, Training with High-Fidelity Simulations calls simulators the most effective way to build the skills and mental models for working with SI systems, and warns that a simulator that leaves out behavior operators will meet, such as under- or over-frequency relay action, could introduce gaps in training. Written about operator training, it applies equally to the simulators in which reconstruction drills run.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-61r3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management, April 2025, from NIST. Subcategory ID.IM-02 identifies improvements from security tests and exercises, including those done in coordination with suppliers and relevant third parties, and notes that exercises prepare involved third parties, such as critical service providers and product suppliers, for future incident response. That bears on drills where SI functionality is provided by external parties.
