Implementation 8.6.3: Ensure Supplier Terms Do Not Penalize Degradation Disclosure

Because a vendor may learn of problems with its model well before the organization does, supplier terms that encourage disclosure help the organization hear of those problems early. Review procurement terms and service-level agreements, as part of any supplier risk management already in place, to confirm they do not create financial or contractual disincentives for vendors to disclose model degradation, behavioral change, or known limitations. This complements the disclosure requirements in Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies and the hazard reachability attestations in Implementation 6.1.5: Operational SI Hazard Reachability Disclosures, both of which currently rely on vendor self-report. Terms that ask the vendor to share the results of its ongoing performance monitoring can give such disclosure a routine channel. Terms to check include service credits or penalties triggered by issues that the vendor itself reports, and confidentiality terms that would bar the organization from passing degradation information to a regulator or to a sharing community under Implementation 8.6.4: Share De-Identified Incident Records for Collective Learning.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which procurement and service terms are changed to remove disincentives to vendor disclosure?

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 2.3, Understanding the Role of OT Vendors in AI Integration, advises negotiating contractual agreements under which OT vendors give explicit details on SI features. It asks that, beyond typical vulnerability disclosure, the SI vendor disclose any indication it has found that the SI system can provide improper advice or take inappropriate action. That is the disclosure that supplier terms should not penalize.
  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). In Chapter 3, under Organizational Level, Vendor Transparency asks SI vendors to share bugs, challenges and biases with their customers, ideally in forums shared across customers or industries. It asks them to align their incentives internally, in how they reward identifying and reporting issues, and externally, in how they respond when that information spreads. Written to vendors, it is the supplier side of the terms this Implementation reviews.
  • Guidance SR Letter 11-7 Attachment, Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency. Section V, under Validation of Vendor and Other Third-Party Products, expects vendors to indicate a model’s limitations and where its use may be problematic, and to conduct ongoing performance monitoring and outcomes analysis with disclosure to their clients. Written by bank supervisors for banks, it is a sector example of the routine vendor disclosure these terms can support.
