Implementation 8.5.1: Identify Third-Party SI Forensic and Legal Experts for Contingency Access

Identify and pre-qualify external AI forensic specialists and legal counsel experienced in AI-related incidents before one occurs, since this market is still nascent and locating qualified help during an active incident wastes critical response time. Deciding which forensic tasks to keep in house and which to hand to outside specialists can take into account cost, response time, and the sensitivity of the data those specialists would see. Tasks that are needed rarely can be more cost-effective to obtain from an external party. For each identified party, record its capabilities, how to reach it and any relationship with the organization’s SI vendors that could bear on its independence, and confirm periodically that it remains available.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which forensic tasks are kept in house and which are handed to outside specialists?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.4.3 suggests engaging external organizations to perform forensic analysis where an organization lacks adequate resources or capability to conduct OT forensics, which is the gap this Implementation fills in advance for SI systems.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-86, Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Response, August 2006, from NIST. Section 2.2, Forensic Staffing, notes that many organizations combine their own staff with external parties, using outside parties when specialized assistance is needed and seeking external assistance when expert testimony is needed for legal proceedings. It lists cost, response time and data sensitivity as factors in deciding which parties handle each aspect of forensics, and notes that actions needed rarely might be more cost-effectively performed by an external party.
