Implementation 3.4.1: Define Observability Requirements

Observation in operation helps an organization see what an SI system actually does, especially where the system’s behavior is learned rather than specified. Identify observable AI system behaviors and metrics, including output patterns, decision frequencies, and validation control states, and, where the SI system depends on recurring data feeds or transformation pipelines, indicators of upstream data health, such as missing or late feeds, unexpected changes in record counts, schema or unit changes, or mapping failures. Include the inputs the SI system receives as well as its outputs, intermediate states and errors, the model and configuration versions in effect, and its use of resources and data. The frequency and repetition of inputs can also be worth observing, since high-frequency or repetitive inputs can be signs of automated attempts at compromise.

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Section 4.1 asks owners and operators to take inventory of AI components and of the components that rely on them, and to log and monitor the inputs and outputs of these components.
  • Guidance AI Incident Response Framework, Workstream 2: Preparing Defenders for a Changing Cybersecurity Landscape, version 1.0, 27 October 2025, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Section 3.3.1, Preparation Phase, lists the monitoring infrastructure for AI systems, including input and output logging, system prompt versioning, configuration management, API usage analysis, resource utilization and data access patterns, and recommends establishing API usage baselines. A fuller list of the observables this Implementation identifies.
  • Guidance Deploying AI Systems Securely: Best Practices for Deploying Secure and Resilient AI Systems, version 1.0, April 2024, joint Cybersecurity Information Sheet from the National Security Agency’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center, CISA, the FBI and partner agencies in Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the United Kingdom. Under actively monitoring model behavior, it asks for logs covering inputs, outputs, intermediate states and errors, and for monitoring of configuration settings for unauthorized changes. Under robust logging and monitoring, it advises watching for high-frequency or repetitive inputs as possible signs of compromise attempts.
  • Guidance AI Data Security: Best Practices for Securing Data Used to Train & Operate AI Systems, version 1.0, May 2025, joint Cybersecurity Information Sheet from the National Security Agency’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center, CISA, the FBI and partner agencies in Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom. Among the causes of data drift, it lists a change in the upstream data pipeline that the training data does not represent, such as units changing from miles to kilometers. The data-health indicators here are meant to catch this kind of change.
