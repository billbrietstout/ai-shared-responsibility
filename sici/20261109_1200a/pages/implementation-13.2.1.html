Implementation 13.2.1: Revoke Credentials and Disable Triggers, and Confirm the Result

Revoking at the issuer, and confirming the result where it is enforced, helps an organization know rather than assume that a retired system’s access is gone. Revoke the identities, credentials, tokens and keys held by the AI system, and disable the mechanisms that can invoke it, including scheduled tasks, event subscriptions, callbacks and registered interfaces. Revoke at the issuer rather than by deleting local copies, and rotate any shared secret the system knew. Where an issued token cannot be revoked before it expires, withdrawal is complete only once the latest such token has expired. In OT, the scope can include accounts on engineering workstations and historians, permissions to write setpoints, connections through jump hosts, and alarm or threshold triggers in supervisory tools, and their removal may need to be coordinated with site change management and outage windows. Confirm each revocation, not only the request, and retain the confirmation. Confirm it where the credential or trigger is enforced, rather than relying on the issuing system’s status, for example by a safe attempt to use it where feasible, and allow for propagation delay, cached authorizations and sessions that remain open. Where consequential actions are mediated through guarded proxies (Implementation 3.2.1 and Implementation 5.3.3), withdraw the retired system’s authority at those enforcement points as well, and confirm that queued or delegated requests relying on that authority can no longer execute. Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools addresses identity revocation as a capability, for example in Implementation 5.3.2: Task-Bounded and Revocable Agent Authority. What this Implementation adds is that retirement calls for revocation to be exhaustive and evidenced.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How is each revocation confirmed where it is enforced, including where a safe test use is not feasible?
  • How is removal of OT accounts, setpoint permissions and triggers coordinated with site change management and outage windows?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-88r2, Guidelines for Media Sanitization, from NIST. Its Appendix C provides a sample Certificate of Sanitization, a record naming what was acted on, by what method, by whom, and how the result was verified. It is the established documented form of the confirmed and retained result this Implementation asks for, and transfers directly to credentials and triggers.
  • Guidance Agentic Identity and Access Management, Workstream 4: Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems, from the Coalition for Secure AI. The Decommissioning subsection of Section 3.5 states the actions this Implementation calls for: revoke the identity and invalidate tokens, securely erase private keys, purge or appropriately handle sensitive data consistent with data-protection obligations, and revoke downstream delegated rights. The paper’s Appendix E supplies the corresponding record, an agent registry holding lifecycle state and an agent.decommissioned lifecycle event, which is where the confirmation is retained rather than in the teardown procedure itself.
