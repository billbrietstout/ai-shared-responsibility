Implementation 1.1.3: Establish Triggers for Re-Validating Requirements on Change of Operating Context

Re-validation triggers help an organization notice when the rules a system must meet have changed, even though the system itself has not. Define the events that invalidate the Requirements Summary produced in Implementation 1.1.1: Operational Baseline Review, and require re-validation when those events occur rather than only on a fixed review cycle.

For assets that relocate, that serve users or customers in more than one jurisdiction, or whose governing rule set is contract-dependent, the applicable standards, regulations, and customer-specific terms can change without any change to the SI system itself. Examples include:

  • a mobile or relocatable asset entering a different regulatory jurisdiction, where a different set of national, sectoral, or customer-specific requirements becomes applicable,
  • an asset that stays where it is but begins to serve another jurisdiction, such as a system hosted in one country being opened to customers in another,
  • a change in contract status such as going off-contract or being stacked, and
  • the layering of customer-specific procedural requirements on top of a regional baseline.

For each trigger, record who is responsible for detecting it, who decides on the response, what depth of re-validation it calls for, from a desk review to a full re-evaluation under Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle, and whether operation may continue in the meantime. Where the organization already runs a management of change process, these triggers can be added to it rather than kept separately.

Where the applicable rules are encoded in the SI system in its prompts, retrieval corpus or rule base, or in the guardrails and safety wrappers around it, treat the change as a required system update under Task 7.5: Conduct Impact Assessments and Risk Management for SI System and Model Updates. Where the rules are not encoded, but the SI system relies on standard operating procedures that reflect them, the trigger should raise an explicit action to review and, where necessary, rewrite those procedures before operation continues. Without such triggers, an SI system may continue to perform correctly against a rule set that no longer applies to the asset, producing outputs that are internally consistent and externally non-compliant. For SI systems built on pre-trained models, guidance, procedures, or regulations that changed after the model’s training cutoff are a further trigger. The model may continue to reproduce the superseded version unless current sources are supplied and take precedence. Where a trigger can be anticipated, such as a planned relocation, opening service to a new jurisdiction, or a change in contract status, re-validate before it takes effect, so that operation need not be suspended when it does.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which events trigger re-validation, who detects each, who decides the response, and what depth of re-validation does each call for?
  • Are the triggers added to an existing management of change process or kept separately?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-37r2, Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy, from NIST. Appendix F, Event-Driven Triggers and Significant Changes, lists changes to the environment of operation that may trigger an event-driven authorization action where they are likely to affect the security and privacy posture of the system. These include moving to a new facility, adding new core missions or business functions, and establishing new or modified laws, directives, policies or regulations. The first and last are changes in where a system operates or in the rules it must meet, rather than in the system itself, which is the category of trigger this Implementation defines. Its Type and Facility Authorizations subsection describes a single authorization for a common version of a system deployed to multiple locations, with the official responsible at each site accepting the risk of deploying it there. This separates acceptance of the system from acceptance of the setting it is placed in, the distinction this Implementation relies on when a relocatable asset enters a different setting.
