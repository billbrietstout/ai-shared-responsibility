Implementation 6.2.1: Register Externally Sourced SI Components in Asset Management Systems

A register of externally sourced SI, including SI embedded in other products, helps an organization know what SI it depends on and where. Add every externally sourced AI model, tool, or service to the organization’s existing asset management system at the point of acquisition, capturing vendor, version, and purpose so it is visible alongside traditional IT and OT assets rather than tracked separately or not at all. Record SI functions that are present in an acquired product but disabled, since a license change or vendor update can enable them without a procurement event. The record can also identify the asset or process each component serves, its owner, its announced end of support, and its AIBOM and hazard reachability disclosure under Implementation 6.1.2 and Implementation 6.1.5. Where the organization also keeps the list described in Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List, externally sourced components can be recorded in both, or the two reconciled, so that the organization does not hold two diverging accounts of its SI systems. Periodic reconciliation of the register against observed network traffic and supplier disclosures can reveal components that were never registered. Include SI capabilities embedded in OT devices and other products, as well as standalone SI models, tools and services. Record where each model is hosted and whether it needs connectivity to the vendor to function. For SI capabilities in OT, the high-priority fields already recommended for OT assets, such as asset criticality, manufacturer, model and the user accounts expected to access the asset, can be recorded for the SI component as well.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are externally sourced components recorded in the asset management system, in the list under Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List, or in both with reconciliation?
  • How often is the register reconciled against observed network traffic and supplier disclosures?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CM-8, System Component Inventory, maintains an inventory of system components at the granularity needed for tracking, including software version numbers and supplier information, and enhancement CM-8(1) updates it as part of component installations, removals and system updates, which is the existing control into which externally sourced AI models, tools and services are registered at the point of acquisition.
  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 2.3, Understanding the Role of OT Vendors in AI Integration, notes that some OT devices now come with built-in AI technology that may require internet connectivity to function. It asks operators to require vendors to clarify the supply chain of AI models in their products, such as where the models are hosted. This bears on what the asset record holds for embedded SI capabilities.
  • Guidance Foundations for OT Cybersecurity: Asset Inventory Guidance for Owners and Operators, 13 August 2025, from CISA, EPA, NSA and FBI with the cybersecurity agencies of Australia, Canada, Germany, the Netherlands and New Zealand. Step 2 lists the high-priority attributes to collect, among them asset criticality, manufacturer, model, communication protocols and user accounts. Appendix A, Table 1, gives the benefit of each, for example that asset criticality lets assets be managed by operational role, safety impact and exposure to risk. This bears on the fields recorded for an SI component in an OT asset inventory.
