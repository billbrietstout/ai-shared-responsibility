Task 9.3: Establish Audit-Ready Systems for Verifying Workforce Readiness

Create formal mechanisms to assess and document the effectiveness of AI risk training through structured assessments, tabletop simulations, and periodic drills. This Task contributes verifiable evidence of workforce readiness where regulators or auditors expect it, and helps ensure that personnel maintain the calibrated judgment necessary for high-stakes decision-making across the AI lifecycle. This evidence is one input to the assessment in Implementation 3.9.4: Assess Operational Capability of whether personnel can perform the roles assigned to them, made before deployment and again after material change.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Accountable: Contributes an auditable record of who has completed required AI risk training and their assessment results, so that an assignment of AI oversight responsibility can be checked against demonstrated competence and gaps in coverage found before an incident exposes them. This Task does not cover determining what competence each role requires, which Task 9.1: Perform Role-Based SI Risk Competency Mapping covers.
  • Safe: Contributes periodic hands-on readiness drills and certification renewal for the roles carrying the highest AI oversight responsibility, so that the readiness of the people an override depends on is demonstrated rather than inferred from one-time completion. This Task does not cover exercising the intervention procedures themselves, which Implementation 4.1.3: Regularly Practice and Evaluate Intervention Processes covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: Training records and readiness results (Implementation 9.3.1: Maintain Logs of Completed SI Training and Basic Proficiency Assessment Scores) show who can override which SI system, and where readiness is weak or out of date. An attacker could use them to target a role, shift, or person less likely to catch a manipulated output, or to choose whom to impersonate. Protecting them is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice, alongside the handling of training records as sensitive data in Implementation 9.3.1: Maintain Logs of Completed SI Training and Basic Proficiency Assessment Scores.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MAP 3.4 (on defining operator proficiency): This Task covers the assessment and documentation of operator proficiency through logged assessment scores, periodic readiness drills and certification renewal for critical roles; it does not cover defining the proficiency each role requires. For that, see for example Task 9.1: Perform Role-Based SI Risk Competency Mapping.
  • GOVERN 2.2 (on training for AI risk management): This Task covers evidence that personnel have received AI risk management training and remain able to perform their duties; it does not cover delivering that training. For that, see for example Task 9.2: Deliver Specialized Training on Human-SI Interaction and Automation Bias.

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-84, Guide to Test, Training, and Exercise Programs for IT Plans and Capabilities, September 2006, from NIST, sponsored by the Department of Homeland Security. Section 2 distinguishes tabletop exercises, which are discussion-based, from functional exercises, which let personnel validate their operational readiness in a simulated operational environment. It notes that exercises can identify cases where personnel need additional training or where training needs to change. Section 5.5 records findings in an after action report. Its subject is IT contingency and incident response plans. Its exercise forms bear on the tabletop simulations and drills this Task uses as evidence of readiness.
