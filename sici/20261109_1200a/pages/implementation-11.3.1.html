Implementation 11.3.1: Identify Standby Personnel and Physical Tools Required for Manual Operations

Name specific trained personnel and the physical tools or spare equipment needed to execute the manual fallback procedures from Task 11.2: Define Manual and Alternative Operating Procedures for Long-Term Mission Continuity, and confirm their availability on a defined schedule rather than assuming they will be reachable when an incident occurs. Count the additional staff that manual operation can need, for example to validate data by hand. Where possible, keep replacements for hard-to-obtain critical components in inventory. For each function, record the minimum number of qualified personnel needed on each shift and how soon they can be on site, so that the scheduled confirmation tests coverage rather than a list of names. Confirm from training records that those named have performed the manual task recently, using the training and testing in Implementation 3.6.1: Baseline Human Competency Retention. In operational technology settings, for example, physical tools can include local control panels, keys for locally operated valves and breakers, printed procedures and drawings, portable instruments, and radios that do not route through the SI system’s platform.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What minimum number of qualified personnel per shift, and what time to site, does each function need?
  • Where too few qualified people are available to cover every shift, what is done instead, and what risk remains?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement CP-2(8), Identify Critical Assets, identifies the critical assets supporting essential functions during contingency operations, and counts among them manually executed procedures and the personnel who execute them, which supports naming the standby personnel and tools needed for manual operation of AI-dependent processes.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.2.4.5 lists resourcing for additional operations support to manually validate data among OT response considerations. It asks that the plan indicate requirements for the timely replacement of components, with hard-to-obtain critical components kept in inventory where possible, and notes that recovery objectives may justify investment in spare inventory, which bears on sizing the standby personnel and spares named here.
