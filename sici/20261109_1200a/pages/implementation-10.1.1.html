Implementation 10.1.1: Identify SI Systems that May Take or Directly Influence Mission-Critical Actions

Knowing which SI systems can take or directly influence mission-critical actions lets an organization focus its most detailed logging where it matters most. Ensure that these systems are appropriately inventoried (Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List) and that logs of inputs, outputs, and other data to reconstruct their decisions are stored in accordance with data retention and regulatory requirements. An advisory SI system whose recommendations personnel routinely accept without independent checking should be treated as directly influencing mission-critical actions (see Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation). Revisit this identification when an SI system gains new tools, permissions or data connections, and record the reason where a system is judged not to influence mission-critical actions. The resulting list names the SI systems whose compromise would most affect mission-critical actions, which could help an attacker choose targets. Protect it under the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice, as for the asset list in Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List. Where this information is already logged (e.g., by other OT/ICS systems) ensure that appropriate metadata is also logged and retention policies synchronized, so that this information can be merged after the fact. Merging records after the fact depends on their timestamps. Synchronize the clocks of the SI system and of the systems whose logs will be merged with it, including OT and ICS systems, to a common authoritative time source, and record the time zone and timestamp format, since an inaccurate clock can make events appear in the wrong order. In OT, mechanisms such as GPS or IEEE 1588 can provide that common time source.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which advisory SI systems are treated as directly influencing mission-critical actions, and what reason is recorded where one is judged not to?
  • Which common time source are the clocks synchronized to?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control AU-11, Audit Record Retention, retains audit records for a period consistent with records retention policy to support after-the-fact investigations and meet regulatory requirements, which is the existing control under which AI decision logs would be retained. Synchronizing that retention with the OT and ICS logs they may need to be merged with is this Implementation’s addition.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, Release 5.2.0, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement SA-15(13), Logging Syntax, added in Release 5.2.0, has the organization require the developer of a system, component or service to log organization-defined event types in an organization-defined secure format at an organization-defined level of detail. Its discussion gives the purpose as supporting incident response and the faster reconstruction of security-related actions, by connecting application-produced audit event logs with operational data. For an acquired AI system, it offers one route by which the metadata needed to merge its logs with existing OT and ICS records can be required of the developer.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-92, Guide to Computer Security Log Management, September 2006, from NIST. Section 2.3, The Challenges in Log Management, describes inconsistent timestamps from inaccurate host clocks, with the example of logs showing one event 45 seconds before another that in fact happened two minutes after it. Section 5.5 has each system’s clock synchronized to a common time source. This bears on merging SI decision logs with the OT and ICS records they relate to.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix F.7.17, the OT discussion of SC-45, System Time Synchronization, has organizations coordinate time synchronization in OT for accurate troubleshooting and forensics, and names GPS and IEEE 1588 as suitable mechanisms. It adds that synchronization aligns audit logs from different systems, so that aggregated logs give an accurate view of events. This bears on merging SI logs with OT and ICS records after the fact.
