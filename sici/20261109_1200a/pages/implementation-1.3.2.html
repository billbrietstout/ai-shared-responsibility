Implementation 1.3.2: Categorization of Testing Environments

Choosing a suitable test environment helps the organization gather evidence about the system while limiting the risk to live operations. TEVV activities across the system lifecycle should be conducted within a validated environment tailored to the operational nature of the underlying infrastructure. For critical applications where service delivery, safety constraints, or physical control loops cannot tolerate production path disruption or performance overhead, adhere to the production path testing exclusions defined in Implementation 1.3.1: Define TEVV Scope and Requirements per SI System.

Testing, including active verification, automated input fuzzing, and adversarial red-teaming should be restricted to non-production systems such as staging mirrors, hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) test rigs, simulators, or high-fidelity digital twins. For critical applications that possess regular, planned downtime or can sustain temporary outages without safety impacts (e.g., administrative or transactional batch processors, data summarization tools, or off-peak fraud queues), it may be safe to test directly on the active production system strictly during authorized maintenance or scheduled outage windows. Where feasible, this may be preferable to testing on simulators or staging systems, whose behavior may unintentionally differ from the live system. This does not preclude advancing an SI system through these environments in stages, with criteria to be met at each stage before testing moves closer to the live system.

A digital twin or simulator supports assurance only to the extent that it has itself been validated against the process it represents. A simulation that has not been checked against measurements from the real equipment can repeat its own errors at scale. Where no validated twin is available, other options include hardware-in-the-loop rigs, replay of recorded operational data, phased commissioning, and passive shadow mode. In passive shadow mode, sometimes called a silent trial, the AI system runs on live inputs in parallel with normal operation, and its outputs do not affect that operation. Passive shadow mode does not show how operators respond to the AI system’s outputs, or how the process behaves once the AI system’s own actions change it.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which SI systems can be tested in an authorized window on the live system, and which only in staging, simulation or hardware-in-the-loop environments?
  • What criteria must be met at each stage before testing moves closer to the live system?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement CM-3(2), Testing, Validation, and Documentation of Changes, tests and validates changes before they are finalized, and its discussion asks that testing not interfere with operations, that testers understand the health, safety and environmental risks of the facility or process, that operational systems may need to be replicated or taken offline, with tests scheduled during planned outages whenever possible, and that compensating controls be used where testing cannot be conducted on operational systems. That parallels the split this Implementation draws between non-production environments and testing in scheduled outage windows.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing. Under Finding 9, the discussion of robotics data notes that, for contact-rich industrial tasks, a simulation is only useful if it matches real equipment, materials and friction, and that the only way to check that match is against measurements from real machines. It adds that a simulation without real-world grounding repeats its own mistakes at scale. This bears on validating a digital twin before relying on its results.
  • Research Tikhomirov, L. et al., “A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence”, Nature Health 1, 532-554, 2026. The introduction defines a silent trial as prospective, noninterventional testing of AI models in the intended setting, with outputs produced in parallel to the standard of care and without affecting it. Its main use is to find out whether a model keeps its performance in a live context. The review of 75 studies also found that few verified outputs against an in situ ground truth. This healthcare example bears on passive shadow mode as a testing option.
