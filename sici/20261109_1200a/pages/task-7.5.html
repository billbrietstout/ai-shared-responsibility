Task 7.5: Conduct Impact Assessments and Risk Management for SI System and Model Updates

Establish procedures for and perform structured risk and performance assessment before deploying any internal updates to AI systems, such as model prompts, configuration changes, data inputs, or safety wrappers. Where increases in risk are identified, if the deployment proceeds then mitigating controls should be identified and implemented. This Task helps check that “minor” internal adjustments do not unintentionally degrade overall system resilience or violate established functional safety benchmarks and requirements (see Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations). Degradation can come from unintended side effects of new behaviors, or from “catastrophic forgetting”, in which an AI system loses previously learned correct behavior after an update or retraining. This Task also applies where a change in operating context alters rules that are encoded in the AI system, such as in its prompts, retrieval corpus or rule base, or in the guardrails or safety wrappers around it, even though nothing in the AI system itself has changed (see Implementation 1.1.3: Establish Triggers for Re-Validating Requirements on Change of Operating Context).

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Reliable: Contributes a check, before any internal update to prompts, configuration, data or safety wrappers reaches production, that behavior which was already correct has not been lost, including through catastrophic forgetting, with shadow-mode comparison against the production system. This Task does not cover setting the benchmarks updates are judged against, which Practice 1 and Practice 2 set.
  • Robust: Contributes a change trigger for replacing a physical component or data feed with an equivalent from a different vendor, scaled to whether the component sits on a safety-critical path, because an AI system may depend on behavior beyond the specification the replacement meets. This Task does not cover verifying the AI system’s tolerance of such variance in the first place, which Task 2.5: Verify that Learned Behavior Is Robust to Component, Hardware, and Data Schema Variance covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: Impact assessments and validation results record where an update loses accuracy and which classes of input are routed to an earlier model. An attacker could use them to target the inputs the SI system handles least well. Protecting these records is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Accurate versus Accurate: Where the classes of input that lose accuracy under an update carry no more consequence than those that gain, an aggregate improvement can be judged on its own terms. Where a class that loses carries higher consequence, such as a type of equipment whose missed faults matter most, the aggregate gain conceals the loss that matters. Options include the fallback in Implementation 7.5.1: Perform Pre-Update Validation and Re-Calibration, which routes that class to the previous model where that version has been kept deployable. Others include re-calibrating thresholds, or declining the update.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MEASURE 2.3 (on ensuring realistic measurement): This Task covers measurement of a prospective update in shadow mode, alongside the production system on the same inputs, before switch-over; it does not cover defining the evaluation metrics used. For those, see for example Task 1.3: Implement Evaluation and Test Plans, Including TEVV, Across the SI Lifecycle.
  • MEASURE 2.10 (on measuring privacy): This Task covers a privacy impact assessment before initial deployment and at each significant model or system update, covering data minimization, re-identification and inference; it does not address privacy risk arising between updates, such as from changes in how the system is used.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How is an update judged when it improves accuracy overall but does worse on a class of inputs where errors matter more? (see the Accurate versus Accurate tension)
  • What happens to a needed update when its assessment cannot be completed in time, and who decides?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CM-4, Impact Analyses, analyzes changes to determine potential security and privacy impacts prior to implementation, and its discussion includes risk assessments to determine whether additional controls are required, which is the pre-deployment assessment and mitigation this Task sets out for model, prompt, data and safety-wrapper updates. Enhancement CM-4(2), Verification of Controls, verifies after a change that impacted controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended and producing the desired outcome.
