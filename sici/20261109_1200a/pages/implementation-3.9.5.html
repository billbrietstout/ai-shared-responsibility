Implementation 3.9.5: Demonstrate Operational Enforceability

Before deployment and following material changes that could affect established enforcement paths, demonstrate that a governance decision to restrict, suspend, roll back, or reduce the delegated authority of an SI-enabled capability can be translated into an actual change in the system’s operational state, not only recorded as policy or intent, and that the change is:

  • Effective: the decision produces the intended change in what the system is permitted to do or continues to do.
  • Timely relative to consequence: the change takes effect within a window that the organization defines and documents in advance, proportionate to the consequence class of the capability and the operational risk it presents, determined using the threshold criteria in Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses.
  • Verifiable: the organization can confirm the change actually took effect, rather than relying exclusively on the capability’s self-attestation that it complied. In distributed systems, this includes confirming that the change reached every relevant enforcement point, such as cached policy or configuration, asynchronous consumers, delegated or subordinate agents and tools, dependent automation, and work already in flight. A change that has propagated only partially has not taken effect.
  • Durable: once enforced, the restricted or reduced authority holds until a new authorized decision changes it. The capability cannot silently resume, re-expand, or reacquire that authority.
  • Sufficient in scope: enforceability covers the range of decisions the governance structure may need to make (restriction, suspension, rollback, and reduction of delegated authority), not a single all-or-nothing stop. Rollback is available only where a previous version has been kept deployable (Implementation 7.5.1: Perform Pre-Update Validation and Re-Calibration). Reverting can bring back defects the update fixed, and can make records written under the newer version harder to interpret.

For higher-consequence capabilities, consider demonstrating these properties by exercising a representative governance decision across the whole enforcement path, from recognition of the triggering condition through escalation, the authorized decision and its translation into technical action, to independent confirmation of the resulting operational state, rather than inferring them from separate tests of each component. Whether the demonstration is reviewed by a party outside the function that deploys the capability is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Outside review guards against the deploying function judging its own enforcement, as Implementation 8.6.1: Separate Benefit-Claiming from Risk-Reporting discusses, and it also costs time and depends on reviewers who may know the system less well. Where no outside party is available, record that and how the gap is compensated. Record who reviewed the demonstration.

The evidence needed to confirm that the change took effect and has held should come from the operating records required under Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities and, where the decision is an incident response, the incident records under Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures, rather than from a separate attestation process. This does not preclude compiling that evidence, with the other Task 3.9: Assess and Document Organizational Readiness for Each SI-Enabled Capability outputs, into a readiness record before deployment.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What window does each consequence class allow for a change to take effect?
  • Is the demonstration reviewed by a party outside the deploying function?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement CM-4(2), Impact Analyses | Verification of Controls, verifies after system changes that the impacted controls are implemented correctly, operating as intended and producing the desired outcome. This is the established form of the Verifiable property, applied here to a governance decision rather than to a code change.
  • Guidance DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 2: Human Performance Tools for Individuals, Work Teams, and Management, June 2009, from the U.S. Department of Energy. Under Checking and Verification Practices, Independent Verification has a person separated in time and distance from the performer confirm the condition of a component, without relying on the performer’s observation or oral confirmation. Its listed uses include restoring equipment to service and changes in equipment status that could affect the safety basis. This bears on confirming a change without relying on the capability’s own report.
  • Guidance Agentic Identity and Access Management, Workstream 4: Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems, version 1.0, 20 March 2026, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Section 3.4 asks that revoking a delegation at any point invalidate the downstream delegations, through centralized revocation services or event-driven propagation. Appendix D asks runtime components to subscribe to revocation events and stop accepting revoked tokens immediately, and to favor rapid revocation over grace periods for in-flight operations on high-risk delegations. These bear on propagation to subordinate agents and to work in flight under the Verifiable property.
