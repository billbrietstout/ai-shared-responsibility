Implementation 8.1.6: Incident Triage Across the Dual Attack/Risk Surface

Explicit triage pathways help incident response teams avoid treating an attack as drift, or drift as an attack. Incorporate explicit triage pathways in incident playbooks to systematically isolate whether an AI anomaly originated on the Attack Surface (e.g., unauthorized access, API key abuse, or malicious prompt injection) or within the non-adversarial part of the Risk Surface (e.g., sensor schema variance, distribution drift, or multi-agent cascade failure). This helps ensure forensic resources are directed toward the true root cause rather than misclassifying systemic algorithmic errors as external cyber intrusions, or vice versa. Before attributing a reported defect to the SI system, triage should also consider whether it arose upstream or downstream of it, for example in source data, ingestion, transformation or mapping, configuration or business rules, post-processing, or human interpretation of the output. Drift can also come from the supply chain, such as an unannounced update to a hosted model or a change in a data feed, and not only from changes in the inputs. Where the origin is unclear, triage can bring together cybersecurity, operations or safety, and the owners of the SI system, keeping both lines of inquiry open until the evidence rules one out.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which functions are brought together for triage when the origin of an anomaly is unclear?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-160v2r1, Developing Cyber-Resilient Systems: A Systems Security Engineering Approach, December 2021, from NIST. Section C.2 notes that an advanced persistent threat can take advantage of, or make its behavior appear to result from, other forms of adversity, including human error, structural failure or natural disaster. That is the case in which an intrusion is read as a non-adversarial failure, one of the two misclassifications this Implementation’s triage is meant to catch.
