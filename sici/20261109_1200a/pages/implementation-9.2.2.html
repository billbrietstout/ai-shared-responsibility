Implementation 9.2.2: Implement Realistic Incident Simulation Training

Realistic simulation lets personnel practice catching SI errors that are hidden, rare, or arrive under stress, at little risk to operations. Personnel who play a significant AI risk management role, such as those supervising or expected to take over from AI systems, should undertake such training. This may include the use of digital twins and realistic simulation environments, both to generate test scenarios and to determine the kinds of failures that are realistic. Where possible, introduce SI system errors without warning and at a rate closer to that expected in operation than in a test, and combine some of them with ordinary stresses such as an alarm flood, a shift handover or a concurrent equipment trip. Debriefs can review which cues were available and when the error was noticed, and detection rate, time to detect and correctness of response can be recorded across crews over time as evidence for Task 9.3: Establish Audit-Ready Systems for Verifying Workforce Readiness. Where simulators are not available, tabletop exercises with recorded or replayed SI system outputs can be a lower-cost starting point. Particular care should be taken in situations where the existence or nature of an AI system failure or error may be hidden or non-obvious, particularly due to other errors in related AI systems. For example, hallucinations in an AI-based summarization or dashboard system may result in early reports of failure from upstream systems being downplayed, omitted, or presented in a confusing or misleading manner, particularly where those reports are very unusual.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which roles undertake simulation training?
  • Are simulators, digital twins or tabletop exercises used, and are errors introduced without warning?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancements CP-3(1) and IR-2(1), Simulated Events, incorporate simulated events into contingency and incident response training so that personnel experience events such as hardware or software failures and understand their responsibilities in crisis situations, which is the training form this Implementation extends to AI failures whose existence may be hidden by errors in related systems.
  • Guidance Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Real-Time System Operations, White Paper Revision 1, November 2024, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). In Chapter 3, Training with High-Fidelity Simulations calls simulators the most effective way to build the skills, mental models and habits for working with AI systems, and warns that a simulator which leaves out behavior operators will meet in real operations can introduce gaps in training.
