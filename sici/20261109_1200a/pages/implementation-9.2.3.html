Implementation 9.2.3: Recognizing Disincentives to Reporting SI Issues

Training that names the pressures against reporting helps personnel recognize those pressures and know which reporting channels they can use. Include, in role-specific training for personnel who supervise, rely on, or can override AI systems, explicit coverage of conditions under which an individual may be discouraged from reporting observed AI degradation, near-misses, or anomalous outputs. Implementation 9.2.1: Include Role-Specific Training on SI Failure Modes addresses automation bias, in which personnel trust an AI output more than it warrants. This implementation addresses a related but distinct condition: personnel who correctly identify an AI failure but face organizational disincentives to report it. Examples include cases where the deployment is attributed as an efficiency gain to the reporting individual’s own business unit, or where reporting is likely to trigger scrutiny of a colleague’s or supervisor’s work. Another example is where individual or team performance metrics are tied to the AI system’s continued adoption, availability, or utilization rate. Training should help personnel recognize when they are in such a position, know which reporting channel is available to them and what protections attach to it, and understand that a near-miss involving no realized harm remains reportable. This is particularly relevant for degradation that accumulates gradually, where no single observation appears individually significant enough to justify the cost of reporting it. Training alone does not resolve this condition. Where an organization’s reporting structure makes disclosure individually costly, personnel who have been trained to report will still under-report. This implementation should therefore be paired with the structural measures in Task 8.6: Establish Non-Punitive Reporting for SI-Related Near-Misses, and organizations should treat persistent under-reporting as evidence of a structural rather than a training deficiency. Signs of suppressed reporting can include override and report rates that fall after deployment without a matching improvement in independent measures of performance, informal workarounds, and concerns raised only off the record.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which signs of suppressed reporting are watched, and who reviews them?

References:

  • Guidance NIST IR 8286r1, Integrating Cybersecurity and Enterprise Risk Management (ERM), from NIST. Section 3.6.1, Continuous Risk Monitoring, and its Table 3 list proactive activities that support a risk-aware culture. They include exercises in recognizing, reporting and responding to risk scenarios, and an environment in which employees may report potential risk situations without fear of reprisal. The first corresponds to the training this Implementation adds, and the second to the structural condition it says training alone cannot supply.
  • Guidance DOE-HDBK-1028-2009, Human Performance Improvement Handbook, Volume 1: Concepts and Principles, June 2009, from the U.S. Department of Energy. Chapter 4, Attachment C, Establishing a Reporting Culture, states that it cannot be assumed that workers will begin to report problems, errors and near misses once a just environment is in place. It names four barriers: reluctance to admit errors, suspicion that a report will count against the reporter later, doubt that management will act, and the effort of writing the report. It bears on the conditions this Implementation trains personnel to recognize and on the structural measures it pairs with training.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-50r1, Building a Cybersecurity and Privacy Learning Program, September 2024, from NIST. Section 3.1.1 lists using training to solve an issue that is not a knowledge or skills gap among the problems that can arise when the analysis phase is skipped, and states that analysis helps determine whether a gap is systematic or structural. It bears on this Implementation’s advice to treat persistent under-reporting as a structural rather than a training deficiency.
