Scenario B.6: Benefits fraud-risk model

Scenario. A self-learning SI model used to flag benefit fraud uses a protected characteristic as a risk factor. Flagged families have their benefits stopped and repayment demanded, and wrongful flags fall disproportionately on some groups.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Fair, Bias Managed

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Government Services and Facilities

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 1: Establish Requirements for Success: A self-learning SI risk-classification model is fielded to flag benefit applications for fraud, using a protected characteristic as one of its risk factors, with no pre-deployment fairness or impact assessment.
  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: Once the model flags an application, benefits are automatically discontinued and full repayment demanded, often with minimal further human assessment before the harm to the family occurs.
  • Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures: SI-driven fraud-risk classifications generate widespread erroneous benefit-enforcement actions, but the organization lacks procedures to detect systemic model-driven harm, reconstruct decision pathways, identify impacted populations, and coordinate corrective action before large-scale public harm occurs.
  • Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust: Officials over-trust SI-generated fraud-risk flags and treat them as determinative findings, causing families to be penalized without sufficient independent review, fact-checking, or calibrated understanding of model uncertainty.
