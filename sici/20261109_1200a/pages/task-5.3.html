Task 5.3: Implement Principles of Least Agency and Least Privilege for SI Entities

Holding an SI system to the access and actions its function needs helps limit how far a compromise or malfunction can spread. Least agency requires that the system’s capability to initiate independent actions or invoke external operational tools is minimized to only what is strictly necessary for its defined function. This is distinct from least privilege, which restricts what the system is permitted to access. Together they help keep systems from holding permanent access to control systems, configuration interfaces, or operational tools beyond the active operational need, and from using that access unnecessarily even where it exists. Minimize access permissions defined under Task 5.2: Determine and Implement Specific Access Requirements to the practical minimum for the AI entity’s defined function, since permissions pared so tightly that the system routinely fails can lead to broad, undocumented exceptions (Implementation 5.3.1: Determine the Practical Minimum Necessary Permissions for a Specific Task and Apply Them to the SI System). Least agency also applies to what an AI system carries from one step to the next: context, memory and retrieved material beyond what the current task requires add to what can be poisoned, leaked, or used to steer later actions. Even without an adversary, such material can distract and mislead the AI system (Implementation 5.3.4: Limit the Context Carried Forward Between Steps). Least agency also covers actions that would extend the SI system’s own reach, such as creating identities, starting further agents, installing tools, or changing its own configuration or permissions. These should be unavailable to it unless the capability has been explicitly reviewed and granted. Least agency also applies to the range of values an SI system may produce: where it writes into a structured field that other systems read, confining it to the set of values those systems were designed for, rather than allowing it to create new ones, helps keep its output within what they can handle.

Controls based on those applied to human users are a suitable point to build from, with additional controls put in place to account for AI agents and systems that may:

  • Have limited needs and whose access should be further compartmentalized.
  • Be influenced by multiple people beyond those notionally responsible.
  • Interact with other AI agents and systems, resulting in potential emergent behavior.
  • Operate at speeds beyond those expected of humans.
  • Be unable to provide meaningful explanations for their actions.

Where authority is withdrawn permanently rather than bounded, Implementation 13.2.2: Revoke Authority Delegated to Other Entities, and Record What Could Not Be Confirmed suggests that the withdrawal reaches every level of delegation and that anything which cannot be confirmed revoked is recorded.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Safe: Contributes a structural limit on what an AI system can set in motion: the capability to invoke operational tools is held to the defined function, and high-consequence actions are reached through a deterministic intermediary holding the execution credentials. This Task does not cover the validation logic and parameter collars that intermediary applies, which Task 3.2: Implement Independent Guardrails for SI Outputs covers.
  • Secure: Contributes the removal of standing authority: permissions bounded to a task, mode or time window, with expiration and rapid revocation, so a compromised or malfunctioning entity holds less at any moment than permanent access would give it. This Task does not cover establishing the identity whose authority is being bounded, which Task 5.1: Establish Identities and Authentication for SI Entities covers.
  • Privacy-Enhanced: Contributes a limit on the information an AI system carries from step to step and passes to the agents and tools it calls, so less sensitive material is exposed to leakage through later outputs. This Task does not cover determining what data may leave the organization for external AI services, which Task 6.3: Identify and Control Dataflows Across the SI Supply Chain identifies and controls.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Accountable: Where a complex AI system reaches an asset through an encapsulated downstream agent, the credential presented at the asset belongs to that intermediary rather than to the system that asked for the action. Tracing the action back to the requesting entity then depends on that entity’s own identity under Task 5.1: Establish Identities and Authentication for SI Entities appearing in the record, and on the access event logging in Task 5.4: Implement Continuous Monitoring of SI Entities Against Access and Action Boundaries and Expectations.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Resilient versus Secure and Safe: Where the authority service is reachable, task-bounded and revocable authority and continued operation move together. Where a site is partitioned, keeping an entity working means letting it act on authority that nobody present can withdraw, whether the reason to withdraw it is compromise or malfunction. One way to balance this, which Implementation 5.3.2: Task-Bounded and Revocable Agent Authority takes, is to revert to pre-engineered local fallback behavior rather than maintain unverified standing authority. Others include siting an authority service where the partition leaves it reachable, or narrowing in advance what an entity may do while partitioned.
  • Accurate versus Secure, Privacy-Enhanced and Accurate itself: Where a task’s information needs are known and stable, passing forward only what it needs costs little in performance, and removing distracting material can improve it. Where what a later step will need cannot be predicted, or depends on what happened earlier in a long interaction, trimming context can remove information the AI system needed and degrade its decisions. One way to balance this is to define, for each step or delegated agent, the context it receives, and to test the AI system with that context rather than with everything available (Implementation 5.3.4: Limit the Context Carried Forward Between Steps). Others include letting a step request specific further context through a mediated and logged interface, or restarting long interactions at defined points from a summary that is itself checked.
  • Safe versus Safe: Where an SI system’s function is advisory or can wait for a human decision, bounding its authority costs little in safety. Where it performs a protective function against a fast-moving hazard, such as isolating a leak or shedding load, narrowing what it may do without approval can slow the protective action. One way to balance this is to set its standing authority for that function against how fast the hazard develops relative to a human response, and against the independent protection that already covers it. Others include authorizing in advance a narrowly defined protective action for a declared condition, or relying on an independent protection layer rather than on the SI system for the fastest hazards.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MANAGE 2.4 (on decommissioning or superseding AI systems): This Task makes authority expire by default, bounding it to a task, mode or time window so that standing privilege lapses without anyone intervening; it does not cover establishing the identities that make selective revocation possible. For that, see for example Task 5.1: Establish Identities and Authentication for SI Entities.
  • MANAGE 2.1 (on allocating resources): This Task substitutes a deterministic, single-purpose proxy tool for the probabilistic agent at the point where a high-consequence action is executed, which is a non-AI alternative applied narrowly; it does not cover considering replacing the AI function as a whole.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are any capabilities that extend an SI system’s own reach granted, and after what review?
  • What may an entity do while a partition leaves no one able to withdraw its authority? (see the Resilient versus Secure and Safe tension)
  • How much standing authority does an SI system performing a protective function hold for fast-moving hazards? (see the Safe versus Safe tension in Task 5.3: Implement Principles of Least Agency and Least Privilege for SI Entities)

References:

  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, Version 2026, published December 2025 by the Agentic Security Initiative of the OWASP Gen AI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project rather than a standards body or a public authority. Entry ASI02, Tool Misuse and Exploitation, carries this Task’s first principle under its own name: the leading mitigation is titled Least Agency and Least Privilege for Tools, and asks for per-tool profiles setting scopes, maximum rate and egress allowlists, with read-only database queries and summarizers holding no send or delete rights as worked examples. Entry ASI03, Identity and Privilege Abuse, carries the second: un-scoped privilege inheritance down a delegation chain, and authorization that was valid when a workflow began but not when it executed, answered by task-scoped and time-bound permissions with automated revocation. The document holds the two apart as this Task does, with ASI02 covering an agent that misapplies a tool inside the privileges it already holds and ASI03 covering an agent that acquires privileges it should not have.
