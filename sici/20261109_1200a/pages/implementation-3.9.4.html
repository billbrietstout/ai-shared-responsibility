Implementation 3.9.4: Assess Operational Capability

Assess whether existing organizational functions collectively provide sufficient capability to execute the governance requirements applicable to the SI-enabled capability. Consider whether accountability, decision authority, escalation, lifecycle responsibilities, monitoring, and control execution operate together sufficiently for the intended use and operating context. Where these conditions are met, existing governance structures may be extended to cover the SI-enabled capability. Where authority, accountability, lifecycle coverage, or operational capability is fragmented or absent, establish additional governance mechanisms as necessary. Where the assessment turns on whether a governance decision could actually be carried out, use the five properties in Implementation 3.9.5: Demonstrate Operational Enforceability as the test, rather than a general judgment of sufficiency. Where the assessment turns on whether personnel can actually perform the roles assigned to them, apply the role-specific training and competency requirements in Implementation 9.2.1: Include Role-Specific Training on SI Failure Modes and the simulation training in Implementation 9.2.2: Implement Realistic Incident Simulation Training. Organizational capability includes the people who exercise it. Repeat the assessment whenever a reassessment trigger in Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers activates, including any increase in the authority delegated to the capability. Include in the assessment whether the organization can sustain the affected functions if the capability is lost, drawing on the procedures in Task 11.2: Define Manual and Alternative Operating Procedures for Long-Term Mission Continuity and the drills in Implementation 11.5.2: Execute High-Fidelity Simulation Drills Involving Total SI Infrastructure Loss and Recovery.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where the assessment finds a function fragmented or absent, who provides the people, time, and budget to close the gap, and does the capability wait until it is closed?

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 3.2, Integrating AI Into Existing Security and Cybersecurity Frameworks, asks operators to embed AI system assessments within existing risk evaluation, mitigation and monitoring processes. Section 3.1 lists the stakeholders an AI governance structure should include, among them leadership, OT and IT subject matter experts and cybersecurity teams, and asks for clear roles and responsibilities. These bear on deciding whether existing functions can be extended to cover the capability.
  • Guidance Deploying AI Systems Securely: Best Practices for Deploying Secure and Resilient AI Systems, Joint Cybersecurity Information, version 1.0, April 2024, from the National Security Agency’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center with CISA, the FBI and four other national cybersecurity agencies. Under Secure the deployment environment, it suggests that the person accountable for AI system cybersecurity be the same person accountable for the organization’s cybersecurity in general. Where a function outside IT deploys the AI system, it asks that function to confirm with IT that the environment meets IT standards. These bear on extending existing functions to cover the capability.
