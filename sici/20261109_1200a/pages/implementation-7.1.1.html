Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List

A master asset list helps the organization see in one place what SI systems it runs, what each one can affect, and what each one depends on. Document the names, versions, vendors, hosts (cloud vs on-premises), responsible contacts, dependencies (such as required authorizations, data, compute, and other resources), operational purpose, and risk management controls for all SI systems and models. Record also the processes or physical assets each SI system can affect, the network zone it sits in and what it can reach, whether it only advises or can act on the process, its oversight mode (human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop or human-out-of-the-loop, as Task 4.5: Define Operational Regimes for Human-on-the-Loop and Human-Out-of-the-Loop Operation defines them), the date of its last validation, and its status, such as pilot, production, suspended or retired. Keep entries for retired SI systems rather than deleting them, so that what was running at a given date can later be established. This includes AI systems used for logistical or administrative purposes, such as chatbots, meeting transcribers, and document summarization tools. Document model lineage and component hierarchy, tracking parent-child relationships between base foundation models, fine-tuned adapters (e.g., LoRAs), vector index embeddings, and quantized runtime distributions. Record each derivation, such as fine-tuning, quantization, format conversion, pruning or the addition of adapters, as a link in this lineage. Where known, record the training data cutoff of each model, so that a reviewer can tell what an output may have drawn from training rather than from current sources, such as a procedure revised or equipment replaced since. For SI systems that support essential functions, also record how long the mission is expected to depend on the system, alongside the periods covered by vendor support, contract, license and data rights, revalidation, and committed resources, and the earliest date at which any of these ends before that dependence does, with an accountable owner. Where a period is unknown or uncommitted, record it as an unresolved gap rather than as an absence of risk. Because the list records network zones, reach, oversight modes, and unresolved gaps, it shows an attacker which SI systems can act on a process and where coverage is weakest. Protecting it is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice (see also the Secure entry under Task 7.1: Maintain an Internal Registry of SI Deployments). Retirement triggers that follow from these dates are identified under Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • For which SI systems are the dependence horizon and coverage periods recorded, and who owns each gap?

References:

  • Guidance NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Suggested actions under GOVERN 1.6 ask that generative AI systems be enumerated into the AI system inventory (GV-1.6-001) and list items to consider in each entry (GV-1.6-003): data provenance information, known issues from bug tracking or incident databases, human oversight roles, special rights and sensitive data considerations, and the underlying foundation models, their versions and access modes. These correspond to several of the fields this Implementation asks the list to hold; the underlying foundation model and version are the first link of the model lineage it also asks for.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-218A, Secure Software Development Practices for Generative AI and Dual-Use Foundation Models: An SSDF Community Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Task PS.3.1 note N1 states that AI models and their components may need to be added to an organization’s asset inventories at release, and task PS.3.2 recommendation R1 asks for the provenance of an AI model and its components and derivatives to be tracked, including the libraries, frameworks and pipelines used to build it. Section 2 notes that generative and dual-use foundation models present additional challenges in tracking versioning and lineage, which bears on the parent-child lineage this Implementation asks the list to record.
  • Guidance Authoritative Guide to AI/ML-BOM: Drive Transparency, Compliance, and Security Across the AI Supply Chain, First Edition (Revision 1), 10 June 2026, from the CycloneDX AI/ML Working Group of the OWASP Foundation. The section Declaring a model’s pedigree lists fine-tuning, quantization, format conversion, pruning and adapters as ways models are derived from pre-trained models, and asks that these transformations be captured in the model’s lineage as a hierarchical graph of its ancestors. This is a machine-readable form for the parent-child lineage this Implementation asks the list to record.
  • Guidance Implementing AI Bill of Materials (AI BOM) with SPDX 3.0: A Comprehensive Guide to Creating AI and Dataset Bill of Materials, October 2024, by K. Bennet, G. K. Rajbahadur, A. Suriyawongkul and K. Stewart, from Linux Foundation Research. The guide explains that SPDX 3.0 separates its AI and Dataset Profiles to clarify the provenance and training methods used in creating an ML model, and its comparison with IBM FactSheets (Table 3) maps lineage questions, such as how the models were trained and which datasets they were trained on, to the informationAboutTraining field and trainedOn relationships. This is the SPDX counterpart of the CycloneDX lineage approach above.
  • Guidance SR 11-7, Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, written for banks. The Model Inventory section asks for information on models in use, under development or recently retired, with any variation that warrants separate validation entered as a separate model and cross-referenced. It also lists the time frame during which each model is expected to remain valid. This bears on the lineage entries and on the dependence and revalidation periods this Implementation asks the list to hold.
