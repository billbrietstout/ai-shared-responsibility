Implementation 11.1.1: Rank Operational Processes Based on the Impact of SI Failure or Unavailability

A documented ranking gives the organization a basis for deciding, in the moment, which system gets restored first. Score each AI-dependent process by the severity of consequence if the AI component became unavailable, using the consequence criteria in Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses, from life-safety and service-continuity impacts down to minor administrative inconvenience, and use this ranking to direct recovery resources during an actual outage. Where recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO) are already set for the business processes concerned, they can inform this ranking and how much redundancy to provide for each process. In SP 800-53’s CP-2, the contingency plan for the system provides restoration priorities. Defined personnel or roles review and approve that contingency plan, and the plan is reviewed at a defined frequency. Where the organization keeps such a contingency plan, record this ranking in it, so that the ranking is approved and reviewed with the plan.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • At which outage durations is each process ranked, and do existing RTO and RPO targets inform the ranking?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-34r1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, May 2010, from NIST. Section 3.2.1 of the business impact analysis expresses process impacts on a scale that indicates impact severity if the process could not be performed, and defines maximum tolerable downtime, the recovery time objective (RTO) and the recovery point objective (RPO). Section 3.2.3 combines process criticality, outage impacts, tolerable downtime and system resources into a recovery priority hierarchy, which bears on the ranking this Implementation produces for SI-dependent processes.
