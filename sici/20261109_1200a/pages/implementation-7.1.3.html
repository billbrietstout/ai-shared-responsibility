Implementation 7.1.3: Integrate Automated Internal Software and SI Bill of Material Management

Scanning and machine-readable bills of materials help the organization keep its inventory matched to what is actually running, and find SI that was never registered. Develop policies for regular scanning of internal networks for AI systems and behavior, and maintain AIBOMs for internally developed or modified AI systems in the same form as those requested of vendors under Implementation 6.1.2: Require SIBOM for Mission-Critical Applications. This integrates with information obtained in Practice 6: Integrate Visibility of the External Supply Chain of SI into Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management. Some components, such as sensors and other IoT devices, cannot support a software agent, so scanning that depends on agents can miss them. In OT environments, a logical survey can be combined with physical inspection and with what documentation records. Periodically compare what scanning and detection find with the entries under Implementation 7.1.1: an observed SI system with no entry, such as a model downloaded and run without going through procurement, is a candidate for Task 3.7: Govern and Mitigate Risks from Unauthorized, Transient, and “Shadow SI” Systems, and an entry with no corresponding observation may indicate a retirement that was not recorded or a gap in scanning coverage. Track both kinds of discrepancy to closure. Generating AIBOMs in the build and deployment pipeline, rather than by hand, can help keep them current with each change to model weights, adapters, datasets, libraries, prompts and guardrails. Each AIBOM can be linked to the corresponding entry in the Master AI Asset List. AIBOMs and scanning results list components and their versions. An attacker could match these against known vulnerabilities to choose a way in. Protecting them is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice, as with software bills of materials.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often are scanning results reconciled with the asset list, and how is physical inspection combined with scanning in OT?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement CM-8(3), Automated Unauthorized Component Detection, detects unauthorized hardware, software and firmware components using automated mechanisms at a defined frequency. Its discussion notes that monitoring may be ongoing or by periodic scanning, and that some components, such as IoT devices and sensors, cannot support the agents that some detection mechanisms depend on. This is the established control form of the scanning this Implementation asks for.
  • Guidance Foundations for OT Cybersecurity: Asset Inventory Guidance for Owners and Operators, 13 August 2025, from CISA with the EPA, NSA, FBI and partner agencies in Australia, Canada, Germany, the Netherlands and New Zealand. The section Steps to Develop an OT Asset Inventory and Taxonomy asks that assets be identified through physical inspection and a logical survey, and that the inventory’s scope consider assets identified in documentation and physical inspection. This bears on scanning for SI components embedded in OT, where network discovery alone can miss assets.
  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications, Version 2026, from the OWASP GenAI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project. LLM04:2026, Supply Chain, mitigation 4 asks for an up-to-date, signed inventory of components using an SBOM extended to models, adapters and datasets with AIBOMs and ML SBOMs, with licenses tracked in the same inventory and audited regularly. This bears on the AIBOMs this Implementation asks to be maintained for internally developed or modified SI systems.
