Implementation 3.6.3: Cognitive Engagement by Design

Interface design that asks for active judgment at the right moments helps operators keep thinking for themselves rather than approving SI output by habit. Design the human-system or human-machine interface (HSI or HMI), and any other point at which a person reviews or approves SI output, including human-in-the-loop approval steps in agentic workflows that have no dedicated HMI, to discourage passive monitoring. Consider active human verification step-throughs or plausibility checks for truly high-consequence AI recommendations to counter the psychological tendency of operators to automatically trust machine outputs (automation bias). To balance active human validation against cognitive exhaustion, these checkpoints can be reserved for high-consequence operational boundary thresholds. They should be deployed flexibly, utilizing the mixed-initiative and calm technology design choices referenced in Implementation 4.4.4: Applying Consolidation and Low-Cognitive-Load Design Techniques to limit how far alert fatigue drives superficial operator compliance. Implementation 3.6.4: Monitor Aggregate Engagement Indicators covers measuring whether these design choices keep personnel engaged.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which review and approval points get active verification steps? (see the Safe versus Safe tension in Task 3.6: Mitigate Automation Complacency and Skills Degradation)

References:

  • Research Endsley, M. R. and Kiris, E. O., The Out-of-the-Loop Performance Problem and Level of Control in Automation, Human Factors volume 37 number 2, 1995, pages 381 to 394, from the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society. In an experiment, people who had worked with an automated decision aid were slower to decide after it failed. The authors attribute the loss of situation awareness mainly to a shift from active to passive processing of information. They report that the level of operator control is a major factor in moderating that loss. This bears on designing the interface against passive monitoring.
  • Guidance OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, Version 2026, December 2025, from the Agentic Security Initiative of the OWASP Gen AI Security Project, a volunteer community project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project. ASI09, Human-Agent Trust Exploitation, describes people approving agent actions without independent validation, with automation bias making abuse look legitimate. Its Adaptive Trust Calibration mitigation adjusts the required human oversight to contextual risk and uses confidence-weighted cues that prompt users to question high-impact actions. This bears on extending this Implementation to approval steps in agentic workflows.
