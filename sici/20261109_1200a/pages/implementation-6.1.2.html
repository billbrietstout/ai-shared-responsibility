Implementation 6.1.2: Require SIBOM for Mission-Critical Applications

SIBOM should be at an appropriate level of detail, from vendors for the current and each subsequent model release so that their AI system’s supply chain is documented. Ideally this should be machine readable. It should include model(s) in use, primary training data sources, data retrieved at runtime, and software used at training and run time including any significant third-party dependencies. Where the vendor controls them, the system prompts, guardrail settings and decision thresholds that shape the SI system’s behavior can be included too, and for a fine-tuned model, the foundation model it derives from and that model’s provider.

Purposes for such an SIBOM include:

  • Monitoring for application and/or environment drifting outside the support of the training data and background information.
  • Detecting the presence of input data and information that becomes or is later found to be inaccurate or invalid.
  • Tracing errors, vulnerabilities, and threats.
  • Checking a model release or agent configuration before it is promoted to production, by confirming that its components match the SIBOM received and that each has been reviewed, so that the SIBOM acts as an admission check rather than only as a record.
  • Recording which components are safety-relevant in this deployment. Bill of materials formats may not carry this, and a component with a low vulnerability score can still sit on a safety path, so the operator may need to add that classification itself, using the consequence criteria in Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses.

The concept of SIBOM/AIBOM is still in its infancy and as of writing, formal specifications are still being developed (see CISA 2026 SBOM Minimum Elements). Operators should consider their application’s risk profile in deciding on the level of detail that they should request of their vendors. For example, a vendor providing an AI system that relies on environmental sensor data as part of its mission-critical decision making should, at a minimum, be obliged to attest that the AI system’s training data includes an appropriate representation from similar environments. Procurement terms can also state performance requirements that apply to every release rather than to a named model version, so that a vendor update that replaces the model remains bound by them.

If a vendor is unable to provide an appropriate SIBOM for a high-consequence application, the operator faces a hidden residual risk profile. Whether an incomplete SIBOM is a bar to acquisition or a gap to be compensated, for each application, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. A hard requirement keeps the hidden residual risk out. While few vendors can meet it, though, it may narrow the field or delay replacement of older systems. Accepting partial documentation keeps options open but relies on compensating controls proportional to the gaps. Options include enhanced runtime monitoring as described in Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior, guardrails as described in Task 3.2: Implement Independent Guardrails for SI Outputs, watchdogs as described in Implementation 2.2.2: Deploy “Watchdog” Controllers, and more frequent recurrent risk and TEVV evaluations as described in Implementation 2.3.4: Recurrent Risk Evaluations.

Any gaps in meaningful supply chain documentation that cannot be compensated for should themselves be treated as a documented risk, escalated to a role with authority to accept it (see Implementation 3.9.3: Establish Escalation to Empowered Authority) and factored into the organization’s go/no-go deployment decision.

To the Reviewer: We’ve framed this above as a gradient of compensating controls rather than a hard requirement, since we recognize AIBOM standards are still emerging and few vendors can fully meet this today. Does this framing strike the right balance for your procurement process, or would a different structure (e.g., minimum mandatory disclosures vs. best-effort extras) work better for your sector?

Decisions for the Organization:

  • For each application, is an incomplete SIBOM a bar to acquisition or a gap to be compensated, and with which compensating controls?
  • Which components count as safety-relevant in this deployment?
  • Which role accepts documentation gaps that cannot be compensated?

References:

  • Guidance 2026 Minimum Elements for a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), published 29 July 2026 by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Sets out what a software bill of materials has to carry and is deliberately format-neutral, asking organizations to accept any widely used, interoperable and machine-processable format, and naming SPDX (ISO/IEC 5962:2021) and CycloneDX (ECMA-424) as the two in wide use. The section titled SBOM for AI Software Systems is the part that bears on this Implementation: it holds that because AI is software the minimum elements already illuminate many of its components, that there may be additional useful supply chain data for AI, for example what practitioners discuss as model cards and data cards, and that the document does not itself introduce elements for AI systems.
  • Standard NERC CIP-013-2, Supply Chain Risk Management for High and Medium Impact BES Cyber Systems, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation, the body that develops reliability standards for the North American bulk power system and enforces them on registered entities. The requirements at R1 and R2 apply to supply chain risk management for the high and medium impact BES Cyber Systems the title names. Energy sector only.
  • Standard System Package Data Exchange (SPDX), a project of the Linux Foundation, published as ISO/IEC 5962:2021. A bill of materials format for software components. Its coverage of AI-specific components is limited in the ratified version, and AI content is being developed for a later release of the specification.
  • Standard CycloneDX, a flagship project of the Open Worldwide Application Security Project, published as ECMA-424, CycloneDX Bill of Materials Specification, December 2025. A bill of materials format covering software, hardware and machine-learning components, with a machine learning bill of materials form already defined, and AI content under continued development.
  • Guidance Establish Risks and Controls for the AI Supply Chain, version 1.0, 12 June 2025, from Workstream 1, Software Supply Chain Security for AI Systems, of the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project whose other workstreams address adjacent parts of this profile’s subject. Its Supply Chain Documentation Requirements section enumerates the runtime half of the inventory this Implementation asks for: retrieval-augmented generation infrastructure including embedding models and retrieval algorithms, vector database providers and their isolation mechanisms, caching systems with isolation and encryption, the tools and external systems reachable during inference, and dependency trees with their integrity verification methods.
