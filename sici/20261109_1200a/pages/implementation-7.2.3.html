Implementation 7.2.3: Assess the Conditions Under Which Source Data Is Created

Understanding how source data is created helps the organization find records that were lost or distorted before they reached a repository. Extend provenance validation upstream of ingestion to the point of capture. Data quality certification performed under Implementation 7.2.1: Implement Data Quality Monitoring can only assess data that successfully arrived; it cannot reveal records that were degraded, overwritten, or never transferred during collection. Document the field conditions, tooling, and manual steps involved in creating source data, and identify where those conditions systematically introduce loss or inconsistency. Much of this data was collected for operations rather than for SI systems. For process data, the conditions include historian settings, such as compression, deadband, interpolation and sampling rate, and the substitution of values when an instrument reports bad quality. Each can make the stored record differ from the process it describes. For event and maintenance records, they include who entered each record, the coding scheme in use and its changes over time, and reporting practices, such as work orders closed with a generic code. Recording changes such as sensor recalibrations, deadband changes and tag renames can help trace them to the SI systems and training periods they affect. Potential failure modes include the following. In a multi-stage transfer chain, field personnel move data from local device storage to intermediate media and then to a central or cloud repository, and an incomplete stage can silently drop records. Stored data can be overwritten inadvertently during later collection cycles. Migration between maintenance management systems (for example, CMMS platforms), or limitations within them, can introduce loss or inconsistency. Where a manual step cannot be removed, consider monitoring how completely it is performed, for example the proportion of expected records that reach the central repository. Findings about where records are lost or substituted also show where altered or missing data is least likely to be noticed. Protecting those findings is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice. Where the capture environment is intermittently connected or resource-constrained, coordinate with Implementation 10.1.4: Asynchronous Telemetry and Inference Logging for Constrained Edges. Findings should be recorded against the relevant entries in the data inventory maintained under Implementation 7.1.2: Maintain a Data Inventory, and should inform training delivered under Implementation 9.1.3: Include Data-Originating Roles in Competency Mapping.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where a manual capture step cannot be removed, is its completeness monitored, and how?
  • Whose time is used to document capture conditions, given that much of it falls to field and maintenance staff outside the SI project?

References:

  • Research Kang, J. et al., “A data quality management framework for equipment failure risk estimation: Application to the oil and gas industry”, Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence 136 (2024) 108834. A framework for deciding whether field data is of sufficient quality for an equipment failure risk estimation model, with a case study of electronic boards in downhole drilling tools. Section 3.5 briefly identifies root causes of poor field data, including manual transfer steps that field personnel skip, inadvertent overwriting, and loss or inconsistency from maintenance management system migrations, and suggests training and data collection KPIs as remedies.
