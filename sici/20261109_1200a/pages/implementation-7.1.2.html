Implementation 7.1.2: Maintain a Data Inventory

A data inventory helps the organization trace a problem with a data source to every SI system that depends on it, and use each source only as its terms allow. Document organizational data sources that are, or may be, used in AI systems. Use may include training, fine-tuning (further training an existing AI model on an organization’s own data to adapt it), retrieval augmentation, or agentic use. Data sources may be static knowledge bases, historic, and/or real-time feeds, or memory and context stores that are shared across SI systems or models and updated as they run. The inventory should identify the data’s:

  • Source and provenance, including its origin and chain of custody. See Task 7.2: Validate, Select, and Prepare Data for SI Systems.
  • Role within the AI system, such as for training or retrieval augmentation, and the SI systems listed under Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List that use it, so that a problem with the data can be traced to each SI system that depends on it.
  • Data type and classification, including format and sensitivity level.
  • Metadata and versioning, including verification of integrity such as hashes, and, for documents such as operating procedures and equipment manuals used for retrieval, their revision status, so that superseded versions can be withdrawn from the index while a record is kept of which version was indexed when. That record keeps past outputs interpretable after the corpus changes (see Task 10.1 and Practice 13).
  • Licensing, terms, and other restrictions on usage, including the uses in SI systems that the data is authorized for, restrictions on secondary use, and the approval needed before a new or materially expanded use.
  • Lineage and transformations performed, including cleaning, masking, gap-filling or estimation, and synthetic data generation, and whether each value is measured, modeled or imputed, so that estimated values are not later used or trained on as if they were measured.
  • For labeled data, the labeling method and who assigned the labels, such as domain experts, other personnel or an automated tool.
  • Quality and integrity metrics and checks performed.
  • Ownership and accountability, including contacts for incident response.
  • Security and access controls, including identity and access management, logging, and encryption requirements.
  • Hosting and storage location, including redundancy and backups.

The inventory records where data is stored and backed up, how it is protected, and which SI systems depend on it. An attacker could use it to choose the data source whose alteration would reach the most consequential SI systems. Protecting the inventory is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What approval does a new or materially expanded use of a data source need?

References:

  • Research Artificial intelligence and open finance: Synergies, trade-offs and policy implications, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 61, 2026, from the OECD Directorate for Financial and Enterprise Affairs. Box 2 and Table 1 describe the Bank of Korea’s move to “AI-ready” data governance, which adds provenance and versioning to lineage to support reproducibility and auditability, and extends controls and accountability to derived artifacts such as embeddings, logs and features. This bears on this Implementation’s lineage, versioning and access-control fields, which can apply to data derived from a source as well as to the source.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing. Under Finding 9, the discussion of robotics data notes that a simulation is useful only if it matches real equipment, materials and friction, that the only way to check this is against measurements from real machines, and that simulated data therefore extends real data rather than replacing it. This bears on the inventory’s record of synthetic data and of whether each value is measured or generated, for SI systems in robotics and manufacturing.
  • Standard ISO/IEC 12792:2025, Information technology: Artificial intelligence: Transparency taxonomy of AI systems, first edition, November 2025, from ISO and IEC. Clause 10, Dataset-level taxonomy, sets out information items for documenting a dataset. Clause 10.3, Data provenance, asks whether data is real (measured, recorded) or generated, and Clause 10.8, Data preparation performed, asks who labeled the dataset and whether the labelers were domain experts. Annex A lists Datasheets for Datasets among data transparency templates. These offer a common structure for this inventory’s entries.
