Implementation 3.7.2: SI Use Detection

Detection can find SI use that declarations miss, whether brought in by people or embedded in equipment. Incorporate the detection and logging of traffic to known public commercial AI API endpoints. Include scanning for unapproved AI applications, including containerized applications and software models. Monitor for unusual high-volume traffic that may indicate an unauthorized AI system ingesting data. Reviews of cloud accounts, SI service subscriptions and spending, and issued API keys can reveal SI services that traffic monitoring misses. In OT zones that are not expected to make outbound connections, consider alerting on any connection to an SI service endpoint rather than only logging it. Not all unvetted SI is brought in by people: smart sensors, vision systems and industrial gateways can carry embedded inference engines that arrive through ordinary procurement, do not appear in IT asset inventories and make no calls to external SI endpoints, so identifying them may require device-level inventory and the vendor disclosures in Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies rather than inference from network traffic.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • In which OT zones does any connection to an SI service endpoint raise an alert?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement CM-8(3), Automated Unauthorized Component Detection, detects unauthorized hardware, software and firmware components and sets actions such as isolating them. Its discussion cautions that some types of components, such as IoT devices and sensors, cannot host the software agents that detection mechanisms may depend on. This bears on scanning for unapproved SI applications and on device-level visibility of embedded inference engines.
  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 3.2 asks operators to collect flow logs and access logs for AI endpoints and to track data egress by asset and identity. Section 2.3 notes that some OT devices now come with built-in AI technology, and asks operators to require vendors to explain how AI is embedded in their products. These bear on the traffic-based and the device-level detection in this Implementation.
