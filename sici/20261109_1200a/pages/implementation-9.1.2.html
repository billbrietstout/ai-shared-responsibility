Implementation 9.1.2: Develop a Comprehensive SI Risk Competency Matrix and Personalized Learning Paths

A competency matrix helps show what each role needs to know about SI risk, who needs training, and who needs retraining when an SI system’s behavior or use changes. Turn the role requirements from Implementation 9.1.1: Identify Required SI Literacy and Risk Awareness Levels for Existing Operational Roles into a structured competency matrix mapping each role to specific training modules, and build individualized learning paths so personnel are trained on what their specific role requires rather than a one-size-fits-all curriculum. The matrix can also state how each competency will be assessed, for example by a test, a practical demonstration or an oral evaluation. Defined assessment methods give the records and drills in Task 9.3: Establish Audit-Ready Systems for Verifying Workforce Readiness criteria to check against. Recording each person’s assessed current level alongside the level that their role requires lets each learning path start from that person’s gap. Linking matrix entries to the SI systems and versions that they cover can show who needs retraining when a system changes.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How is each competency assessed, for example by test, practical demonstration or oral evaluation?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-181r1, Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity (NICE Framework), November 2020, from NIST. Section 3.3, Competencies, describes a competency as a mechanism for organizations to assess learners, made up of a name, a description, an assessment method and a group of task, knowledge and skill statements. It gives tests, lab-based demonstrations and oral evaluations as forms of assessment. The framework is written for the cybersecurity workforce. Its competency structure bears on the assessment method this Implementation adds to each entry in the matrix.
