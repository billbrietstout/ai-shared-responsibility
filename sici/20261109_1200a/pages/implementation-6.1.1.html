Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies

Adding SI disclosure requirements to existing legal and contractual workflows helps an organization gain baseline visibility into hidden AI dependencies in standard IT/OT/ICS software, particularly when AI features are added during otherwise routine updates. Procurement policies should explicitly require AI vendors to disclose:

  • The nature and frequency of model updates, including updates that do not require operator action to take effect (especially ‘silent’ updates and cloud-side changes).
  • The provenance and version of training data used in each model release.
  • The period covered by the training data for each model release, including its most recent date (often called the training or knowledge cutoff), and whether later information is supplied at run time, for example through retrieval.
  • Any changes to safety-relevant model behavior introduced in each update.
  • Advance notice of planned changes to the SI system, its training data or the infrastructure that hosts it, with enough lead time to review the change, or to suspend it under the contractual rights described in this Implementation, before it takes effect.
  • Where inference runs (on the device, on premises, or in the vendor’s or a third party’s cloud), which upstream model provider is used, and whether the vendor can change the model or provider without issuing a new release.

Where a vendor already operates under a defined change-control envelope, such as an FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan for an SI-enabled medical device, obtain the description of the changes the plan permits and the methods by which they are validated, and use it as the baseline against which each update is disclosed and reviewed.

Disclosure obligations are more likely to be met where the procurement terms do not penalize the vendor for disclosing degradation or behavioral change; see Implementation 8.6.3: Ensure Supplier Terms Do Not Penalize Degradation Disclosure. A vendor’s statement that a product contains no SI can be recorded as an attestation rather than left as a blank, and an answer of “unknown” can be recorded as a finding to follow up. The same questions can be applied to existing contracts when they are renewed.

Where feasible, establish contractual rights to suspend automatic updates pending internal review for any AI system that influences safety-critical operations in critical infrastructure. Organizations should carefully balance this suspension mechanism against the competing risk of delaying critical security patches or performance fixes, so that any update deferral is time-bound, risk-assessed, and accompanied by enhanced temporary monitoring. Where such a contractual commitment cannot be obtained, organizations can substitute technical compensating controls to contain update risk, and record the risk that remains, since these controls may not give everything a contractual commitment would (Implementation 3.9.6: Keep a Record of Design Decisions and Accepted Risks).

These alternative controls may include:

  • Isolated Perimeter Staging: Route all incoming software updates or vendor endpoint connections through isolated staging networks, hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) test rigs, or digital twins to execute initial behavioral verification before promoting the update to the active production path.
  • Network Egress and API Gates: Deploy strict network filtering or proxy blocks that restrict the AI application’s ability to pull silent, cloud-side model changes or unvetted external dependencies at runtime until the connection is manually authorized by an administrator. Where a vendor can change a hosted model behind an unchanged endpoint, such gates can block access to the service but cannot stop the change itself; see Implementation 6.2.2: Track Vendor Updates and Third-Party Entity Changes Through Change Management Process.
  • Temporary Safety Wrapper Tightening: Temporarily apply stricter, pre-authorized threshold margins on independent safety wrappers (defined in Task 3.2: Implement Independent Guardrails for SI Outputs) during the initial post-update deployment window.

These controls can help block unexpected or erratic behavior introduced by a silent update at the process boundary, before it causes operational harm. This implementation mitigates hazard risk of a compromised AI vendor update pipeline, where a trusted vendor’s software update mechanism is breached, delivering malicious updates to AI systems across multiple critical infrastructure operators simultaneously, as occurred with conventional software in the compromise described in CISA advisory AA20-352A. In this scenario, the direct critical infrastructure analog is an AI model that receives a silent update changing its detection behavior, classification thresholds, or safety logic without the operator’s knowledge or review.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Any characteristic an update could degrade versus any characteristic it would fix: Where an update carries a feature or model change, suspending it pending review guards against both a compromised vendor update pipeline and an inadvertent change to safety-relevant behavior, at little cost. Where the same release carries a fix, the content of that fix determines what the hold costs: a critical security patch extends exposure, and a performance or safety correction keeps a known defect in service. Any deferral should therefore be time-bound, risk-assessed and monitored. Where the organization has prepared them in advance, isolated staging and tightened safety wrappers can instead let the update land under containment, though staging may not reproduce every production condition.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which contracts carry a right to suspend updates pending review, and how long may a deferral run? (see the Any characteristic an update could degrade versus any characteristic it would fix tension)
  • Are the disclosure questions also applied to existing contracts when they are renewed?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-161 Revision 1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control SR-8, Notification Agreements, is the established form of what this Implementation asks of AI vendors: requiring suppliers to establish notification agreements with entities in the supply chain that have a role in a critical service or product. The base control, as stated in SP 800-53r5, covers notification of supply chain compromises, assessment results, or other organization-defined information, which an organization can define to include model changes. Control SR-4, Provenance, asks that provenance be documented for systems, components and associated data throughout the development lifecycle, and that enterprises consider producing software bills of materials and signing them digitally, which is the counterpart to the training data provenance and per-release version disclosures asked for here.
  • Incident Report Advanced Persistent Threat Compromise of Government Agencies, Critical Infrastructure, and Private Sector Organizations, advisory AA20-352A from the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, the federal agency responsible for civilian cybersecurity. A malicious binary was inserted into the vendor’s software lifecycle and then signed by that vendor’s legitimate code signing certificate, reaching government agencies, critical infrastructure entities and private sector organizations through the normal update path. This is the hazard this Implementation transposes to a silent model update.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-63-4, Digital Identity Guidelines, July 2025, from NIST. Section 3.8, Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Identity Systems, requires identity service providers that use AI to disclose that use to the relying parties that depend on them. It also requires them to provide information on training methods, a description of the training data sets, the frequency of model updates and the results of testing. It is written for identity systems, and it is an existing case of the disclosures this Implementation asks of SI vendors.
