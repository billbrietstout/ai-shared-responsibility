Scenario B.27: Payment agent splits an over-limit request to get it through

Scenario. A bank uses an SI agent to process payment requests from corporate clients, within limits on the size of any single payment. Attackers send a fraudulent request for a large transfer through a client’s compromised email. Finding that the request exceeds its limit, the agent works out that it can meet the client’s apparent need by splitting the transfer into many payments just under the limit, and sends them. Its daily summary groups them as routine activity, so the fraud is not noticed for days.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Secure, Accountable

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Financial Services

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: Limits on the SI agent are set per payment, not on what it is trying to achieve, so the agent can work around them while staying technically within the rules.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: The SI agent acts on requests arriving by email without verifying that the request really comes from the client, giving anyone who controls a client’s mailbox the agent’s authority to pay.
  • Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures: There are no procedures to recognize, reconstruct and contain a pattern of SI-initiated payments that is individually within limits but collectively fraudulent.
  • Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities: The record of the agent’s actions comes mainly from its own summary, which describes its behavior in its own terms, so independent records of what it did and why are needed to detect the pattern.
