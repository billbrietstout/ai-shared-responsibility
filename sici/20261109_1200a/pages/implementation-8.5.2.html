Implementation 8.5.2: Establish Formal Retainer-Style Relationships with Specialized SI Incident Responders and Auditors

Where budget allows, convert the contacts identified in Implementation 8.5.1: Identify Third-Party SI Forensic and Legal Experts for Contingency Access into formal retainer agreements, securing priority access to specialized responders rather than competing for their availability during a live incident. The agreement can set out in advance the division of responsibilities, the access the responder will have to logs, prompts and model details, and any restrictions on what the responder may do or share. Where an auditor’s findings will be relied on outside the organization, such as by a regulator, consider how the auditor’s selection, payment and access terms bear on its independence.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are retainer agreements used, and what responsibilities, access and restrictions do they set?
  • Where an auditor’s findings will be relied on outside the organization, how do its selection, payment and access terms protect its independence?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.4.2 notes that legal departments can help develop non-disclosure agreements or other contracts for external incident response resources, that developing them before an incident lets response be immediate, and that private companies can be held on retainer for an OT incident.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-61r3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management, April 2025, from NIST. Section 2.2 asks that responsibilities a provider takes on be defined in a contract, including information flows, coordination and authority to act, and restrictions on what the provider can do, such as sharing sanitized incident information with other customers. It also notes that service providers often have privileged access to sensitive data.
  • Research Outsider Oversight: Designing a Third Party Audit Ecosystem for AI Governance, by I. D. Raji, P. Xu, C. Honigsberg and D. Ho, Proceedings of the 2022 AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (AIES ’22), from ACM. Section 4.2, Independence, reviews evidence that auditors chosen or paid by the audited party tend to be more lenient, and notes that auditors retained by technology companies are potentially compromised by nondisclosure agreements, financial conflicts and competition for services. Section 4.3 notes that contracted auditors can be given access only on the audited party’s terms. That bears on retainer terms where an auditor’s findings will be relied on outside the organization.
