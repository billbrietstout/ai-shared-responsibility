Implementation 3.2.4: Define Further Logging and Communication Actions

Defined logging and notification actions help turn an SI output that violates a constraint into information that people and systems can act on, though each notification also adds to operator load. Specify logging and communication actions that the organization should take when AI outputs violate constraints. Actions can include notifying necessary operators and systems, and enabling enhanced logging to provide further details. Define the response expected of whoever receives each notification. A notification that calls for no defined response adds to operator load without supporting any action. Established alarm management practice treats such alarms as candidates for removal or redesign.

For example, when a validation control rejects an AI-generated shutdown command on a generation unit, the plant control room, operators and any connected systems should be notified, and the incident logged along with associated information that may be necessary to update the AI system. The log entry for each intervention can include the SI output that was intercepted, the constraint that acted, the input values the check relied on, the action taken, any fallback state entered, and the versions of the SI system and the constraint set in effect, timestamped against the control system’s time source and kept in the tamper-evident storage described in Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities. These records show which commands were refused and by which constraint. An attacker could use them to learn where the limits sit. Their protection follows Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities and the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice. Alarms tracking such failures should be throttled or rate-limited rather than flooding operator consoles and logs with repetitive rejection events. For example, multiple identical failures could be consolidated into a single notification. Consolidation should not hide a change in what is happening. A rising rate of rejections, a rejection of a safety-relevant action, or loss of the validation control itself can warrant escalation rather than being merged with earlier events (see Implementation 3.4.4 and Implementation 3.4.3). See Task 4.4: Attention Governance and Alert/Alarm Flood Mitigation for further details.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What response is expected of whoever receives each notification?
  • Which changes in rejection events are escalated rather than consolidated?

References:

  • Guidance Health and Safety Executive (2000) Better alarm handling, Chemicals Sheet No 6, from Great Britain’s workplace health and safety regulator. Among quick technical improvements for operators, it lists eliminating or reviewing alarms with no defined operator response, adjusting deadbands on repeating alarms, and re-engineering repeating alarms. These bear on the response defined for each notification here and on the consolidation of repeated rejection events.
  • Research Bransby, M. L., and Jenkinson, J., The management of alarm systems, Contract Research Report 166/1998, funded by the Health and Safety Executive, Great Britain’s workplace health and safety regulator. Based on visits to 15 chemical and power plants, Section 5.3.3.1 holds that alarms should be meaningful and require an operator response. Appendix 10 adds that a repeating alarm should be shown only once on the page in view, a principle developed after two repeating alarms swamped an alarm list. These bear on the defined response and on consolidating repeated rejections here.
  • Commentary Understanding and Applying the ANSI/ISA 18.2 Alarm Management Standard, 2010, from PAS, a commercial supplier of alarm management software and services. Quotes the ISA-18.2 definition of an alarm as an indication to the operator of an equipment malfunction, process deviation or abnormal condition requiring a response, and lists frequently occurring and chattering alarms among the recommended analyses of alarm system performance.
