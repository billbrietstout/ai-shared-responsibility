Implementation 10.2.1: Ensure that Existing Logging Facilities Can Absorb SI Logging

Decision-level SI logs, such as records of prompts and context window states, can be much larger than conventional event logs. If existing logging facilities are being used, ensure that they can handle the potential additional bandwidth and storage capacity requirements of AI systems without interfering with other logging activities. For example, it is important that AI logging bandwidth does not “flood out” logging from other devices, such as edge devices, and that the additional storage requirements do not result in logs being deleted earlier than is required. See Implementation 10.1.4: Asynchronous Telemetry and Inference Logging for Constrained Edges for bandwidth-constrained, remote, or legacy edge environments where this is not feasible.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are existing logging facilities used for SI logging, and if so, what capacity is added for it?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control AU-4, Audit Log Storage Capacity, allocates audit log storage capacity to meet defined retention requirements, reducing the likelihood that capacity is exceeded and logging capability lost or reduced, which is the check this Implementation asks for before AI logging is added to existing facilities.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 5.2.3.2, Centralized Logging, recommends that OT organizations establish how long event logs should be retained and ensure that adequate storage is available to support log retention requirements. This is the OT form of the capacity check this Implementation asks for.
