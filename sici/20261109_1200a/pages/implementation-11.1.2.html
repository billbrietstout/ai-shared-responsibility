Implementation 11.1.2: Conduct Comprehensive SI Dependency Mapping Across All Sector-Critical Functions

Mapping dependencies helps an organization plan its response to an SI failure that reaches functions not using the SI component directly. Extend the ranking in Implementation 11.1.1: Rank Operational Processes Based on the Impact of SI Failure or Unavailability into a full map of which sector-critical functions rely on which AI components, directly or indirectly. Where possible, extend the map past the organization’s direct suppliers to the models, services and infrastructure those suppliers themselves depend on, since one shared upstream dependency can disable several apparently independent vendors at once. Where that visibility is unavailable, record the gap as a bounded assumption, as in Implementation 3.5.2: Identify Indirect Shared Infrastructure and Collateral Damage Exposure. Where an SI component is supplied as a service, a vendor’s disaster recovery evidence may show failover between its own sites without showing recovery of the supporting infrastructure or of the sub-processors the service depends on, so confirm what that evidence covers before relying on it in this map. Check the map with the personnel who operate the functions concerned, who may know of informal dependencies and workarounds that documentation omits. Those informal dependencies can include SI components that entered outside procurement, such as models downloaded from public repositories without vetting.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How far past direct suppliers does the map extend, and which visibility gaps are recorded as bounded assumptions?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control RA-9, Criticality Analysis, identifies critical components and functions through a functional decomposition that traces missions to the functions that perform them and to the components that implement those functions, including functions shared by many components and dependencies on cyber-physical systems and outsourced services, which is the mapping this Implementation performs for AI components. Control enhancement CP-2(7), Coordinate with External Service Providers, coordinates the contingency plan with the contingency plans of external service providers where mission and business functions depend on them, which bears on confirming what a vendor’s recovery evidence covers.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-160v2r1, Developing Cyber-Resilient Systems: A Systems Security Engineering Approach, from NIST. Table D-4 describes the Mission Dependency and Status Visualization approach to Contextual Awareness as maintaining current information about the status of missions, their dependencies on resources and the status of those resources, including an up-to-date dependency map for mission-essential functions, which is the map this Implementation builds for AI components. Section D.5.2.9 adds that mission dependencies can be determined during design, using mission or business impact analysis, or identified during operations by tracking resource use.
