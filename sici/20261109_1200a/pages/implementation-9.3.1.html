Implementation 9.3.1: Maintain Logs of Completed SI Training and Basic Proficiency Assessment Scores

An auditable training record helps the organization find gaps in coverage before an incident exposes them, and supports what it tells regulators about workforce readiness. Keep a centralized, auditable record of who has completed required AI risk training and their assessment scores. Record completion and assessment results as values that can be compared over time, not as a coarse rating. An easy assessment can produce high scores that overstate readiness, so review the assessment design alongside the scores. Testing before training, immediately after it and again some months later can show whether knowledge is retained as well as acquired. Training records can form part of employment records and be tied to performance evaluations, so manage them as sensitive data. For each completion, record the version of the SI system and of the training content it covered, and flag personnel whose training predates a significant update to the SI system so that they can be retrained before they are relied on to oversee it. Retain these records long enough to support incident investigation, which may ask whether the personnel involved were trained on the version of the SI system in use. Longer retention adds storage and handling overhead for records that are also sensitive.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How long are training records kept, and who may see them, given that they can form part of employment records?
  • Are personnel whose training predates a significant update retrained before they are relied on for oversight?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control AT-4, Training Records, documents and monitors awareness and role-based training activities and retains individual training records for a defined period, which is the existing record into which completion of AI risk training and proficiency results can be added.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-55v1, Measurement Guide for Information Security, Volume 1: Identifying and Selecting Measures, December 2024, from NIST. Section 2.4, Metrics, advises evaluating training by completion rates and the results of review quizzes rather than a low, medium or high participation rating. Section 4.4 cautions that a poorly designed phishing test can show a better success rate while revealing less about the preparedness of the workforce. This bears on reading the assessment scores this Implementation records.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-50r1, Building a Cybersecurity and Privacy Learning Program, September 2024, from NIST. Section 2.4.1, Measurements, lists testing employees before a learning program, immediately after it and three months later as a way to assess knowledge retention. It also notes that training records are often part of employment or contract records and can be tied to performance evaluations, so learning data carries heightened sensitivity. Both bear on what this Implementation records and how the record is handled.
