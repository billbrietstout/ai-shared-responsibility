Implementation 4.3.2: Implement SI-Independent Channels

Based on Implementation 4.3.1: Inventory Channels for Situational Awareness and Control that May Be Affected by the SI System, establish channels that are as independent as practical of the monitored AI systems, so that monitoring, notification and control can continue when those AI systems fail. Where a channel shares a dependency with an AI system, record which. Depending on the system, having multiple levels of fallback, even down to having personnel available and trained to physically bypass the automated system entirely, along with channels that share as few dependencies with the SI system as practical, such as satellite or cell connections, may be necessary. Independence also covers less visible dependencies, such as shared credentials, identity services, cloud tenancy and network segments. Exercising the independent channels at a defined interval and monitoring their health can help reveal a failed channel before it is needed. In process plants, for example, a small number of emergency alarms are often hard-wired to displays that are independent of the main control system, so that they remain available if that system fails. An independent channel can have its own alarm-flood problem, so keeping it to the indications that need to survive the failure can help it stay usable (Task 4.4: Attention Governance and Alert/Alarm Flood Mitigation).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Does each independent channel carry only telemetry, or control as well? (see the Resilient versus Secure and Safe tension in Task 4.3: Establish Independent (“Out-of-Band”) Communication, Monitoring, Alerting, and Control Capabilities)
  • How often are the independent channels tested?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix F.7.17, the OT discussion of SC-47, Alternate Communications Paths, asks organizations to consider which systems need alternate paths so that loss of communication does not cause an unacceptable loss of view or control. Section 5.3.2.1 adds that redundant communication should be protected to the same security level as the primary.
  • Research Bransby, M.L. and Jenkinson, J., The management of alarm systems, HSE Contract Research Report 166, 1998, funded by Great Britain’s Health and Safety Executive. Volume 2, Section 2.6 reports that a control system’s possible failure may call for a small number of alarms independent of it, and that many sites visited had hard-wired emergency alarms. It also records separate displays for shutdown system alarms that could be swamped by repeating alarms. Both bear on what an SI-independent channel should carry.
