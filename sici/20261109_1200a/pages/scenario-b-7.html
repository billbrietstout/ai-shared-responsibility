Scenario B.7: Automated train passes an obstruction it misread

Scenario. A driverless metro line uses an SI perception system to detect obstructions on the track, with a human supervisor in the control center. The system detects an object on the track ahead but classifies it inconsistently, as debris one moment and as nothing the next, and does not brake. The supervisor, who has rarely seen the system make a mistake and is monitoring many trains at once, does not intervene, and the train strikes a track worker.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Safe, Reliable

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Transportation Systems

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 1: Establish Requirements for Success: The SI perception system is put into passenger service without an established safety case, acceptance benchmarks for obstruction detection, or defined handling of its known functional limitations.
  • Practice 3: Define Risks, Policies, and Oversight for Automated, Agentic, and SI Behavior: The SI perception system classifies the obstruction inconsistently, with no bounded safe fallback, such as slowing or stopping, when its classification is uncertain.
  • Practice 4: Define Procedures for Emergency Avoidance Override, Recovery, and Situational Awareness: The SI system does not alert the supervisor or hand over control when it cannot resolve what it is seeing, so the supervisor has no prompt to intervene.
  • Practice 9: Provide Role-Based SI Risk Training that Builds Well-Founded Trust: The supervisor over-trusts the SI system after long experience of it working, and is not trained or staffed to monitor it actively across many trains (automation complacency).
