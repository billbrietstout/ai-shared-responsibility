Implementation 4.6.2: Derive Confidence and Rationale for Learned Components from Their Decision Processes

Confidence and rationale displays help personnel judge how far to rely on an SI output, when the displays reflect how that output was actually reached. To avoid misinforming personnel with uncalibrated confidence scores or plausible-sounding explanations that do not reflect reality, displayed confidence metrics, data freshness warnings, and rationale summaries should be derived from the underlying AI system’s decision making processes. For learned components, examples include calibrated model probabilities and tamper-evident, decision-level runtime telemetry captured at inference time. Calibration should be established on data representative of the deployment, such as the specific plant and its instruments, and rechecked when the model is retrained or when sensors or the process change (Implementation 7.5.1; Implementation 2.3.4). Natural-language summaries generated by a language model should be treated as user-facing presentation layers rather than as evidence of model decision logic. Interface designers and system leads should not rely on post-hoc, perturbation-based explanation tools (such as SHAP or LIME) as primary sources for rationale displays, as these generate post-hoc hypotheses that need not correspond to the AI system’s actual decision logic or internal state. Such tools, with sanity checks, may still serve as secondary aids alongside rationale derived from decision-level telemetry.

For further discussion on capturing decision-level telemetry, see:

  • Implementation 8.1.2: Identify SI Systems that May Not Satisfy Root Cause Analysis Requirements (Forensic interpretability gaps and limitations of post-hoc attributions).
  • Implementation 10.1.2: Runtime Telemetry and Artifact Tracking (Capturing decision-level runtime telemetry rather than relying on post-hoc explanations).
  • Implementation 12.1.2: Provenance and Traceability (Linking generated logic strictly to hard inference-context metadata for audit paths).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are post-hoc attribution tools used as secondary aids, and if so, with which sanity checks?
