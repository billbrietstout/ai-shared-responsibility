Task 11.3: Pre-Allocate Operational Resources for Alternative Service Delivery

Planning ahead helps an organization keep its manual fallback usable, all the more where an SI system has reduced the people and tools available to do that work by hand. Ensure the availability and maintenance of physical tools, trained standby personnel, and budgetary reserves required to perform fail-over to non-AI backup systems, which may be less efficient and require additional resources and oversight. Organizations that already plan staffing and spares for equipment outages can extend that planning to the work an SI system now does. How large these reserves should be is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Sizing them against the essential functions and restoration priorities in Task 11.1: Identify and Prioritize SI-Dependent Essential Functions and the length of manual operation that may be needed supports sustained continuity. Sizing them against the staffing that remains once the SI system is in place costs less but may not cover a long outage. Record the basis chosen and the outage durations it assumes. Review the allocation when the SI system takes on further functions, each of which adds work to be covered manually during an outage. Standing agreements for operational staff and equipment, such as mutual assistance arrangements with peer organizations, can be set up in advance where the organization’s own resources would not cover a sustained outage. Peers that rely on the same SI system or provider may be affected by the same outage. While Task 8.5: Pre-Arrange Specialized Resources for Investigating and Recovering from SI Incidents secures investigation experts, this Task secures the operational resources needed to keep the water flowing or the power on while the AI is offline.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Resilient: Contributes the standby personnel, physical tools, isolated backup infrastructure and budget reserves that make the manual procedures executable, with their availability and their isolation from the AI system’s failure modes confirmed on a schedule rather than assumed. This Task does not cover securing the specialized expertise needed to investigate the failure, which Task 8.5: Pre-Arrange Specialized Resources for Investigating and Recovering from SI Incidents covers.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MANAGE 2.1 (on allocating resources): This Task covers the operational resources for the non-AI alternative, in named trained personnel, physical tools, isolated infrastructure and budget reserves, checked on a defined schedule; it does not cover resources for investigating AI incidents. For that, see for example Task 8.5: Pre-Arrange Specialized Resources for Investigating and Recovering from SI Incidents.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How large are the reserves, on what basis, and for which outage durations?
  • Are mutual assistance arrangements set up, and do the peers involved rely on the same SI system or provider?
  • Who holds the budget for standby resources, and who can release it during an outage?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-34r1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, from NIST. Section 3.1, on the contingency planning policy statement, says that resources necessary for the contingency procedures must be acquired and maintained, and that personnel must be trained to execute them. Section 3.4.4, Equipment Replacement, describes buying required equipment in advance and storing it offsite, and notes the drawbacks: funds committed in advance, and equipment that can become obsolete or unsuitable as technologies and requirements change. Written for information systems, it bears on the standing personnel, tools and budget this Task sets aside for the alternative independent of the SI system.
  • Research 2013 Long-Term Reliability Assessment, December 2013, from the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC). Under Other Challenges, Aging Infrastructure notes that risk-based approaches to maintaining and replacing bulk power system facilities incorporate resilience planning, including spare equipment and restoration teams trained for fast response, and that in-depth training programs need capital, engineering and labor resources that are in short supply. Aging Workforce notes interest in transferring the expertise of retiring workers. Written for the electric sector about aging assets rather than SI systems, it is a sector example of the standing personnel and spares this Task sets aside.
