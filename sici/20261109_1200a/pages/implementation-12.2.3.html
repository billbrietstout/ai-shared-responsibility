Implementation 12.2.3: Validation Against Tacit and Formal Requirements

(Referencing Implementation 1.4.4: Requirements for SI-Generated Code, Configurations, Procedures, and Other Logic) Ensure the validation process explicitly checks the generated logic against both codified technical standards and the “unwritten” operational norms of the specific facility. Tacit knowledge is hard to formalize and is acquired mainly through experience (see Nonaka 1994). Generated logic can therefore miss a facility’s unwritten norms, and validation benefits from people with operating experience at that facility. Where validation brings an unwritten norm to light, recording it in the requirements under Implementation 1.4.4: Requirements for SI-Generated Code, Configurations, Procedures, and Other Logic can make it available to later reviews and automated checks. For example, a facility may start pumps in a particular order to avoid water hammer, or never fully close a valve because of a known weakness upstream. Structured interviews and walkdowns with experienced operators can bring such norms to light before validation begins. Recorded norms can reveal known weaknesses in the plant, such as the upstream weakness in this example, which an attacker could use to choose where to cause harm. Protecting them is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How are a facility’s unwritten norms brought to light before validation, and who records them in the requirements?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement SA-11(4), Manual Code Reviews, notes that manual review finds weaknesses requiring knowledge of an application’s requirements or context that is, in most cases, unavailable to automated tools, which is the kind of knowledge that checking generated logic against a facility’s unwritten operational norms depends on.
  • Research Nonaka, I., A Dynamic Theory of Organizational Knowledge Creation, Organization Science volume 5 number 1, February 1994, pages 14 to 37, from INFORMS. Section 2.2, Two Dimensions of Knowledge Creation, draws on Polanyi to distinguish explicit knowledge, transmittable in formal, systematic language, from tacit knowledge, which has a personal quality that makes it hard to formalize and communicate. Section 2.4 states that the key to acquiring tacit knowledge is experience, and calls the conversion of tacit into explicit knowledge “externalization”. This bears on checking generated logic against a facility’s unwritten norms, and on recording a norm once it surfaces.
