Implementation 3.3.3: Actionable Operator Decision & Escalation Pathways

Agreeing in advance how far to rely on SI output in each operating condition helps personnel respond consistently, rather than judging trust under pressure. Establish clear, pre-authorized decision pathways that dictate how personnel respond to AI outputs across normal, degraded, and emergency operating conditions. Organizations should operationalize these pathways into simplified quick-reference guides, HSI prompts, and standard operating procedures. Examples to consider include:

  • Trust & Execute: Routine, high-confidence recommendations within validated baseline operating envelopes (as specified in Implementation 1.1.2: Structured Requirements Mapping and validated under Implementation 1.3.1: Define TEVV Scope and Requirements per SI System) and within the consequence thresholds set under Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses, executed without further review where organizational policy permits, and notified for visibility.
  • Spot-Check & Investigate: Recommendations with moderate confidence, derived as in Implementation 4.6.2: Derive Confidence and Rationale for Learned Components from Their Decision Processes, or novel input parameters (e.g., an unusual maintenance condition, borderline prior-authorization claim, or atypical network routing request), requiring a brief manual plausibility check before execution.
  • Escalate to Oversight: Persistent, non-critical model anomalies or recurring “soft” failures that do not threaten immediate safety or service continuity but indicate algorithmic drift or data schema shifts (as discussed in Implementation 3.3.2: Establish Maximum Drift Thresholds), routed to the role holding decision authority for the capability along the escalation paths defined in Implementation 3.9.3: Establish Escalation to Empowered Authority.
  • Immediate Emergency Override: Any AI output that intersects with deterministic safety/policy wrappers (as discussed in Task 3.2: Implement Independent Guardrails for SI Outputs), violates hard physical or financial limits, or occurs during an unannounced multi-system outage. For this pathway, define in advance which roles may move the SI-enabled function to its fallback without seeking further authorization, consistent with the tiers in Implementation 4.1.2: Establish Tiered Intervention Procedures, Training, and Authorization. How the organization treats a good-faith decision to move to the fallback, where the SI output later proves to have been correct, is for it to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Protecting such decisions removes a reason to hesitate when the pathway calls for action, and it also weakens the check against moves made without adequate grounds. Record the position taken and make it known to the roles concerned.

Defining these pathways does not by itself establish that personnel apply them as intended. Periodically assess whether personnel assign outputs to the correct pathway under realistic workload and time pressure, particularly at the boundary between Trust & Execute and Spot-Check & Investigate, where repeated correct outputs can condition personnel to treat later recommendations as routine (see Implementation 9.2.1 and Implementation 9.2.2).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which pathways are used, and where is the boundary between Trust & Execute and Spot-Check & Investigate?
  • Does organizational policy permit Trust & Execute at all?
  • Which roles may move the SI-enabled function to its fallback without further authorization?
  • How is a good-faith move to the fallback treated when the SI output later proves correct?

References:

  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Section 4.1, under human-in-the-loop decision-making, advises bringing the recommendations of more passive AI systems into an existing change management process. Where AI is actively updating control logic, it advises safety thresholds, alternative sensor output or state changes that add human intervention points, since problems can escalate before operators become aware of them. These correspond to the pathways this Implementation distinguishes by how directly an output acts.
  • Research Endsley, M. R., and Kiris, E. O., The Out-of-the-Loop Performance Problem and Level of Control in Automation, Human Factors 37(2), pages 381 to 394, 1995, from the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society. In an experiment with an expert system, operators were slower to perform a navigation task manually after the automation failed than those who had performed it manually throughout. Situation awareness was lowest under full automation and intermediate at intermediate levels. This bears on the Trust & Execute pathway and on the periodic assessment asked for here.
