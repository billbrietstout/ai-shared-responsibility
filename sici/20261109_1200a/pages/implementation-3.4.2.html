Implementation 3.4.2: Establish Operational Baselines and Deviation Thresholds

Define what normal AI system behavior looks like under authorized operating conditions, and establish the deviation ranges to determine abnormal behavior. Draw these baselines from the normal behavior of the process and of the people working with the SI system, as well as from the SI system’s own outputs. This can help reduce nuisance alerts. Organizations that already baseline network traffic and process behavior for OT anomaly detection can extend that practice to the SI system. Those that do not can start with the process variables that the SI system reads or influences. Where a baseline is updated automatically, as some behavioral anomaly detection tools do, review each update before it takes effect, so that a gradual shift does not become the new normal without a decision to accept it. Where updates come too often to review one by one, a fallback is to limit how far each update can move the baseline and to review the accumulated change at intervals. A slow shift that stays within those limits can still become the new normal between reviews. Record the assumptions and operating conditions under which each baseline was established, and the corrective action or review that each deviation threshold triggers. Where the SI system works across distinct operating modes, for example startup, steady operation, peak demand or a maintenance outage, establish the baseline and deviation ranges for each mode, from a period that covers it. A single baseline across modes can raise anomaly alerts at mode transitions and during upsets, when operators are busiest.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Are automatic baseline updates allowed, and how is each one reviewed?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-160v2r1, Developing Cyber-Resilient Systems: A Systems Security Engineering Approach, from NIST. Table D-4 lists Behavior Validation as an approach to the Substantiated Integrity technique: validating the behavior of a system, service or device against defined or emergent criteria such as requirements or patterns of prior usage, and learning which behaviors are normal and which are suspicious, which is the baseline and deviation range this Implementation defines.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 6.3.1, Anomalies and Events, asks that OT alerting thresholds be based on baselines of normal network traffic and data flows and of normal human and OT process behavior, to reduce false positive and nuisance alarms. Appendix E.2.5, Behavioral Anomaly Detection, notes that some such tools use AI and ML algorithms to update the baseline automatically as the environment evolves.
  • Research NIST AI 800-4, Challenges to the Monitoring of Deployed AI Systems, March 2026, from NIST. Section 3.2.1, Functionality, records workshop attendees asking what assumptions monitoring starts from and whether performance baselines were established. Attendees then highlighted the need for deviation thresholds, set after the baselines, that trigger corrective actions or reviews. This bears on the baselines and deviation ranges defined here.
  • Research Bransby, M. L., and Jenkinson, J., The management of alarm systems, Contract Research Report 166/1998, funded by the Health and Safety Executive, Great Britain’s workplace health and safety regulator. Section 5.3.6.5 notes that certain alarms are only relevant in particular plant operating modes, such as shut down, starting-up and steady operation, and that alarm limits may be changed according to mode. Section 5.3.6.6 reports that alarm systems imposed the biggest load on operators in the period after a major plant upset. These bear on the per-mode baselines here.
