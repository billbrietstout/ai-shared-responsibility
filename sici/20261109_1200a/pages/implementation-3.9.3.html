Implementation 3.9.3: Establish Escalation to Empowered Authority

Escalation that ends with someone empowered to act lets a risk beyond one person’s authority be decided, not only recorded. Define escalation paths that move AI risk information to a role with sufficient authority to make the required governance decision. Identify escalation points for circumstances in which the risk, consequence, scope, or required response exceeds the decision authority of the person or function initially receiving or identifying the issue. Identify an alternate for each role at which an escalation path terminates, so that the path does not end with a person who is unavailable. Test or otherwise validate that escalation paths terminate with a role empowered to act rather than solely with a role responsible for receiving or documenting the issue. Evidence that a role is empowered can include past instances in which an escalation to it led to the capability, or its use, being changed. Where the escalation concerns an in-progress operational anomaly rather than a governance decision, Implementation 4.1.2: Establish Tiered Intervention Procedures, Training, and Authorization applies. This Implementation covers escalation of the governance decision itself.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Where does each escalation path terminate, and who is the alternate?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-39, Managing Information Security Risk: Organization, Mission, and Information System View, from NIST. Section 2.3.1, Governance, has senior leaders determine which risk management decisions are reserved for specific senior roles and which can be delegated, and states that clear assignment and accountability for accepting risk is essential. Section 2.3.2, Risk Executive (Function), includes elevating risk from shared responsibility with external providers to appropriate decision-making authorities. These bear on identifying where each escalation path should terminate.
  • Guidance SR Letter 11-7 Attachment, Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, for U.S. banks. Section III states that challenge may fail to be effective without the influence to have model issues addressed, and that this influence comes from explicit authority, stature and support from higher management. Section V adds that influence may be shown by instances in which models or their use were changed as a result of validation. These bear on validating that a role is empowered to act.
