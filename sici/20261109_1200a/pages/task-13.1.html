Task 13.1: Prepare for Retirement While the System Is Still Running

Preparing for retirement while a system still works helps an organization keep options that disappear once it stops, such as exporting what it learned or querying it about past decisions. Establish, during design and operation, the records and capabilities that a future retirement will require. This includes knowing what would trigger retirement, maintaining a current account of what the system actually does rather than what it was specified to do, and retaining the ability to extract the operational knowledge it holds. These are continuing obligations rather than a closing task, because retirement can arrive without notice and can arrive after the system has stopped working, at which point some of them can no longer be performed.

Search Keywords: exit planning, design for decommissioning, retirement triggers, behavioral drift documentation, as-built documentation, knowledge extraction, data portability, vendor lock-in, vendor insolvency, model deprecation

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Accountable: Contributes the conditions under which retirement becomes required and the authority that decides, recorded before either is needed, so that an imposed retirement is executed against a prior decision rather than improvised. This Task does not cover establishing the decision authority itself, which Implementation 3.9.2: Define Decision Authority and Reassessment Triggers covers.
  • Transparent: Contributes a maintained account of what the system does in operation, reconciled against what it was specified to do, so that the difference is known at the point the system is removed. This Task does not cover detecting the drift that produces the difference, which Implementation 2.3.4 and Task 3.4 cover.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Privacy-Enhanced: Retaining an extractable account of what a system learned keeps material alive beyond the system that produced it, and that material may carry the properties of the data it was derived from. Practice 7: Manage Internal SI Supply Chain and Data Provenance governs the provenance and handling of that data, and any retention should be justified against a specific need.
  • Secure: The account of behavior and the extracted knowledge describe the system’s thresholds, known failure patterns, operator workarounds, and the gaps an imposed retirement would leave. An attacker could use them to craft inputs that stay under a threshold or trigger a known failure, in this system or in a successor that shares its behavior. Protecting them is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What need justifies retaining an extractable account of what the system learned, and for how long?
  • Who keeps retirement preparation current while the system runs, and what time and budget does it take?

To the Reviewer: We are actively looking for examples of record retention guidelines and regulations that may require special handling for AI systems.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 1.7 (on establishing decommissioning processes): This Task covers the part of those procedures that cannot be performed at retirement time, establishing triggers, behavioral records and knowledge extraction as duties held while the system runs; it does not cover the withdrawal of authority itself. For that, see for example Task 13.2: Withdraw All Authority and Confirm the Result.
  • MANAGE 4.1 (on post-deployment risk management): This Task covers the decommissioning preparation that a post-deployment plan should carry, including triggers originating outside the organization; it does not cover the monitoring activity itself. For that, see for example Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior.
