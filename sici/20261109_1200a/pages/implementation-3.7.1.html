Implementation 3.7.1: SI Attestation

Consider requiring third-party contractors and integrators to sign a formal attestation declaring any AI-backed analytical tools, Large Language Models (LLMs), or automated code/configuration generation software utilized during their contract period. These should be cataloged in the organization’s AI system inventory and subject to appropriate risk management controls as described in Implementation 7.1.1: Create and Maintain a Master SI Asset List (regarding inventory) and Task 1.3 and Practice 5 and Practice 7 (regarding controls). The attestation contributes visibility, so that the organization can determine whether and how the SI use could affect operations (Implementation 3.1.1: Identify SI System Decision Points and Potential Outcomes of SI System Errors) and route it to controls proportionate to its consequences (Implementation 3.1.3: Define Acceptable Risk Thresholds and Associated Control Responses). The attestation does not by itself authorize the use. Contractors and integrators can be asked to sign it because it can be made a term of their contract. Staff can instead make a periodic declaration under the organization’s acceptable-use policy. Vendors are covered by the disclosure obligations in Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies rather than by a separate attestation. How the first round of attestation treats existing undeclared use, and how later discrepancies are followed up, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. A period in which existing use can be declared without penalty, and follow-up that does not penalize honest disclosure, encourage the declarations the attestation depends on. Sanctions for undeclared use deter it, but they can also discourage disclosure. Record the approach and the reason for it. Early in such a program, a rise in declared or detected use can indicate improving visibility rather than growing risk. Compare attestations with the results of Implementation 3.7.2: SI Use Detection, and follow up on differences in either direction.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who signs an attestation, and who makes a periodic declaration instead?
  • How is existing undeclared use treated in the first round, and how are later discrepancies followed up?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-161 Revision 1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Section 3.1.2, The Role of C-SCRM in the Acquisition Process, lists self-attestation among acceptable methods for validating supplier adherence, alongside certifications, site visits and third-party assessments. It asks that the type and rigor of the method be commensurate with the criticality of what is acquired. This bears on using the attestation to route an SI use to proportionate controls.
  • Commentary Artificial Intelligence Model Risk Management: Observations from a Thematic Review, Information Paper, December 2024, from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, drawn from a review of banks. Paragraph 5.1.2 reports that control functions often lead the identification of AI, for example by setting up attestation processes or acting as the final arbiter of whether AI is being used. Section 5 also describes processes to keep AI inventories complete, capturing the approved scope of use. These bear on feeding attestations into the SI system inventory.
