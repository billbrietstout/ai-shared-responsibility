Task 7.1: Maintain an Internal Registry of SI Deployments

Catalog internal instances, versions, and specific use-cases of SI systems deployed across different departments, and the authorization associated with each (including legacy SI systems that predate formal authorization). This Task helps ensure that internal stakeholders have a clear inventory of “where” AI is operating within the organization, enabling consistent oversight and maintenance even when models are embedded in departmental workflows. Include SI systems embedded in vendor products and devices, such as analytics added to a data historian or inference on an edge device. Such systems can arrive through a routine update without any procurement decision; Implementation 6.1.1: Add SI Disclosure to Existing Procurement Policies covers what vendors are asked to disclose. The registry can sit within the organization’s existing IT and OT asset inventory rather than in a separate list, as externally sourced components do under Implementation 6.2.1: Register Externally Sourced SI Components in Asset Management Systems. Whether registration is a condition of approval to connect an SI system to operational networks or data is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. Making it one helps keep the registry complete, at the cost of a step that can slow urgent connections. Linked to the data inventory and to AI bills of materials, the registry can also help locate the deployments affected when a vulnerability, a vendor model change or a data quality problem comes to light.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Transparent: Contributes a catalog of where AI is operating, at which version, for what purpose and under what authorization, including administrative tools and legacy systems that predate formal authorization, with lineage from base model to fine-tuned adapter and the data sources each draws on. This Task does not cover restricting or removing the unauthorized AI that scanning under Implementation 7.1.3: Integrate Automated Internal Software and SI Bill of Material Management may find, which Task 3.7: Govern and Mitigate Risks from Unauthorized, Transient, and “Shadow SI” Systems addresses.
  • Accountable: Contributes a named responsible contact for each AI system and an owner for each data source, including contacts for incident response. This Task does not cover establishing the team that acts when an incident occurs, which Task 8.3: Designate Roles for SI Incident Governance covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: The registry records where each SI system runs, what it can reach, who is responsible for it, and which controls surround it. An attacker could use it to pick the system with the most reach and the least protection, or to learn whose account or identity to target. Protecting the registry is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice, as with its other asset inventories.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • GOVERN 1.6 (on building the AI inventory): This Task covers the content of the inventory: names, versions, hosts, owners, dependencies, purpose, controls and model lineage for each internal AI system, with a companion data inventory; it does not cover disclosures obtained from vendors. For those, see for example Practice 6: Integrate Visibility of the External Supply Chain of SI into Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management.
  • MAP 4.1 (on understanding AI component-level risks): This Task covers the component record, including licensing, terms and usage restrictions for each data source, from which component-level and legal risks can be mapped; it does not cover assessing those risks.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is the registry kept within the existing IT and OT asset inventory or as a separate list?
  • Is registration a condition of approval to connect an SI system to operational networks or data?
  • Do the named contacts and data owners have the visibility, time, access, and authority to act on what the registry shows about their systems?

References:

  • Commentary Artificial Intelligence Model Risk Management: Observations from a Thematic Review, Information Paper, December 2024, from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, written for banks. Section 5.2, Inventory, reports that banks mostly keep a formal AI inventory. The inventory supports using AI only within the scope for which it was approved, and generally records purpose, scope of use, upstream and downstream dependencies, model status, approvals and responsible personnel. One bank aggregated its AI use case inventory with inventories of data, technology and controls. These observations bear on the authorization and the departmental use cases this Task asks the registry to record.
  • Guidance Supervisory Statement SS1/23, Model risk management principles for banks, May 2023, updated 16 April 2026, from the Prudential Regulation Authority of the Bank of England, which sets its expectations for UK banks with internal model approval. Principle 1.2, Model inventory, expects a firm-wide inventory, alongside any kept by individual lines of business, covering models in use, under development or decommissioned, so that direct and indirect inter-dependencies between models can be identified. This bears on this Task’s catalog of SI systems deployed across different departments.
