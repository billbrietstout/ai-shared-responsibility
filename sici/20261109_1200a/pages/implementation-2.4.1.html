Implementation 2.4.1: Benchmarking on Application-Specific Terminology

A benchmark built from sector and site terminology shows how well an SI system interprets the terms that matter for the tasks it will perform. Develop or utilize sector-specific datasets that include significant application-specific terminology. This includes ambiguous operational terms (e.g., distinguishing between a “soft trip” and a “hard shutdown”), colloquial terms used by different roles in the sector, and terms whose application- and role-specific meanings differ from conventional use. This dataset should be used to measure the model’s precision in interpreting application-specific text, such as in summarizing reports, interpreting natural language instructions, and performing internal and external-facing chatbot tasks. Items drawn from the organization’s own procedures, alarm text, work orders and shift logs, with expected answers agreed by qualified personnel, can tie the dataset to the tasks the AI system will perform. Scoring each response as correct, incorrect, or incorrect in a way that could lead to an unsafe action, and reporting results by category of term and task, can keep a weakness in a high-consequence category from being hidden by a good overall score.

Where such a dataset is published or shared beyond the organization, its items may later appear in the training data of AI systems it is used to evaluate, raising their scores without a corresponding gain in capability. Holding back part of the dataset from release can help reveal this. The same applies where the dataset is shared with a vendor, or where its items could enter a corpus that is used to fine-tune the AI system or that it retrieves from.

References:

  • Research Exploring Possible AI Trajectories Through 2030, OECD Artificial Intelligence Papers No. 55, February 2026, from the OECD Directorate for Science, Technology and Innovation and the OECD Strategic Foresight Unit, a paper in a series that describes preliminary results or research in progress, as published in February 2026. Annex B.3 lists limitations of benchmarks as indicators of AI capability, including insufficient relevance to a system’s intended applications and data contamination, in which benchmark items enter a model’s training data and scores rise through memorization. Both bear on the sector-specific datasets in this Implementation.
