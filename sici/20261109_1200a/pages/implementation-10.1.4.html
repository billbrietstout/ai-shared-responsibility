Implementation 10.1.4: Asynchronous Telemetry and Inference Logging for Constrained Edges

Devices with little bandwidth or storage can still contribute to the decision-level record if their logs are held locally and sent on later. For bandwidth-constrained, remote, or legacy edge environments where continuous, high-volume raw transfer is unfeasible, meet the logging intent of Task 10.1: Define Decision-Level Logging and Metadata Retention Policies via edge-side log compression or secure local storage paired with delayed, off-peak batch telemetry transfers to the central log management infrastructure, or high-level metadata reviews. Size local storage to hold the records generated over the longest expected interval between transfers. Decide in advance what happens if it fills, since a default of overwriting the oldest records can destroy evidence before it is transferred. Where a device cannot transfer its logs over a network at all, an out-of-band transfer, for example on write-once media, can bring its records into the central infrastructure. Protect the integrity of records while they are held locally and while in transit, so that the delayed copy can serve as the tamper-evident record described in Task 10.2: Establish Data Integrity and Tamper-Evidence Controls for Telemetry and Logging. Records held on a remote or unstaffed device can also be read by someone with physical access to it, revealing what the device was instructed to do and where its thresholds lie. Where they hold such content, protect their confidentiality as well, under the organization’s existing handling practice for the device. Where a control loop cannot tolerate the delay, logging on the edge device should not block or delay the control action, and records can instead be written locally and forwarded asynchronously.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often are edge records transferred, and what happens when local storage fills?
  • Which devices, if any, need out-of-band transfer?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-92, Guide to Computer Security Log Management, September 2006, from NIST. Section 3.1, Architecture, notes that a log management infrastructure needs to accommodate hosts with intermittent or low-bandwidth connectivity, and describes out-of-band transfer to write-once media for legacy systems and appliances that cannot transfer their logs to the log servers. Section 4.2 has logging policy settle which hosts transfer logs, by what methods, and how often. This bears on the delayed and batched transfer this Implementation describes.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement AU-4(1), Transfer to Alternate Storage, describes off-loading in systems with limited audit log storage, where the initial storage is used only until the system can communicate with the storage allocated to audit logs. Control AU-5, Response to Audit Logging Process Failures, counts reaching storage capacity as a logging failure and has the organization define its response, which can include overwriting the oldest records. Both bear on sizing edge storage and on what happens when it fills.
  • Working Document NIST SP 800-92r1 ipd, Cybersecurity Log Management Planning Guide, initial public draft, October 2023, from NIST. Play TS-3, Define Target State for Log Storage and Transfer, has the organization determine how long events are preserved at the log source, how and how often log data is transferred, including out-of-band methods, how much storage space is available at the log sources, and how storage and transfer errors are handled. It is the draft of a revision that states it replaces SP 800-92, and it gathers these questions into one list.
