Document Structure

This document is available in a web edition in HTML, intended for reading, and as an LLM-based assistant-ready edition in Markdown with an accompanying knowledge graph in YAML. The document is long, and some assistants read only part of a long file, or summarize parts of it, without saying so. If you use one, ask it to confirm that it has read all 13 Practices and the appendices before relying on its answers. Section 1.4 and Appendix E suggest how to use them with an assistant, including how to check that it has read the whole document.

The document begins with these notes for reviewers. Section 1, the Introduction, covers the profile’s audience, scope, approach to Automation and SI trustworthiness, and how to use this document, and Section 1.5 lists its references. The core content is Section 2: 13 Practices, subdivided into Tasks and Implementations, structured around operational workflows and extensively cross-referenced. After Section 2 come the Acknowledgements, the Glossary and six appendices. Appendix A sets out the source definitions of the Trustworthiness Characteristics, the distinctions between them, and the tensions that recur across this Guidebook. Appendix B maps illustrative scenarios to Practices. Appendix C presents use cases, starting with one. Appendix D lists common control areas, the assumptions each makes that SI systems can break, and where this Guidebook can help. Appendix E suggests ways to use this document with an LLM-based assistant. Appendix F gathers the references cited in Sections 1 and 2, with the places where each is cited. The document ends with Additional Information for the Reviewer, which will not appear in the published Guidebook, the Conceptual Changelog, and the marker END OF DISCUSSION DOCUMENT.

Notes for reviewers are prefixed by a “>” and highlighted in blue; treat these as meta-commentary rather than guidance.
