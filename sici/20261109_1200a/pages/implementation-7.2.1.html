Implementation 7.2.1: Implement Data Quality Monitoring

Checking data before an SI system uses it helps the organization catch errors and gaps that the system would otherwise learn or repeat. Appropriately knowledgeable and resourced personnel should certify that organizational data to be used in an AI system, such as to train a model or to enable retrieval-augmented or database functionality, is appropriate for the task. Certification can include checking that the data is as expected and free of obvious errors, such as missing fields, duplicates, or uneven coverage of the conditions the SI system will meet. In operational settings, safety-related edge cases can be hard to capture in data, and operator expertise can help identify where they are missing. Data from external sources can be quarantined and tested on isolated systems before it enters internal repositories. Where data continues to arrive after it is certified, the runtime input checks in Implementation 7.2.4: Select and Prepare the Inputs Each SI System Receives carry the monitoring forward, and the certification should be repeated when the source or its preparation changes. Automated data quality checks at training time can likewise show when data no longer matches what was certified. Where data changes faster than it can practicably be re-certified, as with a frequently updated retrieval corpus or continuously streamed telemetry, how much to re-certify, and how often, is the organization’s decision, within any regulatory or contractual obligations, scaled to the consequence of the SI system’s outputs. The risk that uncertified data reaches the SI system in the meantime may not be fully avoidable. A record of where uncertified data can reach an SI system also shows an attacker where inserted data is least likely to be caught. Protecting that record is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice. Record it, and rely on controls that do not depend on the data being correct: independent constraints on what the SI system may output or command (Implementation 3.2.2: Define and Independently Enforce Deterministic Operational Constraints), fail-safe fallback when those constraints are violated (Implementation 3.2.3: Specify Fail-Safe States and Fallback Behavior), monitoring for anomalous behavior that can reveal bad data by its effects (Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior), cross-checks of high-consequence outputs against an independent source such as a separate sensor or a person, and provenance records that let outputs influenced by a bad batch be traced and reviewed afterward (Practice 7 and Practice 10).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who certifies data, and which changes to a source or its preparation trigger re-certification?
  • Where data changes faster than it can be re-certified, how much is re-certified, how often, and which controls cover the remaining risk?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-218A, Secure Software Development Practices for Generative AI and Dual-Use Foundation Models: An SSDF Community Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Task PW.3.1 asks that data be analyzed for signs of poisoning, bias, homogeneity and tampering before it is used for training, testing, fine-tuning or aligning. Recommendation R2 lists methods, including anomaly detection, bias detection, data cleaning, curation, filtering, sanitization and fact-checking, and consideration C1 suggests a human-in-the-loop to examine the data, which supports the certification by appropriately knowledgeable personnel that this Implementation asks for. PW.3.1 covers data used to build the model; data used only for retrieval falls outside it.
  • Guidance Artificial intelligence and machine learning: Supply chain risks and mitigations, 2025, from the Australian Signals Directorate, co-sealed by national cyber security agencies of Canada, Japan, the Republic of Korea, New Zealand, Singapore, the United Kingdom and the United States (the National Security Agency). The AI data section, under its mitigations, asks that data be reviewed and preprocessed before use, checking that it is as expected and free of obvious errors and correcting issues such as missing features, duplicates or uneven sample distribution. It also asks that externally sourced data be quarantined and tested on isolated systems before migration to internal repositories. These are concrete checks behind the certification this Implementation asks for.
  • Guidance Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology, December 2025, joint guidance from nine national cybersecurity agencies across seven countries, led by CISA. Section 2.2, Manage OT Data Security Risks for AI Systems, lists data quality and availability among the data-related challenges, noting the difficulty of capturing safety-related edge cases in industrial settings and that OT operator expertise is necessary to capture that specialized knowledge. This bears on who is appropriately knowledgeable to certify data under this Implementation.
