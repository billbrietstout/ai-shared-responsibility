Implementation 13.1.3: Retain the Ability to Extract Operational Knowledge Before It Is Needed

Being able to recover what a system has learned helps an organization brief a successor and interpret past decisions after the system is gone. Establish and periodically exercise a means of recovering the operational knowledge an AI system has accumulated, in a form usable without the system itself. This may include thresholds, learned patterns, exception cases, and the conditions under which the system departs from its normal behavior, and, for a system built on a general-purpose model, the prompts, system instructions, retrieval corpora and tool definitions that shape its behavior. The people who configured and tuned the system can record why its settings are as they are while they are still available, for example where it was adjusted to the sensor characteristics of a particular site. For systems whose behavior is derived rather than specified, this capability has to be built in and maintained. It cannot be added at the point of retirement. Where the SI system relies on a model hosted by a supplier, the model itself is usually not available for export, and recovery then rests on the account of behavior kept under Implementation 13.1.2: Maintain a Current Account of System Behavior, Not Only Its Specification and on the cases used to evaluate it. Where the SI system is acquired, the items that can be exported, their formats, and how long after termination they remain available can be written into the contract at procurement or renewal. One way to exercise the means of extraction is a test with expected outcomes, for example recovering the knowledge and checking it against cases whose handling by the running system is already known. A shortfall found this way shows a deficiency in the procedure while the system can still be consulted. Proprietary formats are a known obstacle. In industrial robotics, for example, a work cell is often supplied end to end by one vendor, with proprietary programming and diagnostic libraries that keep its operational data from being pooled with other data.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How often is the means of extraction exercised, and what result counts as adequate?
  • Which export rights, formats and post-termination availability are written into supplier contracts at procurement or renewal?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-88r2, Guidelines for Media Sanitization, from NIST. Section 4.3.1 makes the same argument for sanitization that this Implementation makes for knowledge: the need and the method should be established before the disposal phase, because the choice of what is used in the system determines what will be possible at the end of its life.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-84, Guide to Test, Training, and Exercise Programs for IT Plans and Capabilities, September 2006, from NIST. Section 6, Tests, describes tests as evaluation tools that use quantifiable metrics or expected outcomes to validate the operability of systems identified as critical in an IT plan. The Executive Summary notes that tests and exercises are ways of identifying deficiencies in plans and procedures. This bears on what periodically exercising the means of extraction can involve.
  • Working Document Preliminary Report, October 2026, from the National Security Commission on Robotics for Advanced Manufacturing. Under Finding 9, it notes that a typical robot cell is supplied end to end by one vendor with proprietary and mutually incompatible programming and diagnostic libraries, promoting lock-in and keeping operational data from being pooled. Written about industrial capacity rather than retirement, it is an example of operational knowledge held in a form that other systems cannot readily use.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-161 Revision 1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations, May 2022, with updates as of November 2024, from NIST. In Appendix A, the supplemental guidance for control enhancement SA-9(3) asks that requirements define what ends a provider relationship, for managing end-of-life processes, and that negotiated termination agreements provide for a safe and secure termination, such as removing data from cloud environments. Written about data removal rather than export, it bears on settling exit terms at contracting rather than at retirement.
