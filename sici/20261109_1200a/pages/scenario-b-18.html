Scenario B.18: Vendor model update pushed fleet-wide by a management agent

Scenario. A satellite communications provider uses an SI agent to manage firmware and configuration across its fleet of field modems, which carry remote control for wind farms and other dispersed assets, some belonging to other operators. Its management vendor ships an updated model for the agent, which the provider accepts without testing. The new model misreads a routine maintenance flag as an instruction to reset, and the agent pushes a configuration that disables every modem in the fleet in a single push. Remote control is lost for every dependent asset at once, including those of operators who never chose the provider’s agent.

Trustworthiness Characteristics: Resilient, Reliable

Critical Infrastructure Sector: Communications

How the Practices apply:

  • Practice 2: Define SI Robustness, Resilience, and Quality of Service Expectations: The SI agent can push a change to the entire fleet at once, with no staged rollout, canary group or rate limit, so a single wrong decision disables every modem rather than a few.
  • Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools: The SI agent’s authority extends to every modem in the fleet, including destructive configuration changes, more than routine management requires.
  • Practice 6: Integrate Visibility of the External Supply Chain of SI into Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management: A vendor-issued model update changes how the SI agent behaves, and the provider accepts it without testing or the ability to defer it, so a change made outside the organization alters what its own agent does.
  • Practice 11: Maintain SI-Aware Mission Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning: Dependent operators have no continuity plan for losing the shared communications service, so when every modem fails at once they cannot maintain or restore remote control of their assets.
