Implementation 8.3.2: Form an SI Incident Governance Team

A designated team helps an organization make decisions about recovering an SI system and returning it to service deliberately and consistently, including when those decisions must be made without a full explanation of what went wrong. The team oversees the recovery and recommissioning of SI systems following an incident. Who sits on it, such as senior leadership, technical and legal staff with expertise in AI, and operations management, and whether it is a standing body or convened when needed, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations. A standing team keeps relationships and knowledge current between incidents, at the cost of members’ time. Drawing in senior leadership gives the team authority over recovery and re-commissioning decisions, and also brings the production pressure to restore service quickly. Record the membership, how the team is convened and the reason for each choice. Where few qualified people are available, for example in a small organization or a field office, the same people may fill several of these seats. Where that happens, record it, and how the team obtains a view independent of the pressure to restore service (Implementation 3.9.6: Keep a Record of Design Decisions and Accepted Risks). Agree in advance who is accountable for decisions such as declaring an AI incident, taking an SI system offline and approving external communications, for example in a responsibility matrix.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Who sits on the governance team, and is it a standing body or convened when needed?
  • Who is accountable for declaring an AI incident, taking an SI system offline and approving external communications?

References:

  • Guidance AI Incident Response Framework, Workstream 2: Preparing Defenders for a Changing Cybersecurity Landscape, version 1.0, 27 October 2025, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Section 1.3.2 asks that AI incident response roles, authorities and decision thresholds be defined during preparation. Section 1.3.3 gives an example responsibility matrix across incident commander, AI/ML engineer, legal and compliance, and communications, for declaring an incident, technical investigation, taking a model offline, external communications and the post-mortem report.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement IR-4(11), Integrated Incident Response Team, describes a team of experts that assesses, documents and responds to incidents, whose personnel include forensic analysts, systems security and privacy engineers and real-time operations personnel. That is the mix of technical and operations staff this Implementation names.
