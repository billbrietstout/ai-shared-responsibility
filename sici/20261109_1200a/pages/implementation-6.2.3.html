Implementation 6.2.3: Manage End-of-Life Transitions for Externally Sourced SI Components

Planning for a vendor component’s end of life helps an organization replace the component in time, since models and hosted services can be withdrawn on much shorter cycles than the equipment that depends on them. Organizations with established obsolescence management for equipment can build on it. Define a formal offboarding process for externally sourced AI components reaching end-of-life or vendor discontinuation, including secure decommissioning and confirmation that no dependent systems are left relying on an unsupported component. Procurement terms can ask the supplier to state how long it will provide security fixes for the component’s models, runtimes and libraries, matched where feasible to the expected service life of the equipment that depends on it. They can also ask for a minimum notice period before a model or hosted service is withdrawn, long enough for the sector’s replacement lead times. Recording each announced end-of-support date in the asset record under Implementation 6.2.1: Register Externally Sourced SI Components in Asset Management Systems, and reviewing approaching dates on a set cycle, can help the organization act on that notice in time. A successor model offered by the vendor should be treated as a new component under Implementation 6.2.2: Track Vendor Updates and Third-Party Entity Changes Through Change Management Process, even where it carries the same product name or the vendor states that it is equivalent. Vendor discontinuation is one of the external triggers for retirement recorded under Implementation 13.1.1: Identify Retirement Triggers, Including Those Outside the Organization’s Control, and the withdrawal of the component’s authority follows Task 13.2: Withdraw All Authority and Confirm the Result. Preparation can begin before any discontinuation is announced. Retaining enough knowledge of the component in-house, and a contingency plan for the case where the vendor’s model is no longer available or supported, can shorten the transition if the vendor ends the contract or leaves the market. Once the vendor’s model is gone, records of decisions it made may become hard to interpret, so deciding beforehand what to keep alongside them, such as the model version, prompts, configuration, and other state and information that influenced the output, can keep them usable (Implementation 10.1.5: Define What Each Operational Record Is For; see also Practice 13: Establish Procedures for Retiring SI Systems and Withdrawing Their Authority).

Decisions for the Organization:

  • What support period and withdrawal notice are asked of each supplier, and how much knowledge of each component is retained in-house?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-47 Revision 1, Managing the Security of Information Exchanges, from NIST. Section 3.4.1, Planned Discontinuance, sets out the steps for ending an exchange with an external party: written notification with reasons, timeline and responsible staff; scheduling that leaves time to notify affected users and identify alternative resources for continuing operations; agreement on the disposition of shared data, including purging moderate- or high-impact data; and updating security plans afterward. For an AI service, the shared data includes what the provider holds from inference requests and fine-tuning.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control SA-22, Unsupported System Components, replaces components when support is no longer available from the developer, vendor or manufacturer, or provides alternative sources of continued support, and its discussion covers components that remain essential to the mission after support ends, which is the situation this Implementation’s offboarding process addresses for discontinued AI models and services.
  • Working Document NIST SP 800-218r1 ipd, Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) Version 1.2: Recommendations for Mitigating the Risk of Software Vulnerabilities, initial public draft, as of December 2025, from NIST. Task PW.4.4, on verifying that acquired third-party software components comply with the organization’s requirements throughout their life cycles, gives as Example 4 checking that each component is still actively maintained and has not reached end of life, extending to components acquired through a merger or acquisition, and as Example 5 a plan of action for each component that is no longer maintained or will soon be unavailable. This Implementation applies the same discipline to externally sourced AI components.
  • Guidance Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management, SR Letter 11-7 Attachment, 4 April 2011, from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, written for the banking organizations they supervise. Section V, under Validation of Vendor and Other Third-Party Products, stresses keeping as much knowledge in-house as possible in case the vendor or the bank ends the contract or the vendor is no longer in business. It asks banks to have contingency plans for when a vendor model is no longer available or cannot be supported by the vendor. This bears on preparing for offboarding before it is triggered.
