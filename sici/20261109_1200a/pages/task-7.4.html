Task 7.4: Define Authorization and Access Controls for SI Modifications

Controlling who can change an SI system, its data, and its safety mechanisms helps keep changes to the system to those that were reviewed and approved. Establish strict identity-based access controls for individuals authorized to modify internal AI models, systems, AI safety mechanisms, datasets, or configurations. This Task implements and extends the principle of least privilege to help ensure that only qualified personnel can alter the behavior of critical functions, reducing the risk of accidental or malicious internal interference. The same restriction applies where the modifier is an agent: an agent should not hold write access to its own system prompt, permissions or safety mechanisms outside the change process this Task defines (see also Task 5.3: Implement Principles of Least Agency and Least Privilege for SI Entities). The controls in this Task determine who may act on the system. Whether a specific proposed action is permitted at the moment of execution is a separate question, addressed in Task 3.2 and Implementation 5.2.2.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Secure: Contributes identity-based, least-privilege restriction on who may modify internal AI models, safety mechanisms, datasets and configurations, reducing both accidental and malicious internal interference. This Task does not cover deciding whether a specific proposed action is permitted at the moment of execution, which Task 3.2 and Implementation 5.2.2 address.
  • Reliable: Contributes regular checks that external SI services interfacing with internal systems have not been unexpectedly altered, such as by a silent vendor update, so that behavior tested earlier is not assumed to continue. This Task does not cover obtaining update disclosures from vendors, which Implementation 6.2.2: Track Vendor Updates and Third-Party Entity Changes Through Change Management Process covers.
  • Accountable: Contributes a record for each change of what was altered, who approved it and the evidence the approval rested on, so that a later investigation can reconstruct why the change was judged safe. This Task does not cover assessing the change’s effect before deployment, which Task 7.5: Conduct Impact Assessments and Risk Management for SI System and Model Updates covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: The list of who may modify each SI system, dataset, and safety mechanism identifies the accounts an attacker would most want to compromise. Protection sits with the organization’s identity and access management practice (Practice 5: Implement Identity and Access Management (IdAM) for SI Agents, Systems, and Tools) and its existing information classification and handling practice.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Safe versus Safe: Where a proposed change can wait, heavier approval keeps an unsafe modification out of service. Where the change corrects a known defect in an SI system or in its safety mechanisms, the same approval keeps that defect in service for longer. Options include holding the SI system in an advisory or fallback mode until the full review is complete, where such a mode has been prepared and tested (Implementation 3.2.3: Specify Fail-Safe States and Fallback Behavior). Others include an emergency change path, if the organization has chosen to establish one, reviewed and recorded through the normal process afterward, or pre-approving narrow classes of corrective change, such as reverting to a previous approved version that has been kept deployable (Implementation 7.5.1: Perform Pre-Update Validation and Re-Calibration). Each carries its own considerations. An advisory mode shifts load onto personnel, and a previous version can bring back the faults it was replaced to fix.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MANAGE 4.1 (on post-deployment risk management): This Task covers approval and logging requirements for changes to models, knowledge bases and guardrails, routing behavior-altering changes to re-evaluation; it does not cover the behavioral monitoring by which a change that bypassed approval may be caught. For that, see for example Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior.
  • MANAGE 3.1 (on monitoring and responding to third-party risks): This Task covers regular integrity checks that external AI services interfacing with internal systems have not been unexpectedly altered; it does not cover the update disclosures obtained from vendors. For those, see for example Implementation 6.2.2: Track Vendor Updates and Third-Party Entity Changes Through Change Management Process.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Is there an emergency change path or pre-approved classes of corrective change, and how are such changes reviewed afterward? (see the Safe versus Safe tension in Task 7.4: Define Authorization and Access Controls for SI Modifications)
  • Does any agent hold write access to its own system prompt, permissions, or safety mechanisms, and if so, under what change process?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control CM-5, Access Restrictions for Change, defines, approves and enforces access restrictions associated with changes, permitting only qualified and authorized individuals to initiate them, and covers operational procedures as well as software. Enhancement CM-5(4), Dual Authorization, calls for two qualified individuals to approve and implement changes to selected components, which suits changes to safety wrappers.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-218A, Secure Software Development Practices for Generative AI and Dual-Use Foundation Models: An SSDF Community Profile, July 2024, from NIST. Task PO.5.1 recommendation R3 asks that training pipelines, model registries and related components be protected according to the principle of least privilege, and recommendation R4 asks that model modifications in the model registry be continuously monitored. These are the development-environment controls that correspond to restricting and recording who may modify an internal AI model, as this Task asks.
