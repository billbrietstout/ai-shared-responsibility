Notes to the Reviewer

Thank you for taking the time to review this early-stage discussion document! To maximize your opportunity to shape this resource from the ground up, we are intentionally prioritizing early availability for community review over editorial refinement. Please expect rough text, incomplete sections, partial/incomplete references, uneven emphasis, and layout inconsistencies as we trial options and rapidly integrate ongoing feedback. To make the best use of your time, please focus on high-level content, structural utility, and terminology. Future iterations will address consistency, copyediting, and formatting.

This document is intended as a reference from which more specific guidance may be written, and which LLM-based assistants can draw on to give more accurate and useful answers about managing the risks of automated, agentic, and super intelligent systems. It is not meant to be read from start to finish.

We are incredibly grateful for the input this community has already shared. If a past suggestion isn’t reflected in this draft, please rest assured it isn’t forgotten. Some were combined with related points, addressed in a different place than where they were raised, or held back because of cross-sector perspectives and scope constraints. To help you avoid duplicating effort, the Additional Information for the Reviewer section at the end lists roadmap items not yet in the text. If there is a missing point you feel strongly about that isn’t reflected in either this draft or that list, we welcome a brief summary that highlights the core issue and its operational importance, so we can ensure it stays front and center.

Tips for Providing Feedback

To help our team give every piece of input the deep attention it deserves and avoid losing important concepts in the noise, we would be incredibly grateful if you could keep these guidelines in mind:

Direct and Actionable: If a specific practice, task, or implementation needs refinement, please include its item number and your suggestion for concrete alternative text or structural changes. Where a point applies to the document as a whole, to the Trustworthiness Characteristics table, or to an appendix, please provide surrounding context in lieu of the item number.

Summarize Big Ideas: For complex or high-level recommendations, please provide a clear, actionable, one- or two-sentence summary of your core point. Feel free to indicate if you are open to a brief follow-up conversation with our team to explore your suggestions further.

Sector and Governance Implications: We value your domain expertise on how Automation and SI impacts the operational, safety, and governance realities of your specific sector. Where this document introduces an Automation and SI concept, the input we find most useful is how it will land with your colleagues: which terms may be read differently in your sector, and how we might introduce them better.

Ground LLM assistants in Real-World Experience: We also use LLM assistants in this work and are particularly looking to you for the insights that they cannot provide, rather than for general explanation of concepts. If you use an LLM assistant to help with your feedback, please ensure that your real-world insights are accurately and concisely captured, so that the output prioritizes actionable advice and references that end users can readily implement. Please do also add a short disclosure and, if you like, a brief description of how the LLM assistant helped you.

Help Us with References: Let us know if the references we have pointed to are in the right place, if there are better ones, and which specific sections offer the most transferable utility to an Automation and SI deployment. Please prioritize sources that are actionable and add a brief note on the operational context and where it applies.

Build on our To-Do Items: Please review the “Ideas and References Already in Progress” section in the Additional Information for the Reviewer section and see if we’re already on the right track, and let us know of any additional insights that might help. Similarly, we welcome suggestions for placeholders currently marked “TBD.”

Key Areas for Feedback

We have inserted reviewer notes throughout the document and at the end of it, in the Additional Information for the Reviewer section. We are also particularly seeking feedback on the following general points.

Scope & Sector Alignment: We prioritize shared challenges that offer transferable, cross-sector insights. What universally critical elements are missing, and which current examples should be adjusted to resonate more widely? Which sections are better suited for future sector-specific companion resources? How can this central document best serve as a foundational reference for guidance that you or your sector’s organizations may be developing?

Operational Feasibility & Safety: Are these tasks realistically achievable across legacy operational technology (OT) and resource-constrained environments? For example, in high-speed automated systems, when are manual “human-in-the-loop” workflows practical versus when do operational realities and resource constraints require automated, deterministic safety boundaries or fail-safe logic? Is the general language clear and functional when applied to your sector’s specific safety realities?

Strategic Governance & Executive Translation (Open Design Area): We seek inputs, including use cases, operational scenarios, and references to regulations and other governance artifacts, to help executive leadership map high-level governance, organizational missions, and compliance obligations to the practices in this document. We also welcome suggestions for how to best facilitate this connection in a way that is appropriate for this cross-sector profile, as well as factors to consider for possible future sector-specific guidance.

Document Design Criteria: Do the current entries successfully hit the relevant Document Design Criteria that appear in the Additional Information for the Reviewer section? Are there additional design criteria we should adopt to ensure this Profile and Guidebook remain practical and actionable?

Glossary Priorities: Which terms are most likely to cause confusion when bridging the gap between SI development and real-world critical infrastructure governance? What specific definitions or semantic conflicts should we explicitly clarify to prevent communication breakdowns between sectors or along the supply chain?

Preparing for public comment: We expect to transition to public comment shortly, where this work will reach readers who have not followed its development. Is there anything that you think would confuse or put off such readers, for example terms that read differently in your sector, claims that might be taken as requirements, or places where it is hard to find a starting point?

Suggested Ways to Review this Document

We suggest reading this front matter and Section 1.4, which describes how to find your way around the document, with or without an LLM-based assistant. We welcome any level of feedback you can provide. Here are some suggested paths, depending on the time you have available.

Narrative Scan: Focus on the Introduction and the high-level Practice/Task headings. Evaluate whether our overall approach and risk families align with your sector’s realities.

Priority Check: Review the core practices in Section 2. If your organization could only focus on a limited subset of these, which are your immediate “must-haves”? Which ones are missing?

Technical Deep Dive: Examine the specific Implementations, references and AI RMF subcategory fields relevant to your technical, operational, procurement, or policy role. Help us refine the operational, contractual, and technical “bridge language” to ensure these controls work cleanly in real-world environments and organizational workflows. (Note: Some practices are more complete than others, and some Reference fields are empty or minimal. We warmly welcome your ideas for filling these out.)

Thanks for helping us make this Profile and Guidebook more useful for your sector!

On the Practices, Tasks, and Implementations

Practices, Tasks, and Implementations

To the Reviewer:

We welcome suggestions for additional practices, tasks, implementations, and references from within and beyond your sector. This profile prioritizes content and examples that generalize and offer broad, transferable value across all 16 critical infrastructure sectors, even if they may be focused on one sector or application. Highly specialized or unique content is still welcome and valuable for potential future sector-specific companion resources, developed by, or in partnership with, sector organizations.

This profile addresses SI risk from an operational and consequence-containment perspective, intentionally deconflicting with and referencing cybersecurity-focused control-level guidance. Security-adjacent content still appears where it best illustrates an SI-specific failure mode, or where a control area sits at the boundary between operational and cybersecurity risk. Our intent is to address the operational context specific to critical infrastructure and to point to cybersecurity-specific resources that will cover it in depth. We welcome suggestions on how to bridge this profile with such resources.

We welcome suggested additions to “Search Keywords” that would help readers scanning for concepts relevant to their sector or role find their way to the right Practice and Task.

References currently appear at all three levels: some at the Practice level, covering multiple Tasks and Implementations at once rather than repeating them; others at the Task and Implementation levels, specific to that item. Adding operational context to these references is ongoing work. We welcome thoughts on how to make these references and associated contextualizations more useful.
