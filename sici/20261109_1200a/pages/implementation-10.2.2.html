Implementation 10.2.2: Protect Logs from Alteration Where They Serve as Evidence

Evidence of what an SI system did carries more weight when the organization can show that the record has not changed since it was made. Where SI system logs may serve as evidence, for example for incident reconstruction under Practice 8: Incorporate SI-Aware Incident Analysis and Response Procedures, for accountability for consequential actions, or under a record-keeping obligation, consider how strongly they need to be protected from later change. Which logs need that protection, and how strong it should be, is for the organization to decide, within any regulatory or contractual obligations, in proportion to the consequence of the actions the logs record and how likely they are to be needed as evidence. Routine diagnostic logs may need nothing beyond normal log handling.

Where strong protection is chosen, write the logs to storage that the SI system, its agents and the identities they use are unable to modify or delete, such as write-once media, append-only stores with retention locks, or a separate collector that accepts new records and offers no means to change existing ones. Hold that storage on infrastructure that shares as little as practical with the SI system, including credentials, so that compromise of the SI system, or an agent acting beyond its authority, is unlikely to also remove the record of what it did. Where infrastructure or credentials are shared, record which (see How Far to Go). Where that is not practical, for example on constrained devices, tamper evidence is a lighter alternative: a cryptographic hash or signature computed when each record is created and checked later does not prevent a change, but shows that one was made. Where records are buffered or forwarded before they reach the store, as in Implementation 10.1.4: Asynchronous Telemetry and Inference Logging for Constrained Edges, protect them at each stage. In OT environments, a unidirectional gateway can carry records out of the control network to the store without opening a path back in. Check integrity at a defined interval rather than only after an incident, and treat a failed check as a possible incident, whether the cause is tampering or a fault.

Immutable storage can conflict with obligations to delete records, such as the retention limits on the separated store in Implementation 10.1.3: Forensic Integrity and Privacy-Preserving Log Partitioning. Where both apply, settle the retention period before records are written, or keep the protected content outside the immutable store and hold only a keyed hash of it there. Record how the conflict is resolved. Immutability protects a record from later change. The accuracy of a record that can only come from the SI system itself remains bounded by that system’s integrity; see Implementation 10.1.2: Runtime Telemetry and Artifact Tracking.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which logs are held in immutable or tamper-evident storage, and on what grounds?
  • How are deletion obligations reconciled with immutability?
  • How often are integrity checks run?
  • What does immutable or tamper-evident storage cost to run for the chosen retention period, and who funds it?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-53r5, Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations, from NIST. Control enhancement AU-9(1), Hardware Write-Once Media, writes audit trails to hardware-enforced, write-once media. AU-9(2) stores audit records on a physically different system or component, so that a compromise of the audited system does not also compromise its records, and AU-9(3) protects the integrity of audit information with cryptographic mechanisms such as signed hashes. These are the storage properties this Implementation describes.
  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Section 5.2.3.1, Network Architecture, describes a unidirectional gateway or data diode as permitting authorized communication in only one direction, so that lower layers are protected from a cybersecurity event at higher ones. This bears on carrying logs out of the control network without opening a path back in.
