Task 4.3: Establish Independent (“Out-of-Band”) Communication, Monitoring, Alerting, and Control Capabilities

Ensure that internal and external personnel who may need to intervene in the AI system’s operation have access to the necessary information through channels that depend as little as practical on the AI system’s functionality. Akin to the analog gauges present on aircraft that otherwise have fully digital cockpits, these mechanisms allow for situational awareness, control, and risk management even when a fault renders AI-mediated channels untrustworthy or unavailable. A primary channel that has become untrustworthy can be harder to recognize than one that has failed, for example where it continues to present a normal-looking picture. Procedures should identify the indications on which personnel decide to rely on the independent channels instead, including disagreement between the two.

Main Trustworthiness Contributions:

  • Resilient: Contributes the oversight path that survives the failure: monitoring, alerting and control that stay available when the AI system degrades them, down to physically bypassing the automation. This Task does not cover keeping the function running, which Task 3.2 and Practice 11 address.
  • Transparent: Contributes availability of the information rather than its content: Implementation 4.3.1: Inventory Channels for Situational Awareness and Control that May Be Affected by the SI System traces which awareness channels the AI system can degrade, including through shared infrastructure. This Task does not cover determining what is captured in the first place, which Practice 10: Implement Multi-Tiered SI System Logging and Audit Capabilities covers.

Trustworthiness Side Effects to Consider:

  • Secure: The channel inventory in Implementation 4.3.1: Inventory Channels for Situational Awareness and Control that May Be Affected by the SI System records which awareness and control channels the SI system can degrade, and which critical channels have no path free of it. An attacker could use it to choose the channel whose loss would leave personnel without a view of the process or control over it. Protecting it is a matter for the organization’s existing information classification and handling practice.

Trustworthiness Tensions to Balance:

  • Resilient versus Secure and Safe: Where the independent channel carries only telemetry, the exposure it adds is small. Where it carries control, as with a satellite or cellular link or a trained manual bypass, each fallback level is a further route into the process, for an intruder and for a mistaken or spurious command alike, and Implementation 4.1.2: Establish Tiered Intervention Procedures, Training, and Authorization places tiered authorization on who may use it. Options include holding a control-carrying channel to the same access controls as the primary path, or limiting it to telemetry and accepting that intervention then takes a slower route. >TBD - Securing the independent channels themselves, as distinct from authorizing the people who use them. Suggestions welcome.

Related AI RMF Subcategories Include:

  • MEASURE 2.4 (on monitoring deployed AI systems): This Task requires that the path by which a system is monitored not depend on the system being monitored, so that a fault cannot conceal itself; it does not cover defining what is monitored or what counts as anomalous. For that, see for example Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior.
  • MANAGE 4.3 (on incident response): This Task keeps the notification path to responders available when AI-mediated channels have become unavailable or untrustworthy, down to satellite, cellular and physical bypass; it does not cover defining what is disclosed outside the organization. For that, see for example Task 8.4: Define External Reporting and Regulatory Disclosure Protocols Following SI Incidents.
  • MEASURE 3.1 (on tracking known and emerging risks): This Task traces the information flows that oversight depends on and identifies secondary couplings, such as a malfunctioning AI system saturating a channel shared with an otherwise independent camera feed; it does not cover tracking the risks arising from the AI system’s own outputs. For those, see for example Task 3.4: Monitor for Anomalous SI Behavior.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • Which indications lead personnel to switch to the independent channels?
  • Where a critical channel has no practical path free of the SI system, who accepts that, and what compensates?

References:

  • Guidance NIST SP 800-82r3, Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security, September 2023, from NIST. Appendix C.3.1 summarizes the December 2015 attack on Ukrainian power companies, in which over 50 regional substations experienced malicious remote operation of their breakers, malware erased files on at least one HMI, and the firmware of serial-to-Ethernet devices at the substations was corrupted. It is an example of one event reaching both the operator interface and the communication devices on which monitoring and control depended, the case this Task addresses.
