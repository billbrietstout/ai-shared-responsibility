Implementation 5.2.3: Determine the Appropriate Level of Agent, Asset, and Permission Granularity

To balance tighter containment against the effort of keeping identities and permissions current, define how finely access should be split, for example, whether a single agent identity should cover:

  • Control of an entire pipeline segment versus individual valves.
  • Edit rights to an entire workspace versus individual documents.
  • Single sign-on access to post emergency updates via all social media accounts versus individual accounts.

This should be based on the severity of consequences if the asset is misused and the resources available to manage that granularity. Legacy or compute-constrained OT devices may require coarser groupings with compensating controls, while high-consequence assets may warrant the finest practical split. For example, in OT the finest split can reach read and write permissions for individual data points (tags) within a PLC or other control device. Where an agent’s access expands, revisit its granularity and move it to a stricter profile rather than widening the coarse grant it already holds. Granularity finer than the organization can maintain can erode over time into broad exceptions or grants that are no longer reviewed, so the split chosen should be one that can be kept current.

Decisions for the Organization:

  • How finely is access split for each class of asset, given the consequence of misuse and the resources to keep the split current?

References:

  • Guidance Adapting Zero Trust Principles to Operational Technology, 29 April 2026, from CISA, the Department of War and the Department of Energy with partners. The Microsegmentation for Enhanced Security section lists separations down to read/write permissions for individual data points within PLCs and other control devices, and describes a strategy that may start with broad network boundaries and extend to granular microsegmented zones. The Identity, Credential, and Access Management for OT section names coarse-grained role-based access control, network segmentation and out-of-band monitoring among the compensating controls where OT technology limits ideal access controls. Both bear on the range within which this Implementation asks organizations to choose a granularity.
  • Guidance Agentic Identity and Access Management, Workstream 4: Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems, version 1.0, 20 March 2026, from the Coalition for Secure AI, an OASIS Open Project. Section 3.2, Capability-risk classification, ties required controls to agent capability and the sensitivity of the resources accessed, states that the classification adjusts the strength and granularity of controls rather than whether agents are subject to IAM, and asks that agents move to stricter control profiles as their access expands.
